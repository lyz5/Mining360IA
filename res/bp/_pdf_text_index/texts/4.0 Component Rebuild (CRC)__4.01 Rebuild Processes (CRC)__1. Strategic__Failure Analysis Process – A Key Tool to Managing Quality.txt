1.0 Introduction  
 
Nearly every Caterpillar dealership has the capability to perform failure analysis, but not many 
have a formalized standard process to analyze every failure that comes into the shop, identify the 
root causes, and communicate the findings and corrective recommendations to Caterpillar, to 
customers, and within their own organizations. 
 
Root causes for most component failures come from three sources: 
1. Factory defects - including parts quality, workm anship, or design weaknesses.   
2. Customer actions - such as operator error, impro per maintenance and monitoring, 
application factors, or improper removal and installation.   
3. Dealer - in the form of workmanship defects or i mproper parts reuse. 
 
Accurate failure analysis can:  
1. Provide Caterpillar the capability to identify a nd correct factory problems quickly, and more 
effectively.  
2. Cause or assist customers to modify their operat ion or maintenance practices, which will 
prevent or minimize future failures. 
3. Cause or assist the CRC to modify rebuild proces ses to deliver higher rebuild quality. 
 
This Best Practice is does not describe how to conduct a failure analysis.  It does describe how to 
establish and use a formalized Failure Analysis program as a valuable tool to improve quality and 
customer satisfaction. 
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
Several dealers have a formal failure analysis process and a standard practice to analyze every 
component failure that comes into the shop.  The program requires a Failure Analysis staff of 3 to 
6 trained, full time failure analysis specialists.  These dealers prepare over 1000 failure analysis 
reports a year. 
 
The key elements of an Applied Failure Analysis program include: 
• Staff.   Specialists who have completed Caterpillar AFA I, II, and III classes. 
• Laboratory.   Equipped with stereo microscope, digital camera with macro capability, 
micrometers, hardness tester, dye crack detection, cleaning equipment, hand tools, 
workbenches, good lighting, storage racks and cabinets.  Ideally, the lab should be located 
in or near the component disassembly area. 
• Database.   A record keeping system ideally should be able to store failure analysis reports 
and sort records by multiple factors, such as by model, by component, by customer, by 
serial number, etc.  It also needs to utilize standard report forms and documentation, 
document communications and feedback.  Several dealers use a Lotus Notes system for 
this purpose. 
• Process.   All failures are analyzed for root cause.  Personnel job descriptions and 
responsibilities are clearly defined.  Communication procedures are established for 
providing analysis results and recommendations.  A process to track feedback and 
corrective actions is established.

       Some of the benefits experienced from a standardized AFA program are significant: 
• Warranty and Policy.  Failure analysis reports are  provided to warranty clerk to facilitate 
claim preparation and SIMS data entry.  The reports improve warranty claim submission 
with better cost recovery. 
• Product Problem Identification and Reporting.  Reo ccurring problems are summarized and 
reported to Caterpillar with detailed failure analysis information.  Early identification leads to 
faster Caterpillar response in support programs, interim measures, and engineering 
solutions. 
• Improve Customer Fleet Availability and Operation Costs.  Identification the root causes of 
failures allows corrective measures to be taken to eliminate or reduce future repeat 
occurrences.  Improvements in maintenance, machine monitoring, and operation can often 
result from AFA. 
• Customer Relations.  Failure Analysis that helps t he customer obtain better performance 
results from his fleet builds confidence in dealer service support and in Caterpillar 
equipment.  Long term, this leads to customer satisfaction and loyalty. 
• CRC Rebuild Processes.  Failure Analysis informati on provided to CRC Supervisors allow 
corrective measure to be implemented in the rebuild process, leading to higher rebuild 
quality and lower re-do rates. 
                                                              
 
3.0 Implementation Steps 
 
1. Define staff requirements and job descriptions t o include roles and responsibilities. 
      AFA Technicians: Perform professional failure analysis to determine root cause. 
      AFA Supervisor: Use AFA to take corrective actions to prevent failures and increase     
                                 customer machine availability and reduce costs 
       
2. Establish a Failure Analysis workstation located  in or near the component disassembly 
area.  It is important to that the Failure Analysis process does not slow down or interrupt 
the rebuild process.  Failure Analysis lab equipment costs are approximately $15,000.

4.0 Benefits 
• Improves warranty claim process and payment. 
• Provides basis for policy or goodwill claims. 
• Help Caterpillar to identify and resolve technical  problems. 
• Improves CRC rebuild process and quality. 
• Gives customer better machine availability. 
• Gives customer lower machine repair costs. 
• Builds customer relations and loyalty.
