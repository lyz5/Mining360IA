1.0 Introduction  
 
Monitoring operator-induced events through VIMS is recommended to ensure machines are being 
adequately operated within their design parameters. Abusive shifts recorded by VIMS have been 
traditionally analyzed and reported as a single generic event.  
 
Note that VIMS has the capability of reporting on the four different types of abusive shifts, these are:  
 
1) First to reverse (directional shift) 
2) Neutral to first 
3) First to neutral 
4) Reverse to first (directional shift).  
 
Identifying the predominant ‘type’ of abusive shifts will help to implement corrective actions.  
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
The following information outlines the process for logging an event due to an ‘abusive’ shift. An event will 
be logged with an engine speed of at least 1500 or 1800 rpm depending on the conditions and the truck 
model. Abusive shifting can result in reduced power train life and can be eliminated with operator training. 
Please note, the most recent software for 785, 789, 793 and 797 has been proven to not be a cause of 
abusive shifting characteristics. 
 
• First to reverse (Directional Shift) 
 
An event will be logged if the operator shifts the transmission control from first gear into reverse with an 
engine speed of at least 1,500 or 1,800 rpm (depending on conditions and truck model).  
 
A transmission shift from first to reverse, whilst maintaining at least eight kilometres per hour (kph) ground 
speed, will hold the machine in forward gear. Once ground speed is less than eight kph the transmission 
will shift into neutral. Ground speed must be less than three kph before the transmission will shift into 
reverse.  
 
• Neutral to first 
 
An event will be logged when a shift occurs from neutral to first with an engine speed of at least 1,500 or 
1,800 rpm (depending on conditions and truck model).  
An abusive shift may occur by shifting into first gear when the engine speed exceeds 1,500 or 1,800 rpm 
(depending on conditions and truck model).  
 
A practical scenario may be seen following a body raise whilst dumping.  
 
The scenario is as follows: 
 
Throttle is increased to induce power to hoist the body in a shorter time. Once the body is completely 
elevated, the hoist control lever is placed into the float position following a shift into first gear.  
Consequentially, there isn’t time for engine speed to reduce to below 1,500 or 1,800 rpm (depending on 
conditions and truck model), resulting in a logged event.

• Neutral to Reverse 
 
An event will be logged when a shift occurs from neutral to reverse with an engine speed of at least 1,500 
or 1,800 rpm (depending on conditions and truck model).  
 
• Reverse to first (Directional Shift) 
 
An event will be logged when the transmission shifts from reverse to forward with engine speed of at least 
1,500 or 1,800 rpm (depending on conditions and truck model).

3.0 Implementation Steps 
 
It is suggested that abusive shifts be tracked and trended over a period of time on a fleet of machines to 
identify any incorrect operating practices. Should these events be present, subsequent action will need to 
be taken by the onsite training department to eliminate and rectify any incorrect operating techniques.    
 
4.0 Benefits 
 
Benefits of analyzing and trending abusive shifts include but are not limited to: 
 
• Improved machine operation 
• Operator awareness and education 
• Reduced downtime 
• Optimized power train component life  
 
5.0 Resources Required 
 
The following items will be required to analyze the four different abusive shifts: 
• VIMS equipped Off Highway Truck 
• VIMS PC
