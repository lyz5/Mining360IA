1.0 Introduction  
 
Where space for parts inventory is limited, onsite parts stores often have to make use of a 
mezzanine level. Typically, parts pallets are elevated up onto the edge of the mezzanine floor by 
forklift, where parts personnel can unpack it from. This requires there to be an area where there is 
no safety guard railing, creating a significant falling risk for the people unpacking the parts.  
 
This Best Practice describes the use of a pivoting gate to allow parts to be stored on a mezzanine 
level while avoiding the risk of working at heights without constant protection.  
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
A two-way pivoting safety gate was implemented on the parts warehouse mezzanine level, in 
order to take away the risk of falling for anyone standing in the vicinity.  
  
   
  
 
The gate is normally in the position shown on the left above. When parts need to be lifted up onto 
the edge of the mezzanine level, the guard is pivoted to the position on the right above. Anybody 
standing on the mezzanine level is then never exposed to a falling risk. When the forklift moves 
away, the guard is pivoted back to the position on the left above. The parts person is now able to 
unpack the parts safely.  
 
The safety gate pivots on two side stands, which are bolted to the mezzanine floor, as seen in the 
pictures above.

Dexion. They only have offices worldwide, but this type of safety gate is custom made only in

4.0 Benefits 
 
• Falling hazard has been reduced thanks to the constant protection offered by the gate.
