1.0 Introduction  
 
This Best Practice deals with Condition Monitoring and Maintenance Management. The on time 
detection of potential problems is the key requisite to achieve the ultimate goal of "Repairing 
Before Failure".  
 
The Field Inspection process includes three major activities:  
1. Operator Inspection,  
2. Field technical Inspection  
3. Pre - PM technical Inspection  
 
These three activities take place in the field and compliment each other to achieve the objectives 
defined below.  
 
Goal: On time detection of potential machine problems or failures. 
 
Objectives: Detect and register  > 80% of pending visual defects of machines.   
 
Scope: Detection of machine problems by visual inspections and basic predictive tests. It applies 
to all machines operated at the site.

The critical points to observe when utilizing this process are: 
 
2.1.1 Log Book 
A logbook should be implemented. The logbook must remain in the machine cab and be 
reviewed by the inspector at the time of the machine inspection.  
 
Implementation of a logbook and the inspector interview of the operator to reduce to a 
minimum the need for double checks. 
 
2.1.2 Inspector 
Operator interview. The inspector should interview the operator when performing the machine 
inspection and understand his feedback, validate and transform it into a technical repair 
request (Backlog Request). 
                                                                                                                                                                                
2.1.3 Operator Checklist 
The operator check list included (printed) in the logbook should be structured into two major 
sections: 
• Shift start section: To be completed through a visual inspection (operator walk around) 
prior to the start of the shift.  
• Shift end section: Completed at the end or during the shift based on the operational 
assessment of the machine. 
 
2.1.4 Operator Backlogs 
The Backlogs that are generated as a result of operator feedback should identify clearly the 
name and / or ID of the operator to facilitate feedback and possible recognition.

2.2 Field Inspection Process Overview (Technical Field Inspections) 
 
The diagram on the previous page illustrates how the Technical Field Inspection Process should 
flow. 
 
Critical points to observe when utilizing this process are: 
 
2.2.1 Inspector-Operator Interview.  
Technical Field Inspections should incorporate all routines from the operator interview and the 
analysis of the Operator Log Book.  
 
2.2.2 Avoid Backlog Duplication  
Before generating a Backlog the inspector must review the Backlog pending list report to avoid 
duplication of entries. 
 
2.2.3 Pending Backlog Report.  
The use of this report is also a big contributor to the efficiency and effectiveness of the 
inspections. The correct use of this report allows the inspectors to concentrate on the detection 
of new defects. 
                                                                                                                                                                                  
2.2.4 Measure the results  
Measure the results /contribution of all the different inspection routines.  
 
In order to assess the contribution of the various inspection routines, be prepared to classify 
the Backlogs generated in those routines.  
 
3.0 Implementation Steps 
 
1. Identify and train Field Inspectors. 
 
2. Provide logbooks and training to the Operators. 
 
3. Provide a designated location for field inspections and operator interviews. Some mines 
have used the fuel island and the refueling stop to perform the daily inspection and 
operator interview. 
 
4. Develop site-specific inspection checklists for each model of machine. 
 
5. Provide the inspectors with the current backlog list for each machine to be inspected. 
6. Implement the Best Practice for Backlog Management. 
 
4.0 Benefits 
 
The on time detection of potential problems is the key requisite to achieve the ultimate goal of 
"Repairing Before Failure".

The on time detection, registration (Backlog generation) and communication of potential 
problems allows Planning and Scheduling to: 
 
1. Implement and manage an effective Backlog function. 
2. Schedule the necessary repairs at the right time (At the right window of opportunity and 
Before Failure)  
3. Provide effective use of all windows of opportunities (Machine stops). 
 
4.1 Measurable Benefits 
 
4.1.1 Time and Cost 
The difference in time and cost between a repair executed before failure and an unscheduled 
one has been established in the range of 5 to 8. 
 
4.1.2 Quality 
MTBS as a reliability indicator is also positively influenced by a well planned and scheduled 
repair. Effectively using windows of opportunities is the first contributor to MTBS results. By 
performing scheduled repairs, the pressure from operations is reduced, allowing the service 
crew to better concentrate and executes the repairs more effectively. 
 
5.0 Resources Required 
 
A successful Field Inspection Program requires a minimum level of resources located in all areas 
indicated above. 
 
5.1 Facilities:  
A suitable location has to be implemented / designated in the field to perform the inspections. 
Some efficient mines have implement field facilities to perform the inspections in conjunction 
with other operational required activities, such as refueling and tire management. (See 
supporting attachments). 
 
5.2 Tools:  
The field crew has to be equipped with all inspection and predictive tools defined in the 
inspection checklists, such as infrared temperature gauge, pressure gauges, magnifying 
devices, penetrating inks, etc. 
 
5.3 Support Equipment:  
Equipped vehicles must be provided to assist the inspections routines. There will be cases in 
which the machines will have to be inspected in another location of the mine.  
(See supporting attachments). 
 
5.4 Labor:  
Trained Inspectors and Operators are key for the success of the inspections. Operators must 
receive basic technical training and inspectors must be trained in specialized predictive 
techniques. 
 
Investment will depend on the specific site conditions.
