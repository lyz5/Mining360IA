1.0 Introduction  
 
To seek continuous improvements in quality, processes and procedures, that Matco perform daily 
at the CRC and to ensure consistent and reliable component performance is achieved in the field. 
Matco designed and custom built a unique dual oil patch sample tooling, for each dyno cell which 
is situated in the control room of the dyno cells, eliminating the need for the cell operator to enter 
the dyno cell, multiple times during any engine testing, to remove/change the sample patch for 
inspection and taking multiple patch samples at three five minute intervals, will assist in identifying 
any issues that potentially could result in considerable internal engine damage, if no patch sample 
testing was performed during the dyno test.  
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
Using this patch sampling, multiple times within the environment of the control room, while the 
engine is operating at the various speeds and load points, provides improved management of each 
patch sample.  
For storage purposes and history of each engine patch test. Matco developed a Patch Test Service 
Report (SR) format that is used to place the patch on, including at what time interval the patch was 
inspected at, as seen in picture 1.

During the dyno test, pressure oil is directed from the engine, prior to the engine filter, through one 
hose that connects the tooling above the sample patch, this patch will then collect any debris for 
analysis and the oil passing through the patch, is then returned to the engine.   
 
The purpose of each patch test at the various intervals, is to ensure we have very clean samples 
with no debris and if for any reason, metallic debris were identified, then it would result in an 
immediate engine shut down, to investigate root causes.  
 
As shown in picture 2, this is the individual patch tooling, used for both dyno cells which is in the 
control center. There are two inlet and two return lines one for each dyno cell. When the inlet valve 
is closed to remove the patch, the lower valve is open, to permit oil drainage into the tank.  
 
 
 
Picture 2: This are the parts used in the patch test system.

During and after the dyno test is concluded, the patches are then labeled by sequence and 
analyzed by the technician. The results are then recorded by pictures and on the paper format for 
filing purposes. 
 
 
4.0 Benefits 
 
- Multiple filter samples can be taken throughout the programed dyno test.  
- Cost saving by eliminating filter replacement after the dyno test 
- No filters are removed in dyno cell 
- Rapid detection of any debris. 
- Eliminates potential costly failures during the dyno test.
