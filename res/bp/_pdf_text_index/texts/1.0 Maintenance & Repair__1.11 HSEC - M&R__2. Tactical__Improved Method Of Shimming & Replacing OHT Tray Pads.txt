1.0 Introduction  
 
The clearance between the OHT tray and the truck chassis has to be adjusted to ensure that 
there are no gaps between the two.  The adjustment is made with the tray empty, and according 
to the instructions per the maintenance manual. 
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
This Best Practice describes the use of a fabricated stand installed on both sides of the chassis 
rails.  The fabricated stand is roughly 350mm in height for a 793 truck, but the exact height will 
vary depending on the OHT model.  When used, the stands are securely clamped to the chassis 
rails with the tray resting on them.  With the use of these stands the mechanic/ technician does 
not have to work with their arms raised and the risk of dropping of shims and bolts is lessened.  
Furthermore, the mechanic/ technician can work on a platform that is lower to the ground reducing 
working at height risks/ injuries. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Figure 1 shows a typical tray with the pad locations.  The idea is to slip a stand between the 
chassis and the tray at the rear of the pads.  The pads can therefore be shimmed without 
obstructions.   
 
Figure 2 shows one fabricated stand.  The stands are always used as a pair with one stand on 
either side.  The height and shape of the stand will depend on the machine model.  The one in 
figure 2 was used on a 793 OHT and is roughly 350mm in height.  The stands should be high 
enough to allow the rearmost pad to be adjusted while not putting the front most pad too high.

3.0 Implementation Steps 
 
Once the benefits of using these stands are recognized, an accredited supplier needs to be 
selected and the stands built. 
1. Provide instructions on the use of such stands. 
2. Select an accredited manufacturer / fabricator. 
3. Measure appropriate machine models. 
4. Have the stands built according to any local legislations, regulations, and safety standards.  
5. Draft procedures on the use of the stands. 
6. Provide instructions on the use of the stands. 
7. Locate proper storage area for stands. 
8. Include the stands on the standard periodic testing list of lifting gear and support stands. 
 
4.0 Benefits 
 
1. Ability to perform the task in a more comfortable position. 
2. There is less risk of injury if a shim or bolt is accidentally dropped when performing the 
task. 
3. The job is performed at a lower position in relation to the ground. 
4. The job is potentially done in a shorter time.
