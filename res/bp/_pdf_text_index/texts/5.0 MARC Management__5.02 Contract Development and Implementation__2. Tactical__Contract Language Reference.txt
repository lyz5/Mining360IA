1.0 Introduction  
 
Mining MARC contracts represent legally binding agreements between customers and dealers, however 
many customers remain unaware of (or consciously disregard) their obligations under these contracts. 
This negligence places pressure on the financial health of mining MARC contracts, resulting in reduced 
dealer profitability and potential risk to Caterpillar when supporting poor dealer margins through CGM Risk 
Partnering (or “MARC Hardship” processes).  
 
Mining MARCs represent significant Parts and Service business to Cat and dealers that is effectively 
protected against competitive threat. Conversely, MARCs also represent a significant risk to dealers 
through reduced dealer profitability. A sizeable proportion of contract cost over-runs (estimated at 20%) 
are caused by customers not fulfilling their obligations (as stated or implied) in the contract. These 
unfulfilled obligations can result from a customer not completely understanding their contractual 
obligations or their unwillingness (or inability) to fulfill them.  
 
Up until now, CGM has used informal contract language guidelines. With increased mining MARC 
business, there is a strong need to develop and deploy a formal set of recommended clauses/ language to 
ensure customers fulfill their contract obligations and do not adversely affect dealer profitability.  
 
This Best Practice contains the top ten clauses to be negotiated into contracts between dealers and 
customers. If a contract clause is removed, dealers will be in a position to explain the commercial reason 
for the removal, which satisfies the contract due diligence process.  This Best Practice supports CGM 
Critical Success Factors 4, “Deploy globally consistent product support processes” and 8, “Profitably 
expand our mining value proposition”. 
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
The objective for this Contract Language Best Practice is to outline those items in Dealer/ Customer 
MARCs that should be managed by each party. Contract language/ verbiage used in contract 
documentation must be sufficient to ensure these obligations are upheld. A set of suggested contract 
language templates have been developed with the intention of closing loopholes that allow customers to 
accidentally or willingly disregard (or poorly manage) their contractual obligations.  
 
Proper contract language will ensure that changes in machine application throughout the life of the 
contract are understood with regard to potential cost increases. Improved contract language will also 
ensure our dealers and Caterpillar are not exposed to liabilities for which they are not or should not be 
held responsible.

2.3 Betterment 
Settlement 
In order to make sure that all parties in the agreement remain free from large or unusual liabilities 
throughout the life of the agreement, a Betterment calculation will be made at each site every month.   
 
Betterment is simply defined as: 
 
 
 
 
 
Remaining Life is the value of the unused hours of any component currently installed in a CPH 
agreement truck.  It can be determined by multiplying the remaining life in hours by the current CPH for 
that component.  Further, the remaining life in hours is found by taking the expected life for that 
component type and location and subtracting the actual life on that component to date.  Note that as a 
component is removed from a CPH agreement truck, that component is removed from the Betterment 
calculation.  The value of remaining life for that component is set to zero, and then replaced by the value 
of remaining life for the newly installed component. 
 
Customer Pre-Paid is the money customer paid on a CPH basis before the component was replaced 
under the CPH agreement.  There is a separate customer pre-paid value for every component on every 
truck that was enrolled into the NAOM Component Agreement though they all start at zero. Pre-paid 
dollars start accumulating after day one in the contract.  The value of the customer pre-paid for a 
particular component is simply the money paid on a CPH basis before that component was removed and 
replaced by the contract.  Taking the hours paid to the contract and multiplying by the CPH for the 
different adjustment periods before the first removal under contract can determine this. 
 
When the Betterment balance becomes significantly large, it will be reduced by payments made from 
either Dealer to Customer or vice versa.  A balance greater than $500,000 at customer site A, $200,000 
at customer site B, and $200,000 at customer site C will trigger these payments.

Currency Exposure 
When customers purchase equipment they require replacement parts and components to perform 
repairs. Dealers purchase parts from OEM suppliers and re-sell those parts to customers taking into 
account their cost to deliver this service. In the event a dealer purchases parts in one currency and re-sell 
in another, the currency exchange rate is considered as a dealer cost and is passed along to the end 
user customer. This is a standard business operation when a currency discrepancy exists. In the case of 
a MARC, dealers need a mechanism to pass along the risk involved with currency growth / decline that 
may occur over the term a dealer is providing a service to the customer. 
 
Currency:  The customer-billing rate will increase and decrease based on a “Basis Point” relationship 
with currency. The relationship between percentage changes and basis points will be summarized as 
follows: 1% change = 100 basis points and 0.01% = 1 basis point. The customer-billing rate will 
increase/decrease after a 100-basis point change in currency. The benchmark will be the exchange 
rate as of <Date here>

2.8 Payload Compliance 
Payload Compliance 
 
• VIMS Payload Monitoring System.  Each haul truck will have a VIMS payload monitoring system 
along with gear restricting software.  <Dealer name here> will maintain the VIMS system and gear 
restricting software and will ensure that the system and the software are accurate, reliable and 
operational. In the event <Dealer name here> cannot maintain the accuracy of the VIMS payload 
monitoring system and gear restricting software, <Dealer name here> and <Customer name here> 
will mutually agree upon action to correct the inaccuracies or software failures.  If the VIMS payload 
monitoring system and gear restricting software fail or are inaccurate, unreliable or un-repairable for 
a time period of greater than six consecutive months, then the obligations of <Customer name here> 
concerning Caterpillar’s overload policy shall have no force or effect during that time period. 
 
• Payload Overload Policy.  Attached hereto as <Exhibit Here> is Caterpillar’s “10/10/20 Policy” 
developed by Caterpillar to minimize overloads and potential truck damage.  The parties agree to 
manage the payloads on the haul trucks to be consistent with Caterpillar’s 10/10/20 Policy by 
reviewing each truck’s VIMS payload monitoring system data monthly and taking corrective action, if 
necessary, to keep the payloads within the stated Policy parameters.

commissioning. The payload target may increase or decrease if modifications are made to a haul 
truck or if an alternate bed is installed at a later date.  In the event of a truck modification, a new 
payload target may be determined by subtracting any weight added to the truck’s original GVW from 
the former target payload.  In the event an alternate bed is installed, subtracting the weight of the

2.9 Performance Reviews: Contract: 
The Customer and the Dealer shall meet at quarterly intervals to: 
• Review the performance of the Equipment during the period 
• Review the Dealer’s Maintenance performance during the period, as determined by the Key 
Performance Indicators agreed to by the Customer and the Dealer 
• Review the Dealer’s management performance for the period, as measured by compliance with the 
Management Plans as otherwise agreed to between the Customer and the Dealer, 
• Review Labor levels. 
• Assess the operation of the Dealer and ensure its smooth operation. 
• Identify and develop improvement opportunities. 
• In recognition of the desire of the Customer and the Dealer to continually improve the performance 
and costs of the Equipment, and the operation of the Contract, other items relevant to this objective 
may from time to time be included in the review. 
The exact timing of Contract Review Group meetings shall be agreed between the Customer and the 
Dealer or as otherwise directed by the Customer’s Representative. 
 
2.10 Reporting Requirements 
The Contractor shall provide to the Principal a report each month containing the following sections: 
• Safety: This section of the report shall contain a list of all incidents occurring during the week and any 
other safety issues arising; 
• Environment: This section of the report shall contain all environmental incidents occurring during the 
week and any other environmental issues arising; 
• Repair and Maintenance of Equipment: This section of the report shall contain details of all repair and 
maintenance activities associated with the Equipment; and 
• Issues Arising: A list of all issues arising including any matters requiring direction from the Principal 
shall be included in this section of the report. 
This report shall be completed by the Contractor and made available to the Principal by 0900 hours on 
the day of the week to be agreed between the Principal and the Contractor.

4.0 Benefits 
 
Reduce the risk of mining MARC contract cost over-runs due to customers not fulfilling their obligations or 
changing machine application without suitable contract rate adjustments.   
Contract Language changes to: 
• Clearly define the customer’s responsibilities under the MARC contract. 
• Define the potential for contract rate rises if machine application moves outside agreed-to/ 
acceptable operating criteria. 
• Ensure timely resolution of contract/ contractual disputes. 
• Ensure fair reconciliation of contracts in the event of Termination, Extension, etc. 
• Ensure the responsibilities of the Cat Dealer are clearly defined 
 
5.0 Resources Required 
 
The resources required to exercise this Best Practice is the desire to change the dealer’s existing contract 
structure and contract negotiation process. This Contract Language Best Practice can effectively change 
the operating relationship dealers have with customers. In some cases, there may be a higher degree of 
strain introduced when dealers attempt to enforce these contract clauses. Historically, customers may 
have become accustomed to ignoring their obligations that they now have a contractual obligation to 
honor.
