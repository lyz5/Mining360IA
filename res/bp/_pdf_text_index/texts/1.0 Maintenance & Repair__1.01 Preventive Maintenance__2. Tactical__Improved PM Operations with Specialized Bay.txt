1.0 Introduction  
 
Planned Maintenance operations represent a significant time and capital obligation to dealers and 
customers. PM causes machine downtime and thus can significantly impact production, 
increasing effects with fleet size. Dealers supporting sites under Maintenance and Repair 
Contracts have an even further interest in performing PMs quickly and safely as to meet contract 
obligations and maintain profitability. Typical barriers to maximum PM efficiency include machine 
access, part availability, tool/equipment availability, and technician underutilization.  
 
After a full PM study including technician “voice of the customer,” a dealer developed a full plan to 
increase Off Highway Truck service efficiency and safety. The basis of this plan includes a PM 
Module to provide a safe and effective working environment for PM technicians. Additional 
improvements were made to the service workforce and “work scope” to fully encompass all 
aspects of PM operations and possible improvements. 
 
This Best Practice illustrates safe and effective machine access through the PM module and also 
contains details on other aspects relating to PM efficiency.  
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
The need for improving PM operations becomes apparent 
when comparing PM operations to a racing team. Just as 
racecars return to the pits for periodic maintenance, trucks 
must receive regular maintenance on-site. Pit crews are 
made of several people each with a very specific function. 
Care is taken to speed and safety – for example, the 
entire team wears helmets and fire retardant suits 
although only one member actually delivers the fuel. The 
time required for the crew to complete their tasks directly 
impacts the success of the team, and great teams often 
set themselves apart with outstanding pit crews. As such, each member of the crew is trained to 
perform flawlessly in his or her assigned function. The result is an operation demonstrating near 
choreographic perfection. 
 
While not as fast-paced as a Grand Prix, mine productivity can greatly benefit from improved PM 
operations. The primary benefit can be gained from providing technicians the best possible 
environment in which to perform their work. The PM Module was developed after a dealer’s 
complete PM study. Its design intention is to provide everything needed to perform the job right 
the first time:  
• Elevated work platforms and access steps 
• Hand rails & pneumatic operated access ways 
• Built-in filtration & lube dispensing equipment 
• Coolant recycle tanks 
• Service Administration office / IT link 
• Oil filter inspection / sample room 
• Tool chests & specialized tooling 
• Parts cabinets

The service workforce also plays an important role in PM efficiency. With skilled technicians 
already in short supply, a workforce re-categorization can help lower costs and still provide 
utmost functionality in PM activities. PM technicians can be trained to perform a specific PM job 
and develop skills by way of becoming familiar with such jobs.

To complement the PM Module, it is important to ensure technicians have a guide to ensure all 
PM tasks are done correctly. Varying skill levels, differing languages, needs for continued 
consistency, and a way to communicate updates all present challenges. The solution is a “work 
scope,” which includes: 
• Multiple languages 
• Step-by-step approach 
• Graphical displays 
• Safety reminders 
• Tooling requirements (including part numbers) 
• Parts required (including part numbers and quantities) 
• Fluid details and quantities 
• Inspection prompts 
 
3.0 Implementation Steps 
 
P.T. Trakindo Utama’s Service Project and Development Department designed the PM Platform. 
It required approximately 2 – 3 months to assemble the PM platform after the Service Project and 
Development Department prepared the materials and sent them to the site.  
 
There are several tools needed on the PM Platform such as Fluid delivery, kidney looping, etc. All 
tools were assembled on PM Platform. 
 
After the PM Platform has been assembled on-site, it is recommended to dry run the platform and 
make any required modifications. Upon Trakindo’s first dry run, a major improvement was 
identified to include a PM office on the platform. This office helps technicians in situations where 
they may need to check SIS about a failure found on the unit, etc. 
 
Once the PM Platform has been installed and ready for production use, the dealer must train the 
technicians and familiarize them with new PM Platform and related processes. 
Inbuilt kidney looping (5 micron final filter) for final drive, transmission, and torque converter oil.

4.0 Benefits 
 
Batu Hijau has 115 units of 793C’s. As a big site, an improvement in the quality of Preventive 
Maintenance has a high potential to increase the availability of units. Before the site used a PM 
Platform, average machine downtime for PM 500 was about 8 hours. After implementation of the 
PM Platform, average machine downtime for PM 500 went down to 6 hours. This reduction 
certainly increases the availability of trucks and at the same time improves the quality of 
Preventive Maintenance.  
 
 
 
 
Referring to the table above, for the fleet of 115 793C’s, production increased 1,136,200 tons per 
year. The benefits of consistent PM also include increased component life and reduced costs. 
Using the PM Platform also increased the mean time of first stoppage for service after PM, 
indicating that the quality of PM had been increased. See the chart below:

5.0 Resources Required 
 
• Investment costs to build PM platform: approximately USD 218,000, not including 
improvement and adjustment costs. 
• PM job tools such as kidney looping, hand tools, hoses & adapters, etc. 
• Technician training in new PM module operation. 
• Socialization to Customer’s operator, to build operators awareness about the new PM 
Platform.
