1.0 Introduction  
 
In today’s CRC environment where CRC efficiency is required, most dealers strive to minimize 
work interruptions. Any time a technician must leave his work bay, this creates an interruption. 
 
Gecolsa, the Caterpillar dealer in Colombia, has recently implemented a process in the shop to 
deliver supplies and consumables to each of the 44 different work bays twice a day, using a 
stocked delivery cart for this purpose.  Supplies and consumables are stored in the tool crib, and 
under the new process, tool crib personnel deliver supplies to the work bays using a fully stocked 
cart. 
 
Technicians no longer need to leave their workstation in order to get common supplies and 
consumables. 
 
The delivery of supplies using a “supply cart” is far more efficient than the almost daily technician 
trip to the tool room previously required. In addition, this practice eliminates a common work 
interruption and better utilizes the time of skilled technicians.  
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
 
         
                            
 
Previously, the supplies and consumables delivery process was centralized. Technicians were 
responsible for getting their own supplies. Technicians had to leave their work bays, walk to CRC 
Tool Room and order supplies or tools required for their tasks. 
 
In order to increase efficiency, a supply delivery process was developed. The target was to deliver 
supplies directly to CRC work bays. A special delivery cart was designed to hold commonly 
consumed items and easily transport them to the workstations.

3.0 Implementation Steps 
 
• Communicate new process to CRC personnel. 
 
        Before                       After 
 
     
  
 
• Determine a supply list - with the highest demand.  
 
Supply lists includes such items as rolls of plastic for Contamination Control, sand paper and 
emory cloth, loctite, brushes, silicon, markers, gloves,  gasket compound, o-ring compound, 
duct tape, greases, cleaners, penetrating oils, paper towels, etc. See attachments for a parts

3.1 Establish a model of inventory EOQ ( The Economic Order Quantity ( EOQ ) Inventory  Model) 
•  Define 
1. When to enter an order for an item - minimum sto ck level. (Re-order point) 
2. Quantity to order – maximum stock level. 
3. Average consumption rates for fast moving items.  
• Design the best route to deliver the supplies, as per bay’s needs. 
 
4.0 Benefits 
 
Between benefits obtained by this implementation are: 
 
• Reduce in dead time to get supplies. (Saves 80%) 
• Improve supply consumption control. 
• Increase quality of Tool Room service. 
• Reduce in congestion and waiting at tool room. 
• Less technician interruptions.
