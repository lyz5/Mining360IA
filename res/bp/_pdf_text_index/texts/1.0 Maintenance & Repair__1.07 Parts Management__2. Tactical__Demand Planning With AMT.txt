1.0 Introduction  
 
The purpose of this best practice is to document and communicate how to use the 3rd party 
software AMT (Asset Management Tool) for mining parts demand planning. AMT is a server or 
cloud-based program (both options available depending on the need of the user) that manages 
the site assets for a customer. The system has been adopted by various dealers around the 
world to manage mining assets, including those under a MARC (Maintenance & Repair 
Contract), and provides total dealer territory component visibility. This tool was developed by 
iSolutions, which was then acquired by RPM (Runge Pincock Minarco) in 2016.  
 
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
OVERVIEW  
The demand planning best practice document provides AMT system user understanding to 
support each customer’s asset needs and have clearer visibility to those product support 
opportunities within a dealer’s territory. Adoption & utilization of AMT systems by dealers 
provides a central planning hub that can be used and shared between internal dealer 
departments.   
 
This internal process should aim to capture field validated data that is utilized across the 
dealership to make sound business decisions. The process owner must ensure departmental 
engagement and a periodic & cyclical process should be embedded into the business to 
ensure the data housed in AMT is current and maintained.  
 
SYSTEM 
In a dealer’s territory where a large installed base of production machines exists, the benefit of 
a tool such as AMT becomes more apparent. Regional population in scope (decided by the 
dealer and based on the points below) for demand planning should be loaded into the system. 
In the past only MARC machines were loaded & managed, but that mindset has since changed 
for several dealers to now load / maintain all mining machines in the territory (i.e. both MARC 
and non-MARC).  
 
Assets generally loaded and maintained in AMT would include:  
• Customer mining machines 
• Machines under MARC and non-MARC  
• Assets primarily used for production or to support production

VALIDATION 
After the assets are loaded and set up in the system, periodic field validation and AMT system 
maintenance is required to ensure the data is updated. Although the frequency is determined 
by the dealer, it is recommended to perform this maintenance / validation at least once per 
month. The data requiring an update would include:  
• Meter reading (SMU – Service Meter Units)  
• Machine utilization (trend) 
• Machine status (i.e. Operating, parked, scrapped, sold) 
• Major component last change-out details (updated) 
• Customer sales status and / or Customer probability of sale for upcoming opportunity 
events (%) 
• Customer’s component maintenance strategy (i.e. Rebuild, Reman, etc.) (Part 
classification) 
• Primary part number of the major component for the specific machine serial number 
(PPN – Primary Part Number) 
• Component schedule understanding / alignment with customer (based on latest site 
plan and condition monitoring) 
• System adjustments in long term planning, aligned with customers plans

PROCESS FLOW 
 
               
 
   
COMPONENT STRATEGY  
Once the customer / dealer component strategy has been validated & maintained in AMT, a 
forward-looking dealer territory forecast can be discussed and shared across dealer 
departments. Three elements of analysis are required as below: 
 
1) Determine whether PEX (Dealer Parts Exchange) stock level is adequate, considering CRC 
(Component Rebuild Centre) component rebuild turnaround time 
2) Determine the piece parts that will be required for the rebuilds using local dealer builder files 
& standard jobs 
3) Understand customer requirements & CRC maximum capacity to determine if peak shaving 
is needed by utilizing Reman major components to support turnaround times & machine 
availability requirements at site 
4) Order the necessary parts and/or components and plan the necessary labor & tooling 
requirements to support the rebuild / replacement events 
5) Collaborate with the dealer’s product support commercial and Global Aftermarket Solutions 
teams to improve the probability of sale %’s for those that have currently been classified as 
less than 50% 
 
PCR / PLESN  
On a quarterly basis, Caterpillar requests a validated, forward-looking PCR / PLESN (Planned 
Component Replacement / Plan for Every Serial Number) forecast for a two-year period from 
dealers. Here, validation means the dealer has spoken with the customer and verified the most 
current site rebuild / replacement plans for the serial numbers in question, rather than just 
using a theoretical calculation to estimate the rebuild / replacement plans. These dealer 
territorial volumes are aggregated into a global perspective to better understand where any 
inventory adjustments are necessary. From an inventory standpoint, the Cat Inventory 
Management (IM) team compares the on-hand and on-order (with our supply base) levels by 
part number to the forecasted demand being reflected in PCR & PLESN. IM then makes the 
decision to adjust schedules on the supply base as needed to meet those projected demand 
levels. After inventory adjustment decisions have been made by IM, the data is categorized by 
value (US$ Dealer Net) by Cat distribution center and by product line, and provided back to the 
dealers on a quarterly basis for those that participated in the particular quarter’s cycle. It 
should be mentioned that all mining dealers are strongly encouraged to participate in each

Also included in the process should be the logical outcome where dealers are placing planned 
orders (CPRO – Committed Planned Replacement Order; or FDO – Future-Dated Order) that 
align with the PCR / PLESN signals received from the site. By placing planned orders that 
align with these signals, dealers can improve the probability their order will be filled when 
needed, given planned orders have a higher priority in the system (i.e. CPRO order type is the 
highest priority order other than critical life and/or war-type situations).  
 
Additionally, the PCR / PLESN information provided by dealers is utilized by other internal Cat 
divisions such as product groups (i.e. Lifecycle analysis, projected opportunities, repair option 
gaps & potential pricing reviews) and regional teams (i.e. Address lower probability of sale 
opportunities to further work these and close to our favor).

3.0 Implementation Steps 
 
Implementing this Best Practice will require the commitment of the following key stakeholders 
to ensure success. 
 
1) Dealer senior level management responsible for: 
a) Committing to the vision of demand planning 
b) Cascading this vision to lower levels within the dealership to ensure all key departments 
are on board and aligned 
c) Empowering the individuals responsible for executing demand planning with the 
necessary resources to be successful 
 
2) Customer facing personnel responsible for (could be PSSR’s (Parts & Service Sales 
Representatives), account representatives, site planners, or other site personnel): 
a) Collecting customer maintenance planning information and repair strategies to be 
pursued 
b) Updating component replacement schedules, machine meter readings and actual 
component replacement data (serial number, major component part number, date when 
service was performed, hours on component when service was performed) 
c) Maintaining the estimated probability of sale %’s and Sales Status for future component 
replacements  
 
3) Service Operations personnel responsible for: 
a) Ongoing Standard Job development  
b) IPP parts identification through average work order analysis routines to ensure these 
are regularly updated as component lives are extended and the associated replacement 
%’s of the required piece parts can fluctuate 
 
4) AMT administration personnel responsible for: 
a) Performing regular analysis of AMT Forecast 
b) Bringing together the component forecast and the piece part requirements 
c) Sharing this data across the dealership to the impacted departments to ensure all 
departments are leveraging the same data / source and planning accordingly 
 
5) Parts Department personnel responsible for: 
a) Analyzing the component replacement requirements against PEX inventory levels to 
determine Cat Reman peak shaving needs, and placing CPRO’s accordingly and as 
needed 
b) Analyzing and placing CPRO orders for required piece parts to meet the expected CRC 
rebuild demand 
 
6) CRC and/or Service Department personnel responsible for: 
a) Analyzing the expected rebuild signals from AMT to ensure sufficient capacity exists to 
address the demand 
b) Planning labor and tooling to align with the expected rebuild demand

5.0 Resources Required 
 
The resources required to establish a robust demand planning process depends on the 
process, people, and system to be leveraged. The process has been laid out in this best 
practice, and the people mentioned above need to have the necessary time to carry out these 
functions. From a system standpoint, AMT has been mentioned here as a tool that has worked 
well for several large mining dealers to support the demand planning process. If AMT is 
considered for a dealer, a decision needs to be made whether to go with the more-expensive 
server option, or lesser-expensive (and fewer capabilities) Cloud-based option. For further 
consideration and discussion, the dealer can contact the regional Global Aftermarket Solutions 
Demand Planner.
