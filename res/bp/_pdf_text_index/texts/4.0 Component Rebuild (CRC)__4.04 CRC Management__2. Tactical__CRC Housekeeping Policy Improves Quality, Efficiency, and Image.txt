1.0 Introduction  
 
Most dealer CRCs promote contamination control disciplines as a means to achieve higher 
quality in component rebuilds and deliver increased component life to the customer. An essential 
part of shop cleanliness and organization is a dedication to a well-defined “Housekeeping Policy.” 
Basic housekeeping delivers benefits beyond just rebuild quality, it also creates a quality image in 
the eyes of your customers and promotes pride, morale, and work disciplines in CRC employees. 
This Best Practice comes from WesTrac, Perth branch.  
 
2.0 Best Practice Description     
 
The success factors to an effective housekeeping policy include: 
• Written policies and guidelines for all employees.  Create the process. 
• Ownership. Responsibilities for shop housekeeping in each shop area is clearly defined with a 
designated individual responsible. Common shop areas must also have a responsible person 
assigned. 
• Policies need to be enforced. A daily supervisor o r management walk-around is an effective 
way to support the policy. 
 
3.0 Implementation Steps 
 
• Create and communicate the policy. Some dealers do  this with posters in the major shop 
areas. There also needs to be all-employee meetings and training to first implement the 
policies. 
                        
  
• Assign housekeeping responsibilities throughout al l areas of the shop, including common 
areas. 
• Assign responsibility for enforcement.
