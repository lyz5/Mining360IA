1.0 Introduction  
 
According to Reuse and S alvage guidelines SEBF8257- 11, worn or damaged housing pin slots 
holes can be salvaged by installing sleeves made of SAE 1020 steel. There is no sleeve part 
number serviced, so dealers typically make their own sleeves with specifications contained in the 
Reuse and Salvage guide: 
 
 
1. Bore a hole into the housing in order to accept a sleeve.  
a. Use a 17.46 mm (0.687 inch) boring tool with a spiral or a straight contour and a 12.7 mm (0.50 
inch) tool guide. 
b. Line up the tool with the unworn portion of the hole. 
c. Be sure that you only bore the deformed portion of the hole. The depth of the counterbore should 
allow the sleeve to remain even with the top of the housing surface. 
2. Fabricate a sleeve for repair from SAE 1020 steel with a 0.05 mm (0.002 inch) press fit. Drill the sleeve 
to 12.29 mm (0.484 inch). Ream the sleeve to the correct size after installation. 
 
 
Ferreyros has identified a supplier of a standard sleeve that meets the specifications required and 
therefore no longer manufactures our own sleeves.  This saves time and reduces cost in the 
shop. 
 
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
The fabrication of sleeve in Ferreyros workshop takes about 30 min per sleeve in conventional 
machine and 10 min per sleeve in CNC machine.  The cost of labor as well as the cost of utiliz ing 
expensive machining tools was considered 
 
By using a commercially available standard sleeve with measures from ID ½” x OD ¾” x ¾ length, 
Ferreyros had reduced repair time by 75% and costs approximately  50%. The ID is standard with 
finish measure from 12.7 +/- 0.005 mm (See the picture 1).
