1.0 Introduction  
 
Transmission control valve testing is typically conducted on the transmission test bench or while 
the valve is installed on the machine.  The valve is tested for appropriate clutch pressures and 
clutch modulation characteristics. 
 
Efficient and accurate valve testing is an important part of the component repair/rebuild process.  
A valve that performs inaccurately can result in poor truck cycle times, reduced transmission 
clutch life, and high fuel consumption.  Other drive train components may see higher stresses and 
reduced life due to inaccurate transmission shift points and poor clutch pressure modulation.  
Additional component failures, increased repair cost, and reduced machine availability. 
 
A control valve test bench improves testing efficiency and quality.  Quick, in-shop testing 
capabilities provide rebuild quality and diagnostic feedback that reduces customer machine 
downtime, dealer repair and/or warranty labor. 
 
Control valve test benches provide dealer and customer value by allowing the dealer to test and 
adjust valves rather than just replace parts when troubleshooting the valve/transmission. It also 
reduces transmission-related warranty costs with enhanced valve performance information. 
 
This Best Practice describes the test equipment, test area organization and process for efficient 
and effective control valve testing. 
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
The common rebuild practice for ICM and other control valves includes: 
- Disassembly and cleaning 
- Standard replacement and worn parts replacement 
- Spring/electrical parts tests for reuse 
- Assemble with original shim packs 
 
The next steps involve installing the control valve on the rebuilt transmission, and testing/adjusting 
the individual pressure regulating/modulating valves to factory specifications.  This process is very 
tedious and time consuming, providing opportunities for dropping shims and other small parts into the 
transmission main case and planetary/clutch groups.  
 
The Best Practice for this procedure would include using a control valve test bench to quickly 
test/adjust valves, independent of the planetary group, prior to installing the valve on the 
transmission.  The bench provides a consistent clutch-fill exercise for the control valve.  It measures 
leakage, determines clutch pressures, and can “trace” the clutch modulation/sequence - functionally 
testing individual valves in the control valve package.  It reduces set-up/test variables before installing 
the valve on the transmission for minor adjustments (if any).  If a shim or other small part is dropped 
during adjustment, it falls on the test bench’s drain pan for easy retrieval.

3.0 Implementation Steps 
 
-  Determine area in shop to be designated for testing. 
• Assure testing area has supporting utilities (solvent washer and compressed air for 
cleaning/clearing, lighting, and electricity as needed). 
• Assure area has adequate space and access for equipment/tools, valve test adapters, and 
parts staging/protection/transfer. 
• Assure area has appropriate environmental contaminations control, similar to the hydraulic 
test bench cell or “clean room” rebuild areas. 
• Oil flow and pressure may be supplied by 1.) The hydraulic test bench, or 2.) An 
independent oil pump/reservoir (optional add-on for the control valve test bench). 
 
-  Determine how current rebuild process would be effected by this capability 
• Adjust product flow through rebuild center through:   
o Specialized control valve rebuild processing 
o Increased testing of valves coming from field 
o “Exchange” valve strategy for instant uptime 
• Determine how valves may be protected for transfer within shops to/from field. 
• Consider the most effective way test specifications would be established, organized, 
stored, retrieved. 
 
-  Establish new/adjusted repair/rebuild time requirement targets as needed. 
• Eliminate the current processes/tooling/equipment strategy. 
• Reduce test cell labor hours and turnaround time. 
• Establish new valve rebuild/test/adjust standard job segments/operations. 
 
-  Install the layout/equipment changes into the shop 
• This may occur in phases, as needed, such as shop arrangement for only valve testing, 
then possibly valve rebuild specialization. 
• Establish valve staging/protection/transport areas for effective product handling/flow. 
• Promote change feedback as installation may show new problems.

4.0 Benefits 
• Saves Labor  – Valve set-up and test process reduces rebuild/test labor.  Valves are 
processed in a controlled area where loose parts are easier to install and hard to lose.  
Test cycles are faster without transmission spin-ups. 
• Saves Time   – Valve rebuild may be rebuilt/tested simultaneously with the transmission 
clutch and planetary groups.  Final transmission test/troubleshooting occurs faster in the 
test cell, increasing cell capacity. 
• Increases job flexibility  – Testing is performed independent of the planetary/clutch group, 
simplifying transmission diagnostic for the rebuild and repair shops, and field service. 
• Increases quality  – Less activity on test bench sump pan reduces test oil contamination.  
Valve tests also provide greater quality control to major component.  Field diagnostics and 
root cause identification are more accurate when supported by an independent valve test, 
resulting in the right repair the first time.

5.0 Resources Required 
 
-  Facility 
• Re-organize work area to accommodate valve test bench, incoming/outgoing valve 
staging/protection, solvent washer, workbench (as needed).  Provide clean room 
environment, electrical and hydraulic utilities as needed.  
 
-  Support Equipment 
• Test bench and hydraulic pump/reservoir as needed. 
• Cabinet for tools/misc parts/supplies. 
• Workbench/vise and general/specific task lighting as needed. 
• Solvent washer and compressed air filter, regulator, and blowgun. 
• Staging racks/enclosures. 
 
-  People 
• Review current valve processing concerns with rebuild team. 
• Establish process, layout, and equipment improvements through team. 
 
-  Training 
• Define/establish/enforce new processes, roles and responsibilities with production teams. 
• Train valve rebuild and testing technicians as needed.
