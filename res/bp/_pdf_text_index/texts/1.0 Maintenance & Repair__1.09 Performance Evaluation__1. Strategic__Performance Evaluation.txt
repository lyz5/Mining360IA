1.0 Introduction  
 
The principal mission of the maintenance organization is the preservation of the primary function 
of the equipment throughout his productive life. All routines and processes required to maintain 
the equipment function must be established in order to minimize equipment downtime and the 
resulting production losses. 
 
Caterpillar Global Mining Maintenance & Repair process contains ten distinctive or specific sub 
processes that are organized in five principal steps: 
 
Detect the Maintenance & Repair Needs 
Plan and schedule the Maintenance Events  
Execute repairs efficiently and effectively 
Evaluate results 
Correct or Improve through the application of direct known actions and/or by Continuous 
Improvement. 
 
As it can be seen one of the key steps is the evaluation of results. The work is not completed at 
the end of the execution of the repairs. Effective management requires measuring performance or 
result, comparing these results with targets and goals, discovering or identifying the problems or 
sub-standard condition, focusing prioritized actions to improve, correct or ensure sustainability of 
gains already obtained. 
 
This Strategic Best Practice is dedicated to describe the overall process and routines of the M&R 
Performance Evaluation Process and how it will together with the rest of the M&R processes 
support your Maintenance organization to achieve the expected results. 
 
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
The demanding goals of today’s Mining Industry has motivated and reinforced the continuous 
search of operating efficiency in all areas of the production process. Mobile equipment plays an 
important role in the achievement of these goals, and their ability to perform according to the 
specifications relies on the observation of three major factors: Maintenance, Design and 
Application. Each of these three areas has to be considered and managed effectively to optimize 
the productivity of the equipment.  
 
A well designed and effectively implemented Maintenance System considers and manages all the 
variables related to these 3 major factors, Maintenance, Design and Application through a series 
of various specific processes with specific outcome that supports the final results of the mining 
equipment fleet. 
 
“You can’t Manage what you don’t Measure”, is an old and well known management expression. It 
reinforces the unavoidable need for measuring, to detect if the outcome of any actions or 
processes is getting better or worse.

Step 1: Strategy Definition 
 
In this section the definition of 6 basic questions are address: WHAT – FOR WHAT – HOW – 
WITH WHAT – WHEN – WHERE ARE WE. 
 
WHAT: what is our mission statement … what do we supposed to do.  
FOR WHAT: What are our goals, objectives, targets of the overall operation and sub processes. 
HOW: Description of procedures and methodology on how we are going to do it. 
WITH WHAT: What are our resources to execute the tasks and achieve the mission goals and 
objectives. 
WHEN: when are the tasks performed, with what frequency, etc. 
WHERE ARE WE: A necessary step to be able to manage the process. In this step we define the 
reporting system.

M&R Processes Goals-Outcomes and KPI’s Definition 
 
We measure because we want to be able to apply the correct actions, manage the processes  and 
ensure the achievement of a specific outcome. The identification of the outcome(s) of a particular  
process is the first step in defining the most appropriate metrics to use. 
 
The Performance Evaluation process takes the lead in linking the o utcome of o verall M&R 
operation and the outcome of each of the M&R processes with the recommended metrics. 
 
M&R Outcomes and KPI’s – Top Level Outcome 
 
First to define is the overall outcome the M&R organization has to aim for. 
 
Top Tier Results 
 
The final performance of the equipment fleet is our goal and ultimate outcome. We can summarize 
this outcome or goal as: 
 
“Maintain the equipment in optimum conditions to support the current and future  
production goals and the operation cost” 
 
The following measures are “Top Tier” performance metrics or Key Performance Indicators (KPI’s) 
that enable management to assess at a high level the results of the mobile equipment fleet. 
 
 Production Plan Compliance   
This metrics is  a comparison between production plan and actual production tonnage, 
expressed in percentage.  Even though this metric relates to the customer meeting the 
production, it is also important for the maintenance organization to understand the core 
business and their participation in the final production value chain 
 
Production Plan Compliance = Actual Production Tonnage
Production Plan Tonnage × 100 (%)  
 
 Utilization (Asset and Availability) 
Utilization or operating hours is the metrics that will provide an indication of revenue (for 
internal business) and level of utilization of the equipment to support the production plan.  
Asset Utilization  is the proportion of time that a machine is operating (operating hours) 
divided by the total calendar time in the period, expressed as a percentage. 
 
Asset Utilization = Operating Hours
Total Calendar Hours × 100 (%)

Another metric to measure the utilization is Utilization of Availability . This metric is defined 
as the ratio of operating hours to the time the machine is capable of functioning (available 
hours). 
 
Utilization of Availability = Operating Hours
Operating Hours +  Standby Hours × 100 (%) 
 
 Availability (Physical, Contractual and Index) 
The availability metrics provide the total equipment hours that the Maintenance and Repair 
organization is giving to the production department. This is a good indication of the 
maintenance organization capability to support the production plan. There are three types of 
availability which are normally used. 
 
Contractual Availability 
Contractual Availability is the ratio of time that a machine is capable of functioning in the 
intended operation (available hours) to total hours in the period under consideration (typically 
either based on calendar or scheduled hours), expressed as a percentage. The calculation of 
available hours is not a pure calculation since the result is amended by dow ntime hours that 
are specifically excluded or limited by the terms of the contract. 
 
Contractual Availability  = Total Hours ∗ − MARC Downtime Hours
Total Hours ∗ × 100 (%) 
 
*Note: “Total Hours” are typically either based on calendar or scheduled hours depending upon the calculation 
methodology defined in the agreement or customer expectations. 
 
Availability Index 
Availability Index is defined as The ratio of MTBS (average shutdown frequency) to the sum of 
MTBS and MTTR (average shutdown duration), expressed as a percentage. 
 
Availability Index  = MTBS
MTBS +  MTTR × 100 (%)

top goals for the maintenance department. However, the maintenance department in order to 
have a more effective availability management tool use the Availability Index, that provides a 
performance result that is not influenced by the utilization of the equipment and also it provides

 Mean Time Between Shutdowns (MTBS) 
The metric is defined as the average operating time between machine stoppages. It is a

MTBS  = Operating Hours

 Mean Time To Repair (MTTR) 
The metric calculates average downtime hours (including delays and response time) required

MTTR = Total Downtime Hours
Number of Shutdowns  (hours) 
 
 Scheduled Downtime 
The metric measures the percentage of total downtime hours performed in a given period that 
have been planned and scheduled. 
 
% Scheduled Downtime  = Scheduled Downtime Hours
Total Downtime Hours × 100 (%) 
 
 Maintenance Ratio 
Maintenance Ratio is a dimensionless ratio of maintenance and repair man- hours to machine 
operating hours.  It is an indication of the amount of effort required to keep equipment in 
service, as well as the efficiency with which labor is deployed and the ef fectiveness of the 
workforce in carrying out its duties. Maintenance Ratio can be calculated as either “charged” or 
“overall.”  “Charged” Maintenance Ratio considers only work order man- hours (direct labor). 
Repair Shop, Component Rebuild Center, and labor  is not included in the calculation.  
“Overall” Maintenance Ratio includes all the elements of “charged” Maintenance Ratio plus 
staff, supervision, and idle time. 
Maintenance and repair man- hours are obtained from the work order.  It should include actual 
time spent working on all forms of maintenance, repairs, and modifications, as well as 
inefficiencies that result from inspection and diagnostic time, or any delay for bay space, parts, 
tooling, literature, repair support equipment, decision making, etc. 
Operating hours are obtained from machine service meter readings and should include 
production delay hours.

3.0 Implementation Steps 
 
The implementation steps to follow will depend on the current situation of your mine site operation. 
By saying that, what we are recommending as a first step is a thorough evaluation of your 
maintenance operation. Determine the “As Is” situation. Compare with the recommended model. 
Determine areas of opportunities and finally establish an action plan to correct or improve those 
areas of opportunities. 
 
1. - Evaluate your on-site maintenance operation 
  
- Apply GAT (Gap Analysis Tool) - available through Global Mining Product Support division 
 
 
 
The GAT is a survey dedicated to evaluate the 10 different  processes recommended by 
Caterpillar Global Mining. 
 
2. - Establish the “As Is” situation  
 
 In establishing the “As Is” situation we recommend performing two distinct activities: 
 
• Determine the Process Flow Diagrams of all your processes implemented on site 
 
Based on the observations guided by the GAT tool and the on-site investigation draw 
and document the current processes on site. 
 
• Establish the Baseline.  
 
Measure the current performance results and document them for future reference. 
Apply the set of metrics recommended in CGM document dedicated to “KPI’s for 
evaluating Maintenance and Repair Processes”

3. - Determine the areas of opportunities 
 
Determine the areas of opportunities by comparing the “As Is” situation with the 
recommended processes detailed in the series of Strategic Best Practices (BP 1007-2.0-
1101 to BP 1007-2.0-1110). 
 
4. - Define the Action Plan 
 
• Document your findings. The use of a 3 W form is recommended to record your findings, 
opportunities. 
• Devise and document the solutions for improving or correcting the weaknesses. Use the 
related BP’s to support your search for ideas / solutions. Use a separate column in your 
3W form to record the solution / actions to take. 
• Analyze and determine resources needed, time to implement and expected benefits. 
• Prioritize the implementation of the different actions based on the Return on Investment 
(ROI) results. 
• Assign responsibilities and expected completion dates for each of the activities.  
 
5. - Get the “Buy-In” of the on-site organization 
 
It is very important to get the buy-in of the on-site organization. They are the ultimate 
“process owners” of the maintenance & repair strategy.  Present the action plan, the 
implementation strategy and the expected benefits. Involve them in the implementation of the 
solutions and plan an in-depth training of all the involved personnel. 
 
6. - Implement 
 
Execute the implementation plan.

7. - Follow up 
 
Follow up closely the implementation of your action plan.   
 
• Set up “check points” / milestones 
• Review compliance of committed dates for tasks completion 
• Receive & analyze work progress from the specific responsible 
• Reinforce the positive results 
• Identify and eliminate threats and obstacles  
• Support the ownership of the on-site organization  
• Support the positive change of personnel, correct the negative attitudes or oppositions 
• Make adjustments as necessary 
 
8. - Measure the Results – Improve / Adjust if needed 
 
It is absolutely necessary to measure the results of the improvements implemented at the 
level of each individual processes and at the final outcome, fleet performance. 
 
Measure and trend the behavior of the KPI’s selected for establishing the “baseline” when the 
“As Is” situation was established. Compare and adjust as necessary.  
 
 
4.0 Benefits 
 
“We can manage what we can measure”, this old and well known management principle 
summarize all benefits related to the Performance Evaluation M&R process. 
 
 
We are confident that the implementation of the performance evaluation process together with the 
rest of the M&R processes and the disciplined execution of them will help you to gain the control 
of your maintenance processes. Gaining control of your maintenance operation is everything. If 
you are in control of the operation you can guide your organization and efforts to the direction 
your maintenance strategy and ultimately the production plan of the mine will indicate.
