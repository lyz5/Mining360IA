1.0 Introduction 
Thi
s Best Practice pioneers building a Preventive Maintenance Model which results in high 
productivity, quality and safety  based on implementing effective and efficient maintenance 
processes that can be applied to the 794AC Caterpillar electric drive trucks. 
In 
terms of maintenance, customers are demanding: 
1) Less
 labor (lower maintenance ratio)
2) Less maintenance downtime
3) More proactive vs. reactive maintenance on their truck fleets in terms of overall maintenance.
Wi
th effective maintenance the customer expects: 
1) Higher
 physical availability (performance)
2) Quality and reliability
3) Lower lifecycle costs.
794AC
 trucks have recently been introduced into the Brazilian market and so aspects of their 
maintenance were not well known. The key competition in 320t size class in Brazil is well 
established and has shown consistent and low variability in the PM downtime; the customers expect 
similar from the 794AC trucks.  
C 
Competitor PM Unavailability Distribution delivered in Brazil. They put standardization into the PM process downtime to control the wastes and let 
more time to backlogs.  
St
arting from the demands as part of the Brazil 794AC NMI, the Sotreq Technical Team utilized the 
LEAN concept to develop a PM model that would eliminate waste during task execution allowing 
for more time for backlog execution as required by planning and condition monitoring while 
maintaining quality and reliability. Basically, what we did was make sure each mechanic had and 
did standard work based on previous technical analysis.     
Thi
s Best Practice is what Sotreq utilized in terms of maintenance to introduce the 794AC in Brazil 
and provide the customer with tangible results (increased productivity, quality, reliability, lower 
direct costs) and practices (lower maintenance ratio). This Best Practice could easily be applied to 
other dealers and customers introducing new truck fleets into their operations.

2.0 Best Practice Description 
Normally, when developing a “Preventive Maintenance” program for mobile equipment the 
concentration is on the processes of planning, scheduling, condition monitoring tasks and quality 
worksheets that will be utilized by the PM team rather than the execution itself.  
Rar
ely, the focus is on identifying models and methods that answer the following: 
• How can we achieve the specific goals of PM unavailability (and the targeted performance)?
• What does the maintenance team need to do to achieve those specific goals?
• How should they perform the specific tasks?
• How are they trained and follow instructions for the specific tasks?
• How well do they perform the specific tasks?
Sot
req did extensive research on fleets worldwide to understand the “unavailability of 794AC fleets” 
and then used that information to design a maintenance strategy to successfully introduce the 
794AC’s into Brazil. Sotreq determined that PM duration should be 2.9% of unavailability to achieve 
a Physical Availability of >90%.  As a note, the unavailability due to tires is  ~3.5%, which is 
considered normal for the site of this project with heavy rainfall and poor roads. 
Unavailability Distribution Required for Physical Availability <90% 
Af
ter further research, Sotreq developed a Preventive Maintenance m odel focused on optimizing 
their work through a detailed PM plan with all resources (parts, tools, devices, etc.) and workflows  
(tasks, routes, durations, etc.) well defined and available. 
Ear
ly in the model development, it was important to establish the maintenance intervals (every 250 
or 500 hours) and the workflows of the maintenance execution for each interval (see table below).

3.0 Im
plementation Steps 
First Key Step:  Each PM interval was organized to technically balance the activities/tasks without 
over burdening any one interval knowing that the overall PM unavailability had to be <2.9%.  To do 
this, Sotreq started by evaluating all the PM tasks/activities and determining the duration for them. 
By adding up all the tasks/activities, the final downtime for each PM interval  was known, the 
maintenance tasks/activities could be distributed by person for efficiency, like in a factory, according 
to Sotreq’s LEAN model: 
• Nor
mally the 2000-hour, 3000 hours & 4000-hour PM’s have extensive maintenance
requirements; with this model the maintenance is distributed throughout the other PM’s.
• This distribution is different than what would be seen in a typical “8 Step PM”.
• Some examples include valve recession which was done at 4,000 hours, and has now been
moved to the 2500-hour PM and front/rear suspension charging from 1,000 hours to 500-
hour PM.
PM Downtime Schematic (not included wash bay + 2 hours) 
At th
is site, Sotreq uses the “maintenance ratio” of 3 PM mechanics + 2 electricians for rapid, 
systematic LEAN PM execution. A s we finish up the PM downtime, the planning team can set up 
extra time with the same manpower to work on conditional maintenance (backlogs). Below is an 
example of the time distribution by mechanic for each type of PM.

Fourth Key Step:  Training is important to the success of  the maintenance. Each team member 
was trained by the maintenance supervisor and technical team.

• Overall knowledge of truck/truck systems
• Specific Tasks/Activities that each team member may be required to perform
