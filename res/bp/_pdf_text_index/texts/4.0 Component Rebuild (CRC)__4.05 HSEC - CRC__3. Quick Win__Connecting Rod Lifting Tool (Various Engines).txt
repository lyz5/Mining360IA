1.0 Introduction  
 
The assembly and attachment process for the connecting rod is a critical part of an engine rebuild. 
Appropriate care and handling of this  major component  is critical for operation; therefore, it is 
important to give it the utmost care when completing an engine assembly or disassembly.   
 
During the process, the connecting rods are stored in baskets and must be manually extracted by 
the technician to carry out the final assembly process. The technician must lean over the basket 
and pull th e rods, as seen in the  figure below.  This repetitive process for each engine will 
eventually lead to discomfort for the technician and may even lead to injury down the road. 
 
Implementation of this change is geared towards improving the handling of the engine connecting 
rods by improving the storage and packing process. 
 
  
  
Before        After 
 
This lifting tool aims to prevent hip and/or lower back pain for technicians, as well as minimize the 
amount of time spent operating lifting machines. Overall, the implementation of this tool will lead to 
an improvement in safety standards and improve production time.  
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
Information on the oil pump lifting tool designed is as follows: 
 
• Geometry: Curved cylindrical bar 
• Appearance: Sickle-shaped 
• Dimensions: 
o Bar thickness: 1-1/ 4" diameter 
o Bar + Cone Length: 32" 
o Weight: 5 kg 
• Materials: Steel, aluminum, and Teflon

3.0 Implementation Steps 
 
• Before using the developed lifting tool, be sure to check its condition (1) 
• Place the lifting tool on the hook of the crane/lifting device being used. This will allow it to 
be moved to the storage area for the connecting rods 
• Align the connecting rods in such a way that the tool can pass through  the upper bore of 
the connecting rod. For this application, 3,500 motors were being used on the crane, which 
allowed for four connecting rods to be lifted at once (2) 
• Raise the connecting rods and move them to the desired area . The repetitive motion for 
the technician is no longer required (3)

4.0 Benefits 
 
The benefits offered by this improvement are as follows: 
 
• Improved safety standards, primarily regarding lower back injuries and/or hernias 
• Quicker fastening of the engine connecting rods without resorting to human force 
• Improved production and energy consumed during the assembly process. From 
implementation, four engine connecting rods can be transported using the lifting tool 
instead of having to be carried one by one 
• Improved maneuverability of the connecting rods is seen as they are placed on the work 
bench
