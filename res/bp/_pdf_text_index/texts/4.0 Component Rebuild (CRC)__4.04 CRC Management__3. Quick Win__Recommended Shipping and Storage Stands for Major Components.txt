1.0 Introduction  
 
From the time a major component is rebuilt and leaves the CRC until it is installed in a machine, 
the component is handled several times and exposed to risk of damage each time it is handled. 
Typically, the component moves from the CRC shipping area on to a flatbed truck for transport to 
the customer, from the truck to a receiving and storage location, and from that location to a 
customer shop for installation. 
 
A well designed, sturdy shipping and storage stand can protect the component from shipping 
damage and make the handling of the component from one place to another much easier and 
more efficient to perform. 
 
Some dealers CRCs require that all components rebuilt in the shop be transported / stored on a 
dealer supplied shipping stand. These dealers often sell, rent, or lease the component shipping 
stands to their customers. 
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
The methods and practices used to transport and store a major component vary widely. It is not 
uncommon to see a major component on a flat bed truck supported by a pallet or wooden blocks, 
and tied down with a few ropes and chains. Makeshift shipping and storage practices expose the 
component to potential shipping damage. 
 
Caterpillar ships Remanufactured major components on custom shipping stands, providing a 
secure mount for the component and built-in channels to facilitate forklift handling. A shipping 
stand core charge is assessed with the sale of the Reman component and credited when the core 
is returned. The same stand that was used to transport the Reman component is used to return 
the core to Caterpillar. Some dealers have developed a similar practice between their CRC and 
customers. Many dealers use their own weld and fabrication shops to design and manufacture 
component shipping and storage stands. 
 
Design features of the stands often include base channels to allow transport by forklift and a 
secure method to mount the component to the stand. Components such as engines are usually 
bolted to the stand using the same mounting locations used to install the engine in a machine. 
Round components, such as final drives and transmissions, are usually chained to a stand and 
supported by cradles sized to securely hold the component. 
 
For engines, there are “universal” designs to fit several engine sizes by using different mounting 
adapters. 
 
While shipping and storage stands are important to the transport and protection of major 
components, the stands cost time and money to manufacture. Some dealers have difficulty in 
getting stands returned in a timely manner or not getting stands returned at all. The solution is to 
sell, rent, or lease shipping stands. In this way, the cost of the stands can be recovered and the 
shop can afford to invest in a sufficient quantity of stands to assure that all components are 
properly shipped and stored on stands.

For cases where the customer requires a permanent set of component stands for an inventory of 
components, stands can either be purchased or leased from the dealer. 
 
For situations where the stand is only required for shipping and temporary storage, there can be a 
refundable deposit on the shipping stand when the component rebuild is invoiced. If the 
component stand is returned within a specified period, the customer receives a full refund of the 
deposit. If the return of the stand goes beyond a set time period, a rental fee is applied.

• Purchase finished component stands or design and f abricate stands to meet market needs. 
Designs are often similar to shipping stands used by Caterpillar Remanufactured Products or 
various assembly stands contained in Service Tool Guides. 
o Determine the numbers of stands required by lookin g at monthly CRC component 
rebuild volumes and average cycle time between shipping a component and receiving 
the stand back. 
o Designate serial or tracking numbers for each stan d. 
o Warning decals to designate weight, lifting, and t ie down instructions.
