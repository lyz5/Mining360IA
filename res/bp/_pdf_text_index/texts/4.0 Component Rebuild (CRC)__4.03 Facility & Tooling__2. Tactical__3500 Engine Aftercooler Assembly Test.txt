1.0 Introduction 
 
 
The operation of the after-cooler system is critical for proper engine operation, and if there are 
any errors in the assembly of the system, it can lead to future leaks.  At Finning Chile (CRC 
Antofagasta), we have created a test to detect any assembly errors to avoid any future redo and 
machine downtime in the field. 
 
To seal the after-cooler group, we have fabricated tooling to permit the simulation of air pressure 
in the after-cooler group, and to check for any potential leakage. 
 
 
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
 
2.1 Install the fabricated covers to the lower manifold and upper housing (some models) will require 
a hose connection at the upper housing utilizing a fabricated blocking plug in the hose. 
 
 
 
2.2 Set the primary and secondary pressure gauges to 30 PSI.

      2.3 After setting the pressure gauges to 30 PSI, connect the air supply hose to the front of the 
lower manifold and open the valve and allow the air pressure to stablize in the after-cooler 
group to 30 PSI. 
         Power line   Gauge pressure connected to the supply line 30 PSI 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
2.4 Once the air pressure has stabilized at 30 PSI, turn the valve off and wait for five minutes for 
any pressure decrease from 30 PSI and if no pressure decrease is observed after five minutes 
the assembled after-cooler group is good. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
2.5 Release the air pressure and remove the tooling.
