1.0 Introduction  
When a component needs to be rebuilt it is  sent directly from the mine site to  the Component 
Rebuild Center (CRC).  It is important that the CRC  keep photographic records of the condition of 
the component as it  was received from the courier company.   The main purpose  of this 
documentation is to permit technicians, supervisors, shop chiefs, engineers and all interested 
parties to have photographic record of reception and dispatching of components.   
 
Sometimes the assembly area needs to know how the component was received.  For instance, 
parts were not sent or parts were missing upon receipt.  They have access to this information by 
consulting work order photos. 
 
This Best Practice is designed to support CRC Shops with managing photos taken upon reception 
and dispatching of components.  
 
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
In the past, the CRC would use 35mm film cameras to document the reception and dispatching of 
components.  T hese photos were stored in a filing cabinet , which limited a ccess to them to only  
one person or group at the same time.   This also incurred costs such as, film developing, storage 
and a dedicated person for administration. 
 
Later, digital cameras were used for documentation and the photos were stored in folders on a 
network drive.  This enabled the photos to be more readily available and eliminated some of the 
before costs.  However, some photos were misplaced or accidentally deleted. 
 
In order to manage the digital photos in a more efficient manner, the Work Order Photos 
Administration S ystem software was implemented.   Technicians have this software available in 
the SIS WEB Workstations located in the CRC.  This software helps manage photos of 
components taken in the reception and dispatch area .  P hotos are uploaded to the software, 
classified by work order and type of photo (reception or dispatch) .  T he software shows detailed 
information of the work order  alongside each photo and multiple users can view the same photo 
simultaneously.  Additionally, the software lists all work orders that do not have photos associated 
with them.  This greatly reduces the audit time in the reception and dispatch area.  
 
The process begins when the reception and dispatch clerk takes photos of the components 
(Figure 1).

3.0 Implementation Steps 
 
• Gather information on the volume of the photos taken each day to establish picture format and 
maximum size. 
• Create the database and the folders to save the data. 
• Install the Work Order Photos Administration System Software. 
• Conduct training for the reception and dispatch clerk and other users (Service Engineers, Shop 
Chiefs, etc.). 
  
The Work Order Photos Administration System can also be implemented in the Service Shops.  
 
4.0 Benefits 
 
• Minimize the time required to access work order photos. 
• Provide a more effective way to search for specific component photos. 
• Avoid the loss or handling of photographic records by the users. 
• Online report of the work orders without photos allowing better control of the process by  
reducing audit time. 
• Allows simultaneous users to view the same picture. 
 
5.0 Resources Required 
 
• A digital camera. 
• Work Order Photos Administration System Software. 
• Contact Dayana Saavedra to obtain a free copy (see section 8.0). 
• A web server with IIS running to host the application, disk space and a database manager 
(Example: SQL Server).  
• Time for training.
