1.0 Introduction  
 
Almost every repaired or rebuilt component must be painted.  A durable coat of paint provides: 
• An easy to clean and dirt repelling product surfac e. 
• Protection from the typical Caterpillar operating environment 
• The ability to help identify any unexpected leaks.  
• A consistent product image.  
• A professional finish that customers expect for a premium service. 
 
In traditional dealer operations, components/machines are typically painted immediately after 
assembly/testing and cleaning. 
 
In the more progressive service operations, piece parts are given an initial coat of paint early in 
the repair/rebuild process.  Once medium/large size parts are reconditioned, exterior surfaces 
area painted while carefully shielding the interior surfaces. These parts, such as final drive 
spindles, wheels, brakes, and engine blocks, are much easier to keep clean through the 
repair/rebuild process. 
 
An effective painting system provides: 
- Professional painted surface finishes 
- Minimal set-up and clean-up labor 
- Multiple user flexibility 
- Minimum supply/material (chemical) costs. 
 
Airless spray systems provide the maximum flexibility at minimum cost of production. 
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
Painting operations are often not a core competency of a repair/rebuild shop.  For that reason, it 
is often assigned to every technician and/or it is not provided effective tools/equipment or 
process. 
 
Most shop managers would prefer to outsource this activity, but since painting is needed on an 
inconsistent basis, it is usually performed by the lower skilled, or all technicians. 
 
Traditional air spray guns  are typically used with a gun cup.  More progressive shops use a 
remote paint tank with an air spray gun.  Both arrangements require thinning the paint and 
filtering it before loading the cup or tank.  The gun cup arrangement requires frequent cleaning 
(daily at minimum) and many shops deal with unclean spray guns that have hardened overnight 
and require extensive cleaning or replacement.  A remote paint tank does not require as much 
cleaning making it well suited for the automotive industry which demands a higher grade finish, 
but may be over-kill (inefficient) for industrial products. 
 
Airless painting  provides an industry acceptable finish without the air spray process complexity.  
It is best suited for frequently used colors (typically Cat™ yellow).  Other colors are best handled 
with aerosol cans or traditional air spray cups, given their limited application.  In addition to being 
easier to manage, airless spray painting typically takes less time and covers surface

4.0 Benefits 
 
• Increased capacity – Airless spraying reduces spra y time with faster flowing paint sprays, and 
applying more paint per coat (fewer coats required).  Paint dries faster because it is not 
thinned (the thinner doesn’t need time to evaporate out of the paint). 
• Reduced labor – Several traditional painting steps  are eliminated: 
o The equipment does not need daily cleaning.  Many shops do not clean their airless 
equipment for months at a time.  They typically shut the unit off and only make sure the 
spray tip is immersed in thinner to prevent dry-out.  
o The paint does not require thinning/straining.  Th e equipment sprays pure paint at high 
pressure (>1000 psi).  Inline electric heaters thin 
the paint if painting is performed in a cold 
environment <60 ° F.  Debris is filtered out with 
an equipment standard inline filter.  The filter is 
typically replaced annually or less. 
o The paint does not need mixing.  A paint 
recirculation circuit can mix the paint.  An 
optional lid (replacing the 5-gallon paint bucket) 
includes a mixing propeller and air-powered 
motor.  The paint never needs handling, outside 
of opening the paint bucket and installing the 
suction-tube/mixer lid. 
o The airless spray unit can be installed outside of  
the paint booth, with only the spray lines/gun 
inside the booth.  This facilitates easier booth 
cleaning and reduces equipment 
contamination/overspray/cleaning. 
• Painting flexibility – The paint equipment needs o nly to be switched on to begin painting.  If 
the paint has been settling for more than a day, the operator may need to turn the mixer on for

• Investment Costs Vary ($3,000 - $4,000 US) – airle ss spray systems could include: 
o High-pressure pumps (such as the Graco Bulldog or King models)*
