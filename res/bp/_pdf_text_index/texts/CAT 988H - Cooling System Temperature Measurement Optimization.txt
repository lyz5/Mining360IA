1.0 Introduction  
 
During a normal cooling system efficiency diagnosis, the measurement of the radiator delta is 
requested (the difference in the inlet and outlet temperature.)   This allows us to  identify any  
abnormalities in the heat exchange system. When the result is different from the specification, there 
are several possibilities such as: water pump, thermostatic valve, coolers and radiator. 
 
 When troubleshooting options converge to the radiator, we must determine which radiator core is 
causing the problem, which can be an internal or external obstruction. 
 
This best practice was designed to optimize machine downtime in corrective maintenance and 
increase the accuracy of the diagnosis by quickly identifying which radiator core has a problem, in 
addition to reducing maintenance costs for the customer by avoiding the replacement  of 
unnecessary parts. 
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
The replacement of all radiator cores is both expensive and timeconsuming.  If replacing all cor es, 
equipment can be stopped for up to  24 hours, depending on the resource availability. This Best 
Practice covers the process to test the cores individually in order to identify and replace only the 
one that has problems.  
 
If diagnosis has found the water pump and thermostatic valves are efficient and not the cause of 
the issue, the individual cores should be tested.  During testing, a uniform temperature between the 
individual cores is expected, however, if any of the cores  present an irregular temperature, it is an 
indication that there is something abnormal and the core should be changed.  
 
For diagnosis of the machine's cooling system, the literature REHS8874 – i05505126 highlights that 
the difference between the lower and upper parts of the radiator must be 4 + 5 –  5 °C (39,2 + 41 – 
41 °F), as shown below.

3.0 Implementation Steps 
 
1. Implement the procedure described in this Best Practice into the maintenance process. 
 
2. Establish a training and development schedule for all maintenance personnel. 
 
3. Monitor and plan incremental improvements to the process as needed. 
 
4.0 Benefits 
 
• Assertiveness in the diagnosis. 
 
• Reduced maintenance cost with trouble-free parts replacement. 
 
• Reduced machines downtime.
