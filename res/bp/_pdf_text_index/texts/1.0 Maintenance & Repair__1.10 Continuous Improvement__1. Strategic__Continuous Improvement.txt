1.0 Introduction  
 
Continuous Improvement is a critical part of the overall Mining Equipment Management process.  
To provide consist product support service to mining customers, it is necessary to continuously 
evaluate all aspects of the operations to identify the gaps in operations and areas that can be 
improved.  This will help you better manage the availability, reliability and cost of the operation. 
 
To accomplish this, a Continuous Improvement program should be implemented that adopts an 
organized approach to define and analyze problems, prioritize problems based on time, money & 
resources, determine corrective action and apply the solutions.  This approach will need to 
involve the entire organization, as the input for Continuous Improvement comes from many 
functional areas within the operation. 
 
The Continuous Improvement process is a loop of an “input of information” that is used in the 
improvement process to identify gaps in performance, develop a fix and the “feedback of 
corrective actions” to the various functional areas for implementation.  The Continuous 
Improvement process starts with  
• Input from the results of the current actions in the form of KPI’s (top tier metrics).  
Example: Low Availability 
• Drill down into the Process Metrics to find the Root Cause of the poor performance.  
Example: MTTR is too high…time to repair.  
• Develop Corrective Action(s), example: Provide training for mechanics to improve 
efficiency. 
• Communicate Corrective Action(s) to appropriate areas in organization.

2.0 Best Practice Description 
The first step is to identify the problems using input from the results of current actions in the form 
of Top Tier Metrics – KPI”s.  The Continuous Improvement process should not be limited to 
technical / "iron" - related issues alone since problems may be the result of operational, service or 
other product support-related areas, e.g. parts. Problem reports should be analyzed on the basis 
of machine, system, component, part number and / or frequency with the key criteria for inclusion 
on the list being costs and downtime. 
 
Once the problems are defined, the challenge for the equipment manager is to identify the root 
cause(s) of each of the problems and to take action to manage them (minimize their impact on 
the operation) until they can be resolved.  To properly identify the root cause, it will be necessary 
to drill down into the Top Tier Metrics to identify the process metric that is associated with the 
poor performance.  Cause & Effect Diagrams (Fishbone) are effective tools to identify root cause. 
 
After the root cause has been identified, a solution to the problem can be developed and 
communicated back to the appropriate area(s) for implementation. 
 
A follow-up check must be performed to validate that the proposed corrective action is producing 
the desired results. 
 
 Ideally, the Continuous Improvement exercise results in problem avoidance or, at least, 
minimization. Steps toward solution may involve modifications to the machine design or 
configuration, modification to the operation or application of the machine, modifications to 
machine maintenance practices, additional manpower, training, tooling or other support 
resources. 
 
This Best Practice describes the strategic best practice of implementing a Continuous 
Improvement program.   
 
To have a successful Continuous Improvement program you will need to include the following 
elements: 
• Strategy 
• Procedures 
• Resources 
• Execution/Implementation 
• Performance Evaluation 
 
2.1 Strategy 
The strategy defines the scope, goals & objectives of your Continuous Improvement Program and 
should address the following areas: 
• “Top Tier” site performance 
• Maintenance & Repair management process performance 
• Application severity 
• Operational issues & operating practices 
• Design / technical issues 
• Costs / financial performance 
• Safety

2.2 Procedures 
A Continuous Improvement program will require the use some type of Business Process 
Improvement methodology that provides the necessary steps to follow and tools to use during the 
various phases of a project.  Some examples of Business Process Improvement methodologies 
would be 6 Sigma and GMP+. 
 
Part of the business process improvement methodology involves: 
• Project identification 
• Project prioritization & selection 
• Project management 
 
2.3 Resources 
A Continuous Improvement team coordinator should be identified to provide leadership to the 
Continuous Improvement efforts within the organization.  This coordinator will provide leadership 
to a team of subject matter experts (SME’s) that will be assigned to each project from the various 
functional areas depending on the nature of the project.  The entire team must be trained and 
competent in the Business Process Improvement methodologies. 
 
2.4 Execution/Implementation 
To ensure successful execution of Continuous Improvement projects, the teams must meet on a 
regular basis to provide consistency and follow-up on the established work plans.  To make sure 
that the project stays on track, a 3W work plan template should be used to identify: 
 
• What:  Task or action to be performed 
• Who:  Person responsible for completing the task 
• When: Date that the task is expected to be completed

For a fully functional Continuous Improvement program, performance must be measured against 
pre-established Top Tier Metrics (KPI’s) and reported on a regular basis.

3.0 Implementation Steps 
The Continuous Improvement process consists of the following steps and tools: 
 
• Identifying key problems that are affecting the performance 
o Comparing actual vs. planned results using KPI’s. 
o Problem reports 
o Root Cause Analysis (Fishbone Analysis) 
o Site/Operation Audits 
 Site Assessment Tool 
 Contamination Control Compliance Guide 
 Gap Analysis Tool 
• Quantify the impact on the fleet operation 
o Cost 
o Availability 
o Repair Frequency 
o Repair Duration 
o Manpower & tooling Requirements 
• Prioritizing the problems for selection of candidate Continuous Improvement Projects 
o A filtering process to evaluate the cost & ease of implementation 
• Identification of root cause by drilling down into the process metrics to determine the 
element that is causing the poor performance.  
o Cause & Effect Diagrams (Fishbone) 
• Identify potential solutions & determine best course of action 
o Pugh Matrix evaluation 
• Develop an action plan which will define the following: 
o What is the corrective action? 
o Who is responsible for implementation of this action? 
o When will this corrective action be completed? 
• Implement and complete the plan 
• Monitor and quantify the results to verify the effects of the corrective action. 
o Establish & monitor Continuous Improvement KPI’s 
 
Each of these steps should be followed using Business Process Improvement methodology and 
tools available.

4.0 Benefits 
Successful implementation and execution of a Continuous Improvement program will: 
• Deliver quality support services in an efficient manner 
• Improve profitability 
• Improve customer satisfaction through  
o Increased availability 
o Lower cost per ton 
o Increased productivity 
 
 
5.0 Resources Required 
Human Resources:  Human resource requirements associated with the implementation and 
execution of Continuous Improvement include: 
• Team coordinator 
o Provides overall leadership to team. 
• Subject Matter Experts  
o Function as team members with specific areas of expertise 
 
Logistical Resources:  Logistical resource requirements associated with the implementation and 
execution of Continuous Improvement include: 
• Information  
o Regular input (monthly reports) from other functional areas 
o KPI’s/Top Tier Metrics 
o Problem reports, etc.
