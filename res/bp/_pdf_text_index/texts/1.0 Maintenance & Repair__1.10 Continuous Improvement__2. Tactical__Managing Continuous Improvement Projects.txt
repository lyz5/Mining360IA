1.0 Introduction  
 
Almost every dealer has an annual Continuous Improvement plan with goals and objectives.  
Many of these goals and objectives center on adding new service capability, expanding facilities 
or level of technicians, adding tooling, or improving on existing tools, facilities, or processes. The 
end objective is to achieve higher performance in terms of revenue, efficiency, quality, lower 
costs, and customer satisfaction. 
 
Often, there is no organized or formal process to manage Continuous Improvement. This Best 
Practice discusses a formal process to identify ideas for improvement, evaluate those ideas, 
develop an implementation plan, and document the benefits. 
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
Why create a formalized Continuous Improvement process? 
• Identify good ideas 
• Research the best ideas from other dealers and Caterpillar 
• Improve internal communications in the development of annual improvement plans 
• Document costs versus benefit of improvements 
 
The key elements of a Continuous Improvements process include: 
• Ownership.  Appoint a person to be responsible for managing Continuous Improvement 
• Idea search/replication.   A process to identify new opportunities and ideas.   
• Review team.  Evaluate new ideas, prioritize, and select for implementation. 
 
      Additional important elements include upper management support, budget where required, and  
      employee involvement. 
            
3.0 Implementation Steps 
 
Ownership 
Appoint a Continuous Improvement Champion and define roles and responsibilities.  This person 
would be responsible for the search and collection of ideas for improvement and lead a team in 
the evaluation of ideas.  Typically, the CI Champion role is a 6 Sigma Black Belt or Master Black 
Belt. 
 
Search for new ideas. 
There are many sources for new ideas.  Some example sources are listed below. These sources 
should be checked on a regular basis for new ideas that may be applicable to your organization. 
Often ideas, such as those posted on the Global Mining Best Practices website, have already 
been developed elsewhere. They represent “replication” opportunities.

Sources for Ideas 
• Caterpillar Global Mining Best Practices website contains an archive of shared ideas from 
other dealers and minesites.   
• Dealer Black Belt Knowledge Network contains 6Sigma project summaries from other 
dealers. 
o Link:   https://kn.cat.com/cat.cfm?id=6139&ke=1&kefolder=7632#currentKE

dealers. 
o Link:   https://kn.cat.com/cat.cfm?id=14304&pur=1#currentPUR 
• Employee Suggestion programs 
• Visits to other Dealers. 
• Caterpillar Conferences and Events. 
• Customer Feedback 
 
Review and Evaluation 
A team consisting of members who would be affected should review each idea under evaluation.  
The team would include management, supervisors, and subject matter experts as required.  
The team would determine: 
• Does the idea apply? Is it needed? 
• What is the cost and time required to implement? 
• What is the expected benefit? 
• Implementation recommendation and priority.
