1.0 Introduction  
 
Great attention is given today to maintaining the cleanliness of parts, both new and reused, 
throughout the rebuild process.  The most common method to protect parts in staging is using 
plastic wrap around a storage box containing the parts or shrink-wrapping the individual parts 
themselves.  New parts are typically delivered to the workstation in their factory packaging, so the 
technician must do the unwrapping and waste disposal of the packaging materials. 
 
An alternative method of protecting parts is the growing use of sealed storage cabinets.   This 
reduces costs in several ways: 
 
• Far less consumption of plastic wrap and shrink-wr ap materials. 
• Time spent in wrapping and unwrapping parts signif icantly reduced.

Fixed Cabinets 
 
Fixed cabinets can be permanently located in a work bay or in a general staging area. 
 
             
  
 
The above photos are from CI Walker.  Cabinets have plexiglass doors that open and close for 
access.  These cabinets provide the most efficiency if located between the piston, rod, liner 
rebuild area and the general short block or general engine assembly bays. This placement 
eliminates one stage of handling and greatly reduces incidental handling damage exposure.  
Parts are loaded through the back and retrieved for final assembly through the front.

3.0 Implementation Steps 
 
• Identify areas in the shop where disposable plasti c or shrink-wrap is being used to protect 
parts in process. 
• Identify areas in shop where skilled technicians s pend time unwrapping or unpacking 
parts. 
• Compare cost of consumables to cost of permanent s torage cabinets. 
• Design / fabricate cabinets. 
 
 
4.0 Benefit 
 
• Rebuild cleanliness standards are maintained. 
• Parts in process are kept organized. 
• Efficient use of skilled assembly technician time 
• No lost time wrapping, unwrapping, or searching fo r parts 
• Effective use of out-of-bay staging and floor spac e 
• Less cost of consumables. 
 
 
5.0 Resources Required 
 
• Metal shop 
• Dealer fabricated costs for cabinets can vary from  approximately $800 for a simple design 
to $4000 for a large high capacity cabinet.
