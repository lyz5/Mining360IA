1.0 Introduction  
 
The two critical success factors to contract profitability are the Project Manager and effective 
measurement of contract performance. The Project Manager is the primary decision maker and 
represents the dealer’s best interests on the mine site. Measuring contract performance will be 
the gauge of contract profitability but will also provide a process to indicate how well the project 
manager is making decisions. The ultimate objective in measuring contract performance is to 
provide early warnings of potential contract shortfalls to the dealer management group. If an 
operation is projecting an unfavorable trend, Dealers and Caterpillar can partner together to apply 
resources to turn the trend around before it is too late. Time is the key advantage to turning 
around a negative trend. The earlier the measuring process is initiated, the earlier dealer 
management will realize a project needs help. The best-case scenario initiates the measurement 
process as soon as the project becomes operational. The Preventive Maintenance service routine 
can be measured against standard job variance or a time requirement guide right from the very 
first PM service performed. This practice can set the standard for the entire project and start from 
day one. 
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
Using 6 Sigma and input from dealers, Cat Global Mining 
determined the top five metrics to measure contract profitability 
in conjunction with dealer support. They are: revenue/expense 
variance, contractual availability, payload compliance, 
component management, and budget/cost variance. These 
metrics are most important in providing the “early warnings” 
required to prevent a potentially unprofitable contract.  
 
Two time frames are considered for measurement: “to-date” and 
“projected”. The “to-date” time frame indicates how the project is 
currently performing with the existing management, resources, 
and operating principals. The “projected” time frame looks into 
the future and projects how well the project will be performing at 
the end of the contract. Looking at the five metrics from these 
two points of view will deliver a clear indication of contract 
profitability. 
 
Revenue/ Expense Variance: This variance is the difference between the currency collected 
from a customer through billings based on the service meter readings and the costs applied to 
deliver those obligated services.  
 
Contractual Availability: Contractual Availability is defined as the ratio of time a machine is 
capable of functioning in the intended operation (available hours) to the total calendar hours in 
the period under consideration, expressed as a percentage.

The calculation of available hours is not a pure calculation since the result is amended by 
downtime hours that are specifically excluded or limited by the terms of the contracts. If there is 
an availability obligation with a penalty, the penalty paid to the customer for non-performance will 
impact contract profitability. The ideal time-period for contractual availability is a rolling 12 month 
average.  
 
Payload Compliance: Adhering to the 10/10/20 payload policy is important in maintaining high 
operational performance for trucks and loaders. Monitoring and reporting payload a dealer can 
partner with a customer to maximize return on equipment investment. The elements tracked in 
payload compliance are: % of loads greater than 110% of target, % of loads greater than 120% 
of target, and mean average payload carried per month.

will be a catalyst to continuous process improvement. Understanding component life (actual life

proper tracking and planning. A project manager will directly impact contract profitability by 
maximizing the life a component will deliver.

Budget/Cost Variance: This variance is the difference between the billing rate per hour charged

Projected Cost Variance (PCV): The contract budget created to build the rate per hour is used 
as a baseline. As expenses are applied to the contract through the work order system, these 
expenses are continually compared to the original baseline. The PCV is an indicator of financial 
performance compared to the original budget from a total contract perspective. If this indicator is 
negative, further analysis will be required to understand the source of the trend and action plans 
put into place to resolve. 
 
Escalation Variance: Escalation variance is defined as the relationship between parts price 
increases to cost of goods sold and the ability to raise the billing rate to the customer. Analyzer 
has the ability to calculate this ratio and display in the dollar format. 
 
3.0 Implementation Steps 
 
Contract Health implementation will require the cooperation of several functional areas within the 
dealership, especially without the use of ANALZYER or AMT. These software tools are capable 
of creating projections through contract end of revenue and contract expenses directly from input 
financial information or reports. If dealers do not use projection software, the department 
responsible for customer billings will provide the “to-date” and  “anticipated” contract revenue 
values. The dealership department responsible for tracking contract cost will provide the to-date 
expenses and the anticipated expenses through the end of the contracts. A dealer operational 
area will provide contractual availability and payload compliance data.  
 
Contractual Availability is defined as the ratio of time a machine is capable of functioning in the 
intended operation (available hours) to the total calendar hours in the period under consideration, 
expressed as a percentage. The calculation of available hours is not a pure calculation since the 
result is amended by downtime hours that are specifically excluded or limited by the terms of the 
contracts. Contractual availability data will be created in the operational area responsible for 
tracking and reconciling downtime directly with customers. The tracking elements are: date/time 
down, reason for unit being declared down, length of down period, reason for down term (i.e. 
waiting on parts, service technicians, waiting on a service bay), the party accountable for the 
reason (customer, dealer, or product), and date/time returned to service. This information ideally 
is reconciled on a daily or weekly basis. For further detail, please refer to the attached document 
Performance metric.pdf. This document provides specific detail for implementing a delay code 
management process, which will facilitate prioritizing the reasons for down time with a generic 
Pareto analysis.

Implementing payload management will require support from the operational department. 
Payload management is defined as an analysis of payload distribution for a fleet of off highway 
trucks expressed in terms of Caterpillar 10/10/20 truck overload policy. The sources of data for 
measuring payload start with the gross machine weights. These can be obtained from the 
Caterpillar performance handbook, machine specifications sheets and information contained in 
the factory documentation for the 10/10/20 payload policy. Ideally, the data is obtained once the 
machine has been assembled and commissioned using an actual scale truck study. The actual 
gross machine weight is an important element for determining the actual payload the machine 
may haul while staying within the payload policy. Factors such as body design, tires, optional 
equipment, carry-back, or other debris accumulation can have a significant effect on the 
accuracy of published estimates. Actual payload data is obtained from the truck payload 
management system (TPMS) or VIMS™ -TPMS reports. For further detail into payload 
management, please refer to the Performance Metric.pdf attachment.  The following table 
illustrates the data format required to develop the reports that will illustrate contract performance 
and establish analysis trends.

Implementing component management will require cooperation between the operational, 
financial, planning and technical departments. Each dealer may be slightly different in functional 
responsibilities, however the elements to be tracked and reviewed will be the same. Each dealer 
will decide where the responsibility resides.  
 
The process implementation starts with planning. Questions to answer include: how many of 
each major component will be required this year and when?, What is the contract budget PCR, 
and how will the dealer track actual life realized? How will actual life realized be improved? The 
operations or financial group will determine when components were removed, SMU, and the 
reason for removal. This group will be the closest to the root cause analysis for the component. 
The technical department will need to know why the components are removed in order to track 
and identify emerging component removal trends. If a trend is emerging, the technical 
department will be in the best position to monitor and consolidate the territory experience.  
 
The trend information can be used in discussions for continuous improvement meetings with the 
customer or product health meetings at the dealership. This will be an integral part of 
understanding the root cause for achieving, or not achieving, desired PCR and the management 
process for improving current trends. This same process will be replicated for each major 
component in the contract. Implementing a “to-date” and “projected” budget cost variance 
analysis will require cooperation between planning, financial, and operations groups. The 
financial planning, or rate development group, will establish the target budgets for the site 
operations groups.

4.0 Benefits 
 
The benefits to implementing the Contract Health Best Practice are: 
 
• Early indication to contract health 
• Thorough understanding of contract profitability 
• Resource management 
• Asset management 
• Measurements for the project manager performance 
 
5.0 Resources Required 
 
This Best Practice needs a dedicated resource accountable for understanding, tracking and 
managing contract profitability. Once trends and issues have been identified, the dealership, 
customers, and Caterpillar can partner together to establish action plans to mitigate contract risk.
