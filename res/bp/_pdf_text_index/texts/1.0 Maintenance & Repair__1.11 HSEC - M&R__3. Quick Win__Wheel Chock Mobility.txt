1.0 Introduction  
 
Wheel chocks used in mining workshops generally weigh more than 20kg (45lbs.).  They normally 
have a handle to lift or drag along the floor with.  In most instances, they are carried, manually 
slid along the floor or transported by a wheeled trolley.  The last option of using wheeled trolley 
involves lifting the wheel chocks on and off. 
 
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
Utilizing a wheeled trolley to mobilize wheel chocks involves manually lifting the wheel chocks on 
and off the trolley.  Dragging the wheel chocks involves strain in the back and knees while 
bending over.  An improved method of mobilizing wheel chocks is the use of wheeled bogeys that 
are dedicated to the wheel chocks.  Figure 1 shows one such example.  The bogey hooks onto 
the handle of the wheel chock and picks it up.  It is then wheeled straight to the wheel and placed 
at the desired spot without any manual handling.  The bogey is then unhooked by pivoting up the 
push handle and wheeled out of the way. 
 
The handle should be approximately 0.9m to 1.2m (3-4ft) high when in the stationary position.  
The height will depend on the average height of the personnel on site.  It should be such that an 
average person can push it along comfortably.  It should be noted that the handle will be pushed 
down to lift the chock when pushing it along.  Make sure to check that the handle will not contact 
the machine tire when the handle is pivoted up to release the chock.

3.0 Implementation Steps 
 
The implementation steps should follow a similar order as listed below. 
i. Locate sample of wheel chock being used on site.  There may be more than one type of 
wheel chock being used. 
ii. Make enquiries to wheel chock supplier if they have ready-made transportation bogeys.  If 
there are, skip to step (v). 
iii. Research for a local supplier who specialises in light engineering fabrication. 
iv. Design the dimensions of the wheeled bogey with the supplier. 
v. Decide on quantity required. 
vi. Obtain quotations and order. 
vii. Mark out location in the workshop for the storage/parking of the bogeys. 
viii. Set out instructions on their use for all workshop personnel. 
ix. Notify workshop personnel when the product arrives. 
x. Ensure the wheeled bogeys are used. 
 
 
4.0 Benefits 
 
The benefit of using such a tool is mainly for personnel health in reducing bending and twisting 
injuries caused by manual handling of heavy objects.  A secondary benefit is the increase in labor 
efficiency while moving wheel chocks to and from parked machines. 
 
 
5.0 Resources Required 
 
Minimal resources are required to implement this best practice.  It will require clearly appointed 
person(s) in the workshop on site to ensure its implementation.  The person(s) in charge of the 
workshop may be the Maintenance Superintendent or Dealer Project Manager on a MARC site.
