1.0 Introduction  
 
The purpose of this best practice is to establish a preventive maintenance procedure to avoid 
unexpected failures of grease  lubricated pin joints  in Large Wheel Loaders.  Depending on the 
equipment application, this procedure will allo w for wear trending and aid in determining useful life of 
the pin and successfully completing repairs before a failure occurs.  
 
By inspecting greased lubricated pins periodically, we can: 
 
• Identify a temperature increase related to the wear  of the pin  or failure on the lubrication 
system. 
• Optimize grease use on every pin. 
 
Grease lubricated pin joints can have  sudden failures, which cause issues with the production and 
maintenance flow of a mine site. Sometimes more grease than is needed is used, contamina ting the 
environment and increasing operation costs. This best practice will aid in reducing this occurrence.  
 
The pin inspection process and subsequent data analysis w ill aid in avoiding unscheduled downtime 
and help to increase machine availability. 
  
By optimizing the amount of grease used we will reduce Owning and Operating  costs and also avoid 
contamination of the environment.

2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
The full pin inspection includes: 
 
• Measuring the joint temperature imm ediately after operation , at refueling  or shift changes, 
and during Pre-PM inspections. 
• Measuring the amount of grease injected into each pin.  
• Document the temperatures and volume of grease injected. 
 
In order to perform the best practice a n infrared thermometer will be needed to read pin joint  
temperatures. To measure the injected grease volume, the mechanic will need a measured container.  
. 
The information must be recorded, s o tha t the data can be trended  and analyzed to help achieve 
accurate repair before failure. 
 
As pin operating temperatures will change due to ambient temperatures and operating conditions 
different baselines may need to be set for each machine. The variation from these baselines will 
indicate that a failure is immanent.

3.0 Implementation Steps 
 
1. Inform the preventive maintenance manager and inspectors of the process 
2. Establish who will be responsible for carrying out the procedure 
3. Establish baseline pin temperatures 
4. Begin regularly scheduled data collection.   
 
In order to set a baseline pin temperature a machine in good condition should be operated in its 
normal operation environment. The various pin temperatures should then be recorded along with the 
ambient temperature. The delta between the pin temperature and the ambient temperature  will be 
monitored for deviation from the baseline.  
 
Once the process has been implemented and a baseline set if during future inspections deviation is 
noted between the baseline and the actual temperature reading a potential problem may be indicated. 
 
This best practice has b een set up in various mine  sites o ver the past 5 years where the 
implementation process took less than a month. The areas involved were Planning and Maintenance.

4.0 Benefits 
 
Implementation of this best practice can aid in reducing costly pin joint failures and the unscheduled 
downtime associated with these failures.  
 
Experience has shown that a specific 994F wheel loader with no inspections of the pins o r grease 
system presented major joint failures. In this instance the repair required two D pins ($3699USD) and 
an A pin ($2 ,499USD), these costs do not include labor to repair  or downtime. The failures occurred 
during the first 4000 hours of operation. 
 
Another machine, which experiences a major load, had experienced 3 pin D failures in the fir st 2000 
operation hours ($5,400USD) 
 
 
Loaders placed into service after the implementation of this best practice in 2007 have had no failures 
related to the pin joints.
