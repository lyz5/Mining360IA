1.0 Introduction  
 
Significant cost savings can be achieved by salvaging the upper- and lower-cylinder block liner 
bores of the C175 engine cylinder block using metal spray technology. 
 
 
 
Figure 1. Pitting on upper- and lower-cylinder block liner bores 
 
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
This process has been developed as a superior salvage procedure. This procedure eliminates the 
need to install a machined sleeve when pitting exists on the upper - and lower-cylinder block liner 
bores in C175 engines. 
 
Upper- and lower-cylinder block liner bores salvage procedure consists of: Machining the cylinder 
block liner bores to remove the pitting, grit blasting the surface to create the bond interface, metal 
spraying the surface, and machining the inside diameter of the bore.

3.0 Implementation Steps 
 
3.1. Rough cut: Upper- and lower-cylinder block liner bores must be machined to remove the pitting.  
Most of the time, cutting off 0.040” to the diameter of the bore must by enough. It depends on how 
deep pitting is. 
 
 
 
Figure 2. Rough cut on upper and lower seal surfaces 
 
 
3.2. Top deck bore and thread protection: Use anti -bond materials to cover and protect all the 
threads and bores which are near to the area to be sprayed. 
 
 
 
Figure 3. Top deck bore and thread protected

3.3. Grit blasting: Follow the instructions in SEBF2120 “Thermal Spray Procedures for Engine 
Cylinder Block Top Surface”. Propel a stream of aluminum oxide against the block surface under 
high pressure to prepare the work piece for metal spray. 
 
 
 
 
Figure 4. Grit blasted surface 
 
 
 
3.4. Metal spraying the surface: Using an industrial robot and metal spray equipment it is possible 
to control velocity, distance and position of the spray with a high degree of accuracy improving the 
strength of the bond and finish of the coating.  For the spraying parameters use the specifications 
found in SEBF2120 “Thermal Spray Procedures for Engine Cylinder Block Top Surface”. 
 
 
 
 
Figure 5. Spraying upper- and lower-cylinder block liner bores

3.5. Finishing cut: Machine the upper and lower bore surfaces according to the specifications found 
in the Reuse and Salvage G uidelines SEBF9185 “Specifications and Salvage for Cylinder Blocks 
in C175 Engines”. 
 
        
 
Figure 7. Upper- and lower-cylinder block liner bores repaired 
 
4.0 Benefits 
 
4.1. Increases the life of the salvage using wear resistant materials to create the coating. 
 
4.2. Eliminates the need to install a machined sleeve when pitting exists on upper- and lower-
cylinder block liner bores. 
 
4.3. Reduces labor time.
