1.0 Introduction  
 
Computerized torque wrenches electronically record final torque readings and enable those readings 
to be stored in a computer file.  This information is especially useful when tightening critical bolts 
using a torque-turn method.  This includes cylinder head bolts, connecting rod bolts, counterweight 
bolts, as well as other applications. 
 
Conventional torque wrenches do not typically measure the final torque after the turn is applied and 
do not record those values.  With a computerized wrench, a technician can easy review the final 
torque values on all the bolts to ensure the final torques are consistent.    
 
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
Digital computerized torque wrenches in various sizes are available from a number of different 
suppliers.   
 
Example is the CDI Computorq (Snap On) ¾ in wrench pictured below.   
 
A recent example of how this tool can improve quality comes from Ferreyros, the Caterpillar 
dealer in Peru. 
 
When Caterpillar changed the recommended connecting rod bolt lubrication from a 6V4876 lube 
to Loctite Molypaste 51049, Ferreyros immediately noticed a change in final torque.  Using the 
6V4876 lube, with 65 ft-lbs followed by a 90 degrees torque turn, the final torque was 150 ft-lbs. 
With the Molypaste, the final torque was 135 ft-lbs.  The Caterpillar recommended lube was 
changed to a Bel Ray 6770 lubricant as result.

3.0 Implementation Steps 
 
1. Research market for best digital torque wrench that meet dealer requirements.  Purchase 
wrench 
2. Establish standard process to record critical torques and check for bolt consistency. 
 
 
4.0 Benefits 
 
1.  Critical torques are documented and checked for consistency as a quality control process. 
 
 
5.0 Resources Required 
 
1. Cost of a ¾ inch wrench ranges from US $1,500.00 to $2,500.00. 
2. Establish a file to retain records.
