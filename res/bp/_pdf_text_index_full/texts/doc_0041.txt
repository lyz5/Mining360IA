1.0 Introduction  
 
Preventive Maintenance service provides a unique opportunity to plan and execute key Condition 
Monitoring routines that must be performed at regular intervals. The machine is stopped every 
250 or 500 hrs, washed, and remains in the shop for 8 to 12 hours. The planning and scheduling 
department includes the Condition Monitoring activities in the PM services. The Condition 
Monitoring, or “Detection” routines normally included in the PM stops are: 
 
1. Visual Inspections 
a. Pre-Wash Inspection 
b. During PM Inspection 
c. Post PM Inspection 
 
2. Performance Tests (including operational tests such as brakes, steering, etc.) 
 
3. Fluid Analysis 
a. SOS sampling 
b. Fluids cleanliness (ISO rating) 
c. Magnetic Plugs inspection 
d. Magnetic Screens inspection 
e. Filter cutting / inspection 
 
This Best Practice deals with the handling, organization, and interpretation of the Fluid Analysis 
samples taken during PM service. 
     
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
This dealer’s facility designed, fabricated and incorporated four (4) small carts to assist in handling 
and gathering all Fluid Analysis samples taken during PM service.  
 
 
     
Sampling Carts assigned to the PM bay

Implemented working procedure: 
 
1. Initially these carts are equipped with a set of new Magnetic Plugs and Screens, properly 
stored in plastic bags.  
2. The PM service crew selects a cart and moves it to the specific sampling area at the 
machine.   
3. SOS samples are taken, labeled and stored in the assigned compartment of the cart.

3.0 Implementation Steps 
 
1. Determine the type and quantity of samples to handle based on machine models to be 
serviced. Consider the number of machines, frequency of service operations, sampling 
locations (pre-PM and/or PM bay), turnaround time of sampling table (time in the lab to 
analyze and release the cart), etc. 
 
2. Adopt the design provided in the attachment section of this Best Practice, or customize it 
to suit particular needs. 
 
3. Fabricate the appropriate number of carts. Stainless steel is the preferred material. 
 
4. Train the PM personnel in the correct procedures. 
 
5. Assign and train a person responsible for receiving and analyzing the samples.

6. Acquire and equip carts with an exchange set of magnetic plugs / magnetic screens. 
 
7. Assign carts to PM bays and/or pre-PM locations.

4.0 Benefits 
 
The main benefits identified are: 
 
• Improved discipline of Fluid Analysis sampling. 
 
• Minimizes possibility of error from sample mishandling. 
 
• Improves organization of PM routines - resources are allocated in advance. 
 
• Reduces the execution time for sampling activities, which reduces overall PM execution time. 
 
• Improves interpretation accuracy by gathering and providing all samples to one dedicated 
expert. 
 
• Improved Contamination Control of Fluid Samples. 
 
 
5.0 Resources Required 
 
• Fabricated Cart: the cost of fabricating each cart locally was US $580. Use this cost as 
reference only. The number of carts required depends on fleet size and the frequency of the 
PM services/sampling. 
 
• One set of Magnetic Plugs, Screens, Magnetic Screens per service. The quantity of items and 
parts number will depend on the specific equipment to service. 
 
• Assign and train one person in the analysis and interpretation of samples that are gathered 
during PM.
