1.0 Introduction  
 
Typically, a site replaces filters at a fixed number of hours or PM interval. Using VIMS®, however, 
it may be possible to manage and optimize air filter service based on condition rather than a 
predetermined hour interval. This Best Practice discusses the process of Trending Air Filter 
Restriction using VIMS® data and managing change out routines to maximize air filter life based 
on their condition rather than elapsed time. While some particularly dusty sites may require filter 
replacement before PM’s, many can benefit from the economy of servicing filters only once used 
up. Implementation of this practice coincides directly with the steps outlined in the Best Practice 
Utilizing VIMS®  Data for Effective Planned Maintenance.  That Best Practice contains the steps 
for obtaining the necessary VIMS®  data to make filter replacement decisions.  
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
A new air filter typically causes a restriction of about 2 – 2.5 kPa (8-10 in. H20). As the machine is 
run, dust collects in the filter and increases the restriction pressure. Engine performance begins 
to decline at a restriction of 6 kPa (24 in. H2O). Rather than waiting for a predetermined time 
period to pass, use the air filter restriction data in VIMS®  to determine a need for filter 
replacement. For instance, filters could be serviced when restriction pressure reaches 5 kPa (20 
in. H
2O).

Knowledge of air filter life under particular site conditions will need to be developed to determine 
whether air filter extensions are possible on the site. 
 
While VIMS
®  reports air filter restriction at less then 5.0 kPa (24 in. H2O), no action is required.  
 
When restriction surpasses 5.0 kPa (24 in. H2O), or if filters cannot wait until the next PM, filters 
should be serviced. 
 
The Recommended Trend Analysis Period is several days before PM service is due, using the 
last 250 hours of recorded data.  Analyze the rate of change in air filter restriction to serve as a 
guide to how fast the restriction is occurring. 
 
If air filters will last only one PM service and are expected to plug to the point of engine derate 
before the next PM service, then their life cannot be extended unless efficient changing of air 
filters in the field is possible. However, if the filters will run successfully to at least the next PM 
service, plan to resample several days prior to the PM and again assess whether they can be 
extended. Repeat this analysis until a point is reached where the filters will not reach the next PM 
without engine derating.    
 
2.1 Management 
 
An appropriate system for marking filters and recording when filters are changed in the work 
order must be implemented in order to avoid the cost of unnecessarily changing good filters and 
to help extend filter life.  A Condition Monitoring (CM) analyst or field mechanic should be able to 
quickly identify when the filter was last serviced in order to avoid servicing new filters should an 
event arise in the field.  Tracking filter changes may also lead to increased inner filter life when 
one set of inner filters is used with three sets of outer filters.  
 
Air filters actually become more efficient as they begin to become plugged.  As they trap 
particles, some of the smaller holes naturally found in filter media become plugged, preventing 
other particles from passing through.  Extending filter life extends the period that the filter is used 
when it is more efficient.  One of the goals of filter management is to extend filter life, and then to 
replace the filter before there is any decline in engine performance. 
 
The air filter management process should begin when all new filters have been installed in a 
machine.  When the filters are installed, the inner and outer filter should be marked with the date 
and SMH, and the information should be recorded in a log available to the onsite CM analyst.   
This information needs to be accurate and visible to all parties involved in managing air filter life 
(Planning, Field Repairs, PM techs, Reliability Engineer, CM Analyst, etc.). 
 
When restriction data shows the need to replace a filter, the outer filter should be replaced.   The 
outer filter should again be marked with the date SMH and the inner filter should have an X 
placed on it.  The goal should be to use 3 outer filters to every inner filter, so first change = 1"X", 
second change = 2"X's", third time = new inner filter.

3.0 Implementation Steps 
 
Guidelines for Implementing a CM Based Filter Management Process: 
 
• The process should be implemented on 3-5 trucks. The Best Practice value can be 
measured before the process is applied to the entire fleet. 
 
• Start the management process with all new filters (inner and outer).  Starting the process 
on a machine with a new or rebuilt engine is also beneficial. 
 
• Gather the VIMS®  data prior to the PM.  Collecting data at pre-determined intervals will 
provide consistent results. 
 
• Analyze the VIMS®  data.  A record should be kept of how much the air restriction drops 
when a new outer filter is replaced.  It should drop to almost the same level as all new 
filters (slightly higher).  If the drop isn’t this large it may indicate a need to replace the inner 
filter as well. 
 
• Make decision based on the data VIMS
®  provides. The onsite CM analyst should be able 
to use the log determine if air filter restriction is a possible cause of a low power complaint.  
This will help avoid changing a good air filter when there is a different root cause. 
 
 
 
4.0 Benefits 
 
• Saves time and resources by making maintenance decisions based on the VIMS
®  data 
instead of performing maintenance at standard time intervals. 
• For an actual fleet of ten 793’s over 6 months: 
o Changing air filters based on time: 268 outer filters (67 sets) at $800 per set 
 Total parts cost: $ 53,600 
o Changing air filters based on VIMS®  data: 112 outer filters (28 sets) 
 Total Parts cost $22,400 
o Using VIMS®  saved $31,200 
 
 
5.0 Resources Required 
 
The major resource required to implement this Best Practice will be the people and the time 
dedicated to gathering and analyzing the data collected from the machine.  The VIMS
®  data will 
be collected from the machine using the VIMSpc®  software.  VIMS®  Application training may 
also be required if the users are not familiar with the analysis of VIMS®  data.
