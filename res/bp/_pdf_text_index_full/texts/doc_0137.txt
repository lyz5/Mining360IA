1.0 Introduction 
Some specialized mining work still remains essentially manual. This is the case for the installation 
and removal of the spool and wedge retention system found on large mining Ground Engaging 
Tools (GET).  Because the work is manual, there may be increased safety risk.  The following is 
one dealer’s suggestion for an improvement the dealer believes has reduced safety issues. 
 
 
Current process - Unsafe 
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
The use of a hydraulic hammer fitted with a wrist mount that can be orientated on a small backhoe 
attachment installed on the pit crew service truck has removed most of the sledge hammer work 
associated with the task of exchanging GET.  This has resulted in reduced repair time for GET 
exchange and improved safety conditions of the task. 
 
3.0 Implementation Steps 
The customer identified the task of large GET removal as potentially dangerous. The physical 
strain associated with the extensive use of a sledge hammer, and the situation where pieces of 
metal dislodge from either the hammer or the wedge and become airborne projectiles. 
 
The taper pin is installed from the top, and to remove it, it needs to be “unfrozen” with a hammer 
or else from the underside; then it can removed manually from the top.  This is the manufacturer's 
recommended removal procedure for this taper pin. 
 
A team was formed to find alternate solutions. After evaluating what was available on the market 
and with GET suppliers, it was decided to have a team work on a local solution.   
 
Trials were first made with a pneumatic hammer on a telescoping arm, but two downsides were 
identified: the large compressor needed (40cfm) and there are freezing problems associated with 
compressed air in sub-zero temperatures. The hydraulic hammer option was then reviewed, and 
because the service truck already had a hydraulic crane to maneuver the GET, few modifications 
were needed to add the new CAT BH160 backhoe attachment with the CAT H35D hammer on 
the truck’s rear.  The modifications included:  fitting the backhoe on the truck’s frame, upsizing the 
hydraulic tank on the truck, adding a hydraulic pump on the truck’s Power Take Off (PTO).

The backhoe comes as a complete attachment available for fitment on skid steer loaders.  
 
The selected hydraulic positioner (shown in red) is made by Rototilt.  
 
A radio frequency wireless remote control was added by a third party to add convenience mainly

A 45 degrees bracket was added between the Rototilt and the hammer to increase the angle of

4.0 Potential Benefits 
Since the new equipment has been put in service, the only sledge hammer work still done is to 
replace one or two broken adapters.   The above equipment is used for all major work.  It has 
boosted productivity because human fatigue has been drastically reduced, and the number of 
safety issues related to sledge hammer usage has been substantially reduced.
