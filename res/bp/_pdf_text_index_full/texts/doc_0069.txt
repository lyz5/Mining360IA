1.0 Introduction  
 
When the large mining excavators are being assembled and disassembled during build-up and 
dismantled transportation, the stick cylinders are generally left to hang loose by the barrel end 
being pinned to the boom.  They tend to rest on the hydraulic and grease lines.  This can create a 
situation for damage to the hydraulic and grease lines on the top of the boom when bounced 
around.  The transport brackets described in this Best Practice helps in preventing the cylinders 
from hitting the lines and thus avoid damage to the hydraulic lines and grease lines. 
 
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
During assembly of the new machine, the boom and stick cylinders are generally attached to the 
boom first before the whole unit is lifted up to be attached to the upper structure.  This reduces the 
working height if the cylinders were to be fitted after the boom alone was fitted first.  The boom has 
lugs welded on the side for attaching the boom cylinders.  The stick cylinders do not have any 
securing mechanisms.  Figure 1 shows the boom assembled and ready for attaching to the upper 
structure.  The boom is also removed in this manner when certain repairs are performed.  Such 
repairs would include boom pin/bush replacements and boom repairs.  The boom can also be 
removed in this manner for transportation due to weight restrictions.  Figure 2 shows the top 
surface of the boom where hydraulic lines and grease lines are subject to crushing potential if the 
stick cylinders are not restrained. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
A dealer had made brackets to restrain the stick cylinders after installing into the boom.  The stick 
cylinders are then ready for connection to the stick after installation of the boom but will not be 
loose during assembly.  Figure 3 shows a clearer view of the bracket in use on the left hand side of 
the boom.  The brackets rest on the boom by the cylinder weight and the cylinders are restrained 
from sideway movements by the cylinder pins at the barrel ends.

3.0 Implementation Steps 
 
1. Review delivery orders and available machines in territory or site. 
2. Determine models required. 
3. Investigate local legislations and regulations for supporting tools.  Follow requirements if any. 
4. Determine final dimensions from existing machines on site. 
5. If there are no existing machines, make final dimensions when components arrive. 
6. Fabrication brackets. 
 
 
4.0 Benefits 
 
1. Safety with more secure stick cylinders. 
2. Cost savings in parts and labor by preventing accidental damage to hydraulic and grease 
lines, and mounting brackets on the top of boom. 
3. Applies anytime the boom is removed or installed during new machine assembly, or during 
repairs. 
 
 
5.0 Resources Required 
 
Labor is required to investigate, obtain final measurements and fabricate.
