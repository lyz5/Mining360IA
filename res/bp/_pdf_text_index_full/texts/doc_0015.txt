1.0 Introduction  
Inspecting the Torque Converter Sump Screen is one of the Preventive Maintenance tasks and 
should be a part of the Condition Monitoring program. Dealer experience indicates to remove the 
screen for inspection, it is necessary to drain approximately 10 L (2.6 Gal) of oil from the torque 
converter housing. This oil is then discarded and new oil is used to adjust the oil level. 
 
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
This Best Practice outlines the parts and procedure to drain the oil and pump into the tank utilizing a 
kidney loop tool. Using this procedure minimizes the risk of spilling oil and eliminates wasted oil. 
 
 
3.0 Implementation Steps 
To implement this Best Practice: 
 
1. This method can be performed on the following trucks:

2. Install the appropriate adapter and connectors on each truck in the fleet. These parts are 
available from Caterpillar. Reference part numbers listed below. 
 
3. Utilize a kidney loop tool to drain oil from the torque converter housing and pump into the 
hydraulic tank. 
 
4. Remove the screen for inspection and clean if necessary and then reinstall. 
 
5. Check oil levels and adjust if necessary.

4.0 Benefits 
Although additional parts must be purchased and installed, there is no additional labor time needed 
during the PM to perform this procedure. No oil is discarded so there is a savings in the amount of oil 
needed to perform the screen inspection during PM. Since a connector is used instead of the drain 
plug, you no longer have to replace the drain plug gasket at each PM. 
 
Example for a fleet of 50 X 777C/D: 
Assume the screen is inspected at each PM and the PM interval is 330 hours. Approximately 10 liters 
of oil is discarded at each PM. This results in 884 PM events per year for the fleet and 8,840 liters of 
discarded oil.
