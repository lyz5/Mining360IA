1.0 Introduction  
 
Maintenance and repair programs dedicated to support and ensure mining equipment performance 
are always challenged by application type and production plan requirements. Mining customers 
continuously seek operational excellence to achieve expected production at the optimum cost/ton.  
 
In this scenario,  equipment performance is a key variable to control and maintain over time, 
motivating production and maintenance department s to work together toward common goals, of 
which Safety, Availability, Reliability, Productivity, and Cost per Ton are the usual goals considered. 
The maintenance department leads  control for availability and reliability  while the production 
department is responsible for  utilization, productivity and operating the equipment within  
specifications. Both departments share the responsibility for safety and cost per ton. 
 
Ideally, joint efforts of the production and maintenance departments, supported by the equipment 
supplier, should ensure proper and stable equipment performance results. However, due to several 
internal and/or external factors, the equipment performance can be affected and start to decline 
over time. The first indication of this undesired situation is normally reflected in equipment 
availability and reliability.  Subsequently, this will negatively affect production results. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Typical behavior of mining production equipment in terms of MTBS vs SMU (Hours) 
 
This normal behavior, as illustrated in the graph above, can be aggravated if the maintenance effort 
is not increased, affecting availability and reliability  results. When maintenance departments f ace 
this type of challenge, there are several improvement options to consider, namely improving 
maintenance processes or machine application conditions.  Improving M&R processes is a “long 
term” effort that requires intensive discipline and focus of the maintenance organiz ation, which at 
the same time, is being challenged by frequent unscheduled events.

This best practice describes the strategic foundational elements and methodology  to improve 
equipment fleet availability and reliability in a relatively “short time”. This approach is based on the 
concept to identify and recover/repair the worst performing equipment.   
 
There are multiple advantages of applying this option, the most important being: 
• Improved fleet performance results will be achieved in a shorter time. 
• Each repaired machine will reduce the amount of support required. 
• Program results can be measured machine-by-machine, rather than the whole fleet. 
• Repair programs can be executed by a separate parallel crew, without disturbing the regular 
maintenance operation. 
 
This best practice is the result of strategies, concepts, and experience gained when applied to a 
fleet of (61) Caterpillar 797F OHT operating in Buenavista del Cobre (BVC), Cananea, Sonora, 
Mexico. The owner of the BVC operation, Grupo Mexico, agreed to j oin efforts with MATCO and 
Caterpillar to implement and execute the 797F fleet recovery.  
 
Note: Although this BP was applied to 797F trucks, the concept is applicable to other mobile fleets. 
 
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
The procedure or action plan presented here is applicable whenever there might be a need to 
improve availability and reliability results of a mobile mining equipment fleet. This approach should 
be considered as a “shock treatment” and the objective should be to recover fleet performance in 
a short timeframe, not as a continuous maintenance practice or methodology.   
 
The proposed recovery plan is based on selecting and repairing the worst perform ing machines, 
namely those that negatively influence overall fleet performance the most. By  repairing the worst 
equipment, the final impact on fleet performance will be more significant, enabling performance 
goals to be achieved faster.

The recovery of equipment fleet performance must be supported by strong initiatives to improve or 
re-enforce the Maintenance & Repair Processes  to “sustain the gains”  and avoid performance 
deterioration to a level in where another recovery program must be applied. 
 
  
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
The repair plan is designed to recover the “worst” perfomers and includes five different stages to 
follow.  The recovery team includes Planning & Scheduling, Parts, and Service Departments. 
 
The recovery process plan has 5 stages, abbreviated DPEEC. 
 
 
 
 
 
Stage #1:  Detection Phase.   
There are two distinct activities performed in this phase: 
1. The selection of equipment to enter the repair program. 
2. Detailed machine inspections designed to determine the specific repairs / 
adjustments / upgrades  needed to fix the causes of poor equipment 
performance. 
Key points to consider in this Phase: 
 Equipment s election:  Although the Planning Department  leads this , it’s 
considered part of this stage to define and prioritize the worst performers. 
 Performance analysis (for equipment selected):  Thorough analysis is critical to 
prepare and focus inspection toward key areas or specific machine systems. 
 Inspection: Conduct detailed machine inspections with the goal of determining 
all “remedies” (fixes) for equipment problems. 
 Document corrective actions: Generate all b acklogs and documentation to 
support the repair program definition.

Stage #2:  Planning Phase. 
Planning drives three main activities: 
1. Evaluation and selection of equipment to enter the repair program. 
2. Definition of the detailed repair program. 
3. Setup of targeted performance goals for equipment after the repairs. 
Planning & Scheduling is involved in other stages, but now we will focus in stage 2. 
Key points to consider in this stage: 
 Equipment Ranking: Based on equipment performance, Planning determines 
equipment rankings used to select equipment that will enter the repair process. 
 Lead the scheduling process for the detailed inspections and repairs. 
 Determine the detailed repair plan based on the inspection results. 
 Define the Post Repair performance targets of the equipment. 
 
 
Stage #3:  Execution Phase. 
This stage is where all planned repairs completed. The main responsibilities of the 
repair crew are to perform with effectiveness (quality)  and efficiency (speed) , 
observing the duration and quality targets defined by Planning. 
Key points to consider in this stage: 
 Ensure completion of all repairs planned. 
 Manage resources to achieve repair efficiency and quality. 
 Maintain communication to the entire organization, informing of repair progress 
and new-found defects. 
 Work closely with Planning to ensure the best program results. 
 
 
Stage #4:  Evaluation Phase.  
This stage includes two types of evaluation: 
1. Execution of the repair program. 
2. Performance of the equipment “post-repair”. 
Key points to consider in this stage: 
 Repair Program: What “went right” or what “went w rong” with respect to the 
program and the execution of it. 
 Post-repair:  Equipment performance results after returned to operation.  
 Follow-up: It is very important to consider a follow crew to ensure machines 
operate as expected and symptoms of abnormal conditions are promptly fixed. 
 It’s important to use the correct KPI´s and targets defined in the Planning Phase. 
 
 
Stage #5:  Correct / Improve Phase 
Continuous Improvement is applied, directed to any phase of the process. 
Key points to consider in this stage: 
 The recovery team analyzes performance of each phase of the DPEEC process. 
 Identify opportunities to improve. 
 Generate 3W action plans  to follow -up the corrective/improvement actions 
implementation. 
 After validation of improvement actions, solutions must be incorporated into the 
recovery plan process.

3.0 Implementation Steps 
 
This describes the steps to implement and execute this best practice, allowing the project to achieve 
successful results.  Several adjustments introduced after the original plan are included here.  
 
Customer’s Needs Identification 
 
The first step is to clearly identify the pain or customer needs , regarding equipment fleet 
performance. In the case of BVC, the concerns were 797F fleet availability and reliability results. 
 
To identify the best course of action, a team of MATCO and Caterpillar specialists visited the mine 
and evaluated the maintenance and repair processes and practices. In this case, the customer 
provided all needed information and supported this evaluation.  
 
This team highlighted several M&R opportunities that related to or caused low equipment 
performance.  The team indicated the need to launch M&R process improvements, but also realized 
that fleet performance instability (reflected in high percent of unscheduled work) would make this 
effort difficult to successfully complete.  
 
This challenge indicated that the best course of action would be to stabilize the fleet performance 
results (short-term) and improve M&R processes to ensure sustainability (medium/long term).

Project Approval 
 
The MATCO and Caterpillar team presented a proposal to the customer mining department, which 
included two main initiatives, a short-term plan and medium/long term plan. 
 
The short-term plan was designed as a “shock treatment”, to stabilize fleet performance in a short 
time period by identifying then repairing the worst performing machines. This would reduce 
workload of the M&R team, allowing them to focus on improving the M&R processes.  
 
The medium/long term plan would focus to improve M&R processes, support the efforts of the 
maintenance organization, and allow them to be self-sufficient and achieve the expected equipment 
performance results.  
 
In summary, the alternatives presented and accepted by Grupo Mexico, included a short -term  
project plan and a medium/long term project plan. The graphics below illustrate the objectives and 
the expected outcomes of each. 
 
 
Short term Project 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
As mentioned before, short -term project o bjectives were to stabilize and improve the 797F fleet 
performance results. The three main activities considered were: 
1. Repair the worst performing trucks. 
a. The main metrics to consider are physical availability and MTBS. 
b. Additional metrics for consolidation are MTBF and the ratio of MTBS/MTBF 
2. Actively implement quick wins related to M&R processes. 
3. Work to improve the equipment application and operation (if possible).

The main initiatives of the medium/long term plan were designed to provide the right tools and

results achieved by the short-term plan. The four main initiatives were: 
1. Improve and implement all updated M&R processes. 
2. Develop processes owners of MATCO and BVC.

These objectives were to align the team, define the “pain”, summarize the project objectives, and 
clearly document commitments and expectations.

establishing expected performance results (in terms of availability). A big part of the recovery plan’s 
success was to build a solid methodology to select  machines and estimate the impact of each 
machine repaired.  
 
The following is the calculation methodology used to determine the impact of each machine 
repaired, based on the initial availability performance of a specific machine. The impact calculation 
will easily highlight that highest positive impact on the equipment fleet is obtained by selecting and 
repairing the worst performing equipment.  This methodology will also help determine the recovery

5.0 Resources Required 
 
Successfully implementing a fleet recovery plan such as this one, requires assigning adequate 
resources to ensure the proper execution of all steps and phases of the plan. It’s recommended for 
the recovery plan to be implemented separately from the regular daily M&R activities. That means 
that the resources here included are to be considered 100% dedicated solely to the recovery plan.  
 
When properly selected and managed, the five types of resources noted below guarantee the 
established goals for future recovery repairs will be achieve without setbacks. The main types are: 
 Human Resources – Personnel 
 Manual and specialized tools 
 Facilities – Repair shop bay and Offices 
 Parts and Components 
 Vehicles – Follow up and Service 
 
 
HR – Personnel 
 
Personnel is possibly the most important resource to be considered and selected in advance of 
project startup. The project team followed and recommend are to carefully select adequate people 
to fill the responsibilities of: 
• Leadership  
• Repair Execution  
 
The people assigned to leadership roles were tasked with the following responsibilities:  
• Customer relationships. 
• Project management. 
• Planning. 
• Coordination of parts and components. 
• Guide and manage the execution of repairs. 
• Supervise the “follow up” of equipment already repaired. 
 
The technicians selected for repair execution must have: 
• Solid knowledge of the machines being repaired,  
• Experience in component replacement (remove, install, commissioning),  
• Troubleshooting,  
• Data analysis,  
• Inspections routines,  
• A very proactive approach to failure avoidance  
• Full commitment for all safety guidance and procedures.  
 
The quantity of technicians and supervisors will depend on the quantity of machines to be repaired 
in parallel (simultaneously).  The quality and experience of t echnicians is critical, and these 
resources cannot be underestimated.  In the case of this project, the team selected and recruited 
the best technicians throughout the entire MATCO dealership.
