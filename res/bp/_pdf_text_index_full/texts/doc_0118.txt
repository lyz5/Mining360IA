1.0 Introduction  
Properly designed and optimized haul roads can help to reduce cycle times, reduce fuel burn, 
reduce component wear, and increase profit.  The haul road optimization process is designed to 
identify haul roads that don’t meet optimum performance criteria and improve them to realize cycle 
time and fuel gains.  The Global Mining CI team has developed a process to provide several 
optimized options for haul roads and provide estimates in the gains or benefits that could be 
realized with each option. 
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
2.1  Opportunity Recognition 
 The best opportunities for haul road optimization lie in permanent haul roads with up hill 
hauls.  The optimization process takes some time, and haul road work may not be able to be 
started right away, so selected roads need to be part of the mine’s long term plans.  
Optimization candidates can be identified by: 
o C ontaining slopes with greater than 10% grade 
o S egments of non-constant grades 
o S egments where operators are unable to maintain full throttle. 
o Segments in which transmission downshifts to 1
st gear 
o Poorly designed intersections or swithcbacks

2.2  Data Collection 
 The data collection can be done by onsite personal with or without Caterpillar Dealer or 
Global Mining personal present.  Once a target haul road has been identified two types of data 
are required for the optimization process; GPS data and truck Datalogger data.  Mine 
production and cost data is also required for the financial models. 
 
2.2.1  GPS Data – The GPS data should be taken on the target road with the hardware used 
on site.  This insures the data can be merged with the site property maps without data 
correction.  Data should be from production run only and should not go through scales or any 
other short term deviation

2.2.2  Datalogger Data –  
 Datalogger data should be taken at least 5 times from at least 3 different trucks.  All three 
trucks should have good struts and the oil and grease should be recently recharged.  The 
analysis of all these Dataloggers will help eliminate variations in driver performance from the 
study.  The data should be watched for anomalies caused by haul road traffic, or serious 
driver deviation 
 Data should be taken from normal production runs, not runs where the truck stops for 
scales. Going through the scales adds distance to the run, and affects the speed profile as the 
truck slows to enter the scales and accelerates out.  If the drivers are aware a study is taking

2.2.3 Production and Cost Data –  
Collect production and cost numbers from the mine in order to build the financial model, 
this includes: 
o Target Payload (ton) 
o Scheduled Hours: (hrs/day) 
o On-site fuel cost: (US$/gal) 
o Operator efficiency (%) 
o Utilization? (%) 
o Striping ratio ( tons:tons or yd3:ton) 
o Ore Grade ( oz/ton or lb/ton) 
o Incremental margin on commodity price (US$/lb or oz) 
o Ore Recovery Factor (%) 
 
 
2.3  Road Optimization 
 The data gathered from the site is then sent to the Global Mining CI group.  The CI group 
will optimize the GPS data and provide several options with varying levels of optimization.  The 
most optimized roads will provide the most benefit, but will also require the most amount of 
road work.  Estimated cycle times and financial benefits will also be provided for each design. 
 The CI group may also provide other recommended changes to the haul road, including 
possible redesigns that would reduce cycle times, super-elevations required to maintain speed 
around corners and cross fall recommendations to ensure proper drainage.

2.4  Presentation 
 The optimized GPS data can be loaded into either the mine’s mapping software or into a 
3D program to produce a 3D representation of the road.  Mine software can also get estimates 
of the material that needs to be moved for the road work required. 
 A presentation should be prepared and presented to the mine management that will be 
making road change decisions.  Road options, cycle time changes and financial benefits 
should be provided for consideration. 
 
 
 
Figure 5: Example of road design using a 3D mapping software 
 
 
2.5  Follow Up 
 Once an optimized option has been selected, mine personnel should be worked with to 
come up with a time table for road work to begin.  If the site does not currently employ any 
systems such as CAES, the options can be presented to them as a way to improve their road 
work efficiency, speed and accuracy. 
 When road work is completed new cycle time and GPS data should be collected so the 
actual realized gains can be measured and reported.  The GPS data can be sent back to the 
CI team and any gains from further optimization can be looked at.

3.0 Implementation Steps 
 
1. Haul roads that would benefit from optimization should be identified.  The possibility of 
improvements should be discussed with the mine management. 
2. Data should be collected and sent to the Cat Global Mining CI group for optimization. 
3. When the haul road data has been optimized and returned a presentation should be prepared 
and presented to the mine management. 
4. When an optimized option has been chosen, a road work plan and timeline should be 
discussed with the mine.  Options for additional systems that would aid in the road work 
should also be discussed. 
5. Once road work is complete new data should be collected and analyzed for actual gains. 
 
4.0 Benefits 
Optimized haul roads can provide substantial gains in cycle times, depending on the state, 
length and grade of the current haul road.  Reduction in cycle times can lead to either an increase in 
production or a reduction in O&O costs. 
An optimization process at one mine resulted in a 9% improvement in cycle time on a 3 mile 
haul road.  Additional gains can be seen in component life gains from reduced hard shifting along 
the haul road. 
 
 
5.0 Resources Required 
• Mine site Surveyor – Current road to be surveyed using mine equipment 
• CI team – Provide optimized options and estimated gains. 
• Dealer support – Collect data to be sent to CI team, present optimized options to the mine 
management, and to follow up on road work to be completed.
