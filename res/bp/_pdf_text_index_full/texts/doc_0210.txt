1.0 Introduction  
 
Caterpillar has many guidelines on parts reuse criteria, and inspection / measurement procedures 
to determine reusability.  These guidelines are primarily technical guidelines and do not address a 
“process” by which to determine parts reuse during component rebuilds. 
 
Parts “reuse” or “replace” decisions during major component rebuild are one of the most 
important controllable cost drivers in the rebuilding process. Proper reuse decisions are vital. 
Rebuild cost increases when parts are replaced, that could have been reused. If the rebuild is a 
component exchange flat rate rebuild or MARC contract rebuild, this action directly reduces CRC 
margins. In addition to increased costs and reduced margins, poor rebuild/reuse decisions can 
result in component failure or shortened component life. 

===== PAGE 2 =====
 
Often, decisions to replace or reuse a part will be made by virtually any mechanic in the shop.   
This decision often lacks consistency from one mechanic to another due to experience, training, 
and even a fear of risk.  
 
This Best Practice discusses managing parts reuse decisions through a consistent process. 
 
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
Some dealers have been very successful in managing parts reuse by creating a specialist level 
position in the shop.  This position has been assigned overall responsibility for the parts reuse 
decisions and standard practices.  
  
Responsibilities include: 
• Inspect all parts for reuse during the disassembly  or parts cleaning steps of the rebuild 
process. 
• Facilitate the ordering of parts being replaced or  transported for salvage, early in the rebuild 
process, to avoid delay/interruption during assembly. 
• Assist with warranty failure analysis. 
• Establish shop standard reuse policies and practic es. 
• Create and maintain Required Bill of Materials (RB OM) and/or “Must Replace” parts kits for 
the most common component rebuilds.  
o Establish parts reuse performance analysis reporti ng for training program 
development. 
• Measure program effectiveness through annual scrap  analysis and “Re-do” analysis. 
• Participate in CRC cost management initiatives. 
• Conduct parts reuse, and salvage training. 
• Assume lead-person responsibilities in disassembly  and cleaning areas.  Coach 
technicians in proper disassembly & assembly techniques

3.0 Implementation Steps 
 
• The individual must be well trained in reusability  guidelines, applied failure analysis, and 
the use of inspection/measurement equipment or tooling. 
o The Reuse Specialist should be available for each shift of a multi-shift operation. 
Due to the importance of the position and the daily workload, a back-up person 
should also be available and qualified. 
o The Reuse Specialist would work primarily in the D isassembly or Parts Cleaning 
area in an “assembly line” rebuild process.  For a “Bay Build” process, the reuse 
specialist would float from bay to bay throughout disassembly. 
o A reuse inspection workstation may be required wit h a computer terminal, good 
lighting, magnification equipment, crack detection and other inspection and 
measuring equipment.  Close access to magnaflux, crankshaft and camshaft 
inspection benches also helpful. 
 
4.0 Benefits 
   
• Manage parts reuse decisions as part of a consiste nt process. 
• Remove repair decisions from the hands of lesser-e xperienced people. 
• Reduce rebuild costs and / or re-do risks. 
• Impact CRC margins and profit. 
• Improve rebuild quality and component life. 
• Improve shop efficiency – (specifically if “Must D o” parts kits are developed).
