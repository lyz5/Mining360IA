1.0 Introduction  
 
Safety is a top priority. Often, company safety programs are based around monitoring and 
reporting safety incidents and responding to and monitoring the frequency of safety incidents.  
Local safety laws usually require this minimal practice. As a reactive process, such a program 
has limited ability to prevent safety incidents from occurring.  
 
Some dealers have developed CRC and shop safety inspection checklists based around known 
requirements and collective safety knowledge. Making periodic inspections using a well-
developed, exhaustive checklist is a proactive practice to improve safety. 
 
This Best Practice shares an example of a safety inspection checklist used by one dealer. Due to 
regional differences in occupational health laws and regulations, the calculation methods, the 
frequency of checks, and the requirements listed in the checklist must be reviewed and modified 
as appropriate for your local area. 
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
This is a process by which safety inspections of the CRC are made on a periodic basis, 
weaknesses are identified, and corrective measures are taken to improve working safety 
conditions.  
 
A company safety specialist who is familiar with safety requirements and laws should perform the 
inspections.   
 
To assist with the inspection process, a checklist should be developed and utilized. The 
recommended categories of items include: 
• Administration: Includes policy, records, and other administration-level items 
• Training: Defines the breadth and depth of safety training for employees  
• Risk Management: Performing risk assessments  
• Emergency Contingencies:  Preparedness for evacuation emergencies  
• Communication:  Existence of various safety meetings  
• Management:  Tracking and recording, Safety Act Observations  
• Suppliers: Safety efforts on part of dealer suppliers (i.e., require proper licensing)  
• General Facilities:  Inspection for safety efforts and practices in all dealer facilities  
• Risk Assessment:  Practice of planning activities safely before performing them  
• General Attributes: Addressing various specific risks  
• Maintenance Department:  Safety concerns for maintenance workers  
• Tool Store & Shop Tooling:  Addresses various safety risks in the shop  
• Parts:  Safe practices in parts warehouse including fork trucks and rack storage  
• Laboratory:  Use of protective equipment and chemical storage in dealer lab  
• Contract Mining:  Ensuring the safety practices of the dealer are followed at contract sites  
• Office Administration:  Addresses safety concerns in an indoor office environment 
• Paint Shop: Protective equipment and ventilation
