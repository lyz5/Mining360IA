1.0 Introduction  
 
A fleet of four 994K Wheel Loaders was introduced to a mine in Brazil in early 2020 in loaders feed 
mobile crushers and load off-road trucks at the mine.  It is important to maintain feeding of the crushers 
to avoid interruption in the ore processing to support the crusher operation the loader operate in “load 
and carry” mode where the loader is constantly moving the material.  
 
The mine was designed to be essentially “TRUCKLESS” so the mine does not have fixed fuel stations, 
causing that fuel is brought to the mine’s fuel distribution point and then transferred to other tanks and 
then to the loaders themselves , this  results in poor fuel quality due to contamination and water 
condensation in tanks and ultimately in the loaders. 
    
The loaders are equipped with 3516 engines with an average of 150 liter per hours of fuel consumption, 
in this fleet the Preventive Maintenances (PM’s) are performing every 250 hours and normally the fluids 
and filters would be replaced during the PM’s but because of the fuel quality and contamination issues 
the fuel filters reach saturation with contamination around ~125 hours.   
 
As there is no by -pass in the systems, when a filter is clogged the machine is stopped due to low 
performance this “corrective stop” to replace the primary filter results in: 
 
a. lower MTBF,  
 
b. increased maintenance time and  
loss of production. 
 
In this site, the client doesn't have fuel cleanliness system. It is very important and Sotreq also 
recommends to work on filtering the fuel before it is put into the machine. 
 
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
Adding a second primary fuel filter in parallel in the fuel line is this Best Practice idea that was applied 
to the loader (and can be applied to other machines) to mitigate the poor fuel quality at site and the 
resulting losses in maintenance/performance and production. 
 
With the second primary fuel filter in place, fluid and filter changes can occur at the regular PM interval 
of 250 hours instead of 125 hours.

3.0 Implementation Steps 
 
After a Sotreq multidisciplinary team identified “poor fuel quality” as the main contributor to the need to 
change the fuel filter at 125 hours, a simple but effective solution was implemented in 3 steps: 
 
1) Procure a secondary primary filter (P/N: 438-5385) 
2) Design and develop a support to hold the two primary filter solution, this support bracket was 
developed by Sotreq is shown below.

Additionally, Sotreq has been working with the customer to better understand the machine 
maintenance / performance issues related to the poor fuel quality and consider additional steps for 
control contamination at the site. 
 
As a result, in addition to the two primary filter system on the loader, the customer is in the process 
of implementing a fuel station where c ontamination can be better controlled as well as other 
contamination control procedures throughout the fueling process. 
 
 
4.0 Benefits 
 
Benefits: 
• Less Maintenance / Increase MTBS Performance 
• Increase Productivity / Production 
 
Less corrective maintenance to replace the primary fuel filter, consequent, increasing the value of 
MTBS and decreasing the accident risk of the technician exposure to field work . Furthermore, 
increasing of equipment overall effectiveness.
