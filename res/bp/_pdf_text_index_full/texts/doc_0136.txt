1.0 Introduction  
 
The ground engaging tool in the Motorgraders must withstand high speeds and high work 
abrasion on very compacted surfaces. The tips are made of high strength metals and abrasion 
resistance to provide long service life under tough conditions. However, these undergo wear and 
should be replaced approximately every 400 to 450 hours.  The minimal amount of tip-rippers 
from motor graders range from 6 to 12 through the 16 to 24 series in all the models, with an 
approximate weight per tooth up to 10.9 kg. The tips are secured to the shank-ripper through a 
pin and a retainer; they must fit in well so that the tip does not slip out during operation; These 
conditions during the disassembly of the tip require a lot of time and also physical effort from the 
technician by using a pin puller and a hammer to remove them (see Fig. 1).  The time length 
varies depending on the strength of each technician because they need to take rest intervals 
during the disassembly of each tooth, Moreover, the constant risk due to accidents by blows, 
bruises, back pain, sore hands, excessive noise, etc. remains because of hammering and poor 
working postures during the extended working time. (See Figure 1) 
 
 
 
 
Figure 1. Parts Identification & disassembly process of the tip-rippers 
 
 
This Best practice proposes using a hydraulic tool that facilitates and streamlines the task of 
disassembly of the tip ripper regarding the series and aforementioned models, significantly 
reducing the work time, the physical exhaustion, and accident risk of the personnel. 
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
The tool consists of a steel pyramidal liner A572 GR 50 thickness which is inserted into the tip to 
be disassembled embracing and adjusting it perfectly to its geometry; this is supported through a 
spring-operated lock that is hooked over the edge of the tip-ripper. It is welded to the liner and it 
has a ½" thickness reinforced metal armor secured from end to end with 4 bolts (9mm x 140mm) 
and their nuts and washers. Inside contains a double-acting hydraulic cylinder, 140mm long.

The 1" diameter shaft is collinear to the pin axis that secures the tip-ripper being disassembled. 
The cylinder consists of a quick coupling system for the hose connections to electrohydraulic 
pump capacity. This creates enough hydraulic pressure to get the rod out of the cylinder and 
simultaneously displace and remove the pin that secures the tip-ripper to the shank ripper (See 
Figure 2).

3.0 Implementation Steps 
 
• Make the tool according to the description and attached drawings.  
• Operation (See figure 3) 
 
1. Lock the equipment using the locking tool on the lift cylinder (either of the two).  
2. Check the status of the pump, then proceed to install the electrohydraulic pump and the two 
hoses (Check the oil level). 
3. Attach the hoses and the pump to the tool, make sure the joints are secured and the fittings 
are working well.  
4. Install the tool to the tooth. Place the latch to lock the tool to the tooth. Consider the 
component weight and the body position to work comfortably and safely.  
5. After pulling the pin, hold the tool with both hands to remove it. Remove the tool, clean it and 
place it in a safe place.  CAUTION:  If the pin is tight, it can "twang" out of the hole when being 
pushed out by a hydraulic cylinder.  The pin can hit the next shank and bounce back.

4.0 Benefits 
 
• Easy manufacturing and operation tool. 
• Reduction of accidents due to bruises, back pain, sore hands, excessive noise, etc... due 
to the elimination of hammering and poor working postures during the extended working 
time. 
• Reduction of the technical personnel for the development of the activity (it is required just 
one person, previously 2 were required). 
• C onsiderable saving of time in the development of a task (From 4 hours min to 1 hour  min 
approximately) nearly reduce 75%. Thus, it increases the machine availability.
