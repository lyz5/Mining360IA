1.0 Introduction  
 
Improving component life is a key factor in achieving the lowest cost per ton. Few factors can 
extend component life as much as clean oil. 
 
Modern powershift transmissions and implement hydraulic systems use large numbers of electro-
hydraulic control valves, which require very clean oil to operate properly. Even in gear-case 
environments, such as the final drives and differentials, roller bearings are highly loaded and can 
be damaged from microscopic abrasive debris in the oil. 
 
Fluid cleanliness begins by ensuring that oil put in the machine is clean to begin with. 
Nearly all new oil is contaminated to some degree with dirt, metal particles, plastic, water, or 
other foreign debris. A widespread misconception is that new oil is automatically clean because 
of its appearance. However, contaminants are introduced in the transportation and storage 
process from the time the oil leaves the refinery until it is used. Very little new bulk oil meets the 
recommended Caterpillar cleanliness spec for new oil of ISO16/13. This includes oil delivered in 
bulk tanks, steel barrels, plastic cubes, and small plastic containers. 
 
New oil that does not meet the cleanliness spec should be filtered until it does meet the following 
cleanliness specs: 
  
Desired  ISO 16/13 
 Maximum  ISO 18/15 
 
There are a variety of methods for filtering new oil. These include: 
  
1. Permanent filtration installation 
2. Portable filtration 
a. Barrels 
b. Cubes 
3. On-Machine 
 
The method used may vary depending on a number of factors. These include:  
1. Volume of oil being used 
2. Location 
3. Available infrastructure 
4. Cost 
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
2.1 Permanent Filtration Installation 
Large, permanently installed filtration systems are often the best choice for facilities which use 
large volumes of new oil and have centralized oil supply systems.

Typical users of permanent filtration systems include: 
• Dealer main store shops 
• Large branch stores 
• Component rebuild centers 
• Large shops at mine sites.  
 
Permanent systems usually consist of three major components: 
  
2.1.1 Main Storage Tank 
A large storage tank holds the entire supply of a particular grade of oil, and usually has a 
capacity of several thousand gallons. A large storage tank is required for each grade of oil used. 
 
2.1.2 Day Tank 
It is time-consuming and impractical to try to clean several thousand gallons of oil in the main 
storage tank. The primary reason is that the entire supply will be re-contaminated with unfiltered 
oil when the tank is re-filled. The recommended method is to pump oil from the storage tank into 
a smaller day tank sized, which has adequate capacity to supply needs for 1-2 days. The smaller 
volume of the day tank can be refilled and filtered at night for use the next day. It is also not 
affected by re-filling of the main storage tank. 
 
2.1.3 Re-circulating Filtration System 
Permanently mounted pumps continuously circulate oil from the day tank, through a bank of 
particulate filters and back into the day tank. This process is usually performed at night during off-
shift periods continues for several hours until the desired cleanliness is achieved in the day tank.  
While this system does require a sizeable capital investment, it is the most economical method 
for supplying large volumes of clean oil for high volume applications. 
 
Caterpillar does not supply permanent system components, but can assist in recommending 
proper system design and reputable suppliers. 
 
2.2 Portable Filtration 
Large, permanently installed filtration systems are often too expensive for smaller branch stores 
and mine site repair shops. In these applications, various forms of portable filtration is often the 
best option.  
 
2.2.1 Filter Carts 
Smaller facilities often purchase and store new oil in steel barrels or 1,000-liter plastic cubes. 
Caterpillar offers portable filter carts and fittings to clean the oil in the barrels and cubes before it 
is pumped into the machine. This is the same basic process of re-circulating oil through a filter 
bank as permanent installations, except on a much smaller scale. Portable carts are much less 
expensive, but also filter much smaller volumes and are more labor intensive to manage. 
However, they are the most practical and economical alternative for smaller facilities.

to clean new oil before it is pumped into the machine, new oil may also be cleaned after it is 
placed in the machine. This is a two-step process: 
 
2.3.1 Series Filtration from the Container 
Unfiltered new oil should never be taken directly from the container and placed into the machine. 
This is true whether the oil is being used for refilling a compartment at an oil change interval or 
simply topping off a system.  
 
If a re-circulating filter system is not used, a simple single-pass filter arrangement offers an 
acceptable alternative. The filter arrangement consists of three 5 micron, beta 75 ultra-high 
efficiency hydraulic filters in series followed by a water absorbing filter. The new oil flows from the 
container through each of the five-micron filters in series, and then through the water-absorbing 
filter.  
 
The filter bases must not contain bypass valves, so that if particulate filters begin to plug, oil flow 
stops requiring the plugged filters to be changed. The water-absorbing filter effectively absorbs 
any water in the oil and automatically blocks flow when the water absorbing media is depleted.  
 
Using this arrangement will assure that most large particulate contaminants and all excess water 
is removed from the new oil. 
 
2.3.2 Off-Board Machine Filtration  (Kidney Loop) 
The use of off-board filtration carts is required to clean machine systems after a major 
component or system repair before the machine is returned to service. They are also commonly 
used during machine PM to clean the oil while the machine is already down for service. These 
carts should also be used during oil changes to filter new oil that was not cleaned to ISO 16/13 
before being placed in the machine.  
 
The new oil must be filtered in the compartment until it reaches the desired cleanliness before the 
machine is started or placed backing service.

4.0 Benefits 
 
Assuring clean oil is supplied to machine systems increases both reliability and durability.  
 
Electro-hydraulic control valves, which are widely used in transmission controls and implement 
hydraulics, are very intolerant of microscopic, ferrous debris.  
 
Heavily loaded wheel and final drive bearings, as well as, duo-cone seals are also easily 
damaged by abrasive debris commonly found in new oil.  
 
Assuring the new oil is cleaned to the desired cleanliness level significantly reduces the 
number of contamination-induced failures and repairs.
