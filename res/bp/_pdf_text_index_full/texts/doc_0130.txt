1.0 Introduction  
 
Tire expenses on large mining trucks is often a very significant portion 8-15% of the hourly 
operating costs of the hauling fleet.  “A rock the size of a fist is large enough to cause damage to 
equipment tires” is a common statement that is used to help educate operators as to what size 
spillage and rocks should be avoided along the haul road.   
 
When the operator returns to the cab of a mining truck however, a rock that could damage the 
tires can now appear extremely small and insignificant.  The perception of the rocks spilt onto the 
haul road changes significantly and the operator may elect to drive over these sections without 
full recognition of the potential consequences. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
To improve these operator perception issues Bulga Coal Mine site has initiated a tire awareness 
campaign utilizing Wiffleballs. These wiffleballs are highly visible and of an equivalent size to the 
rocks that the pit supervisors would like operators to avoid however if hit will not cause damage to 
the tires.  Details as to how this campaign is managed is listed within the implementation steps 
below.  
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Wiffleball Campaign 
Softball Diameter – 8.9 cm 
Baseball Diameter – 7.6cm

3.0 Implementation Steps 
 
1. During Pre-shift meeting, pit supervisor raises the issue of damaging tires and quotes 
statistics on the costs to their site due to rock cuts.  
 
2. The whiffle balls are passed around to operators as an example of the size of rocks that can 
cause damage. 
 
3. During the shift, the pit-supervisor is to distribute the brightly colored wiffleballs within the load 
areas, along the haul roads and dump areas where spillage is often found. 
 
4. Operators are then able to identify the wiffleball along the road and understand what a rock of 
that size would look like from the cab of a truck.   
(The intent of the campaign is not to treat the wiffleballs as if they were a rock.  
Operators can elect to run over / straddle / drive around the balls during the shift.  The 
intent is to ensure that they improve the understanding of what rocks should be avoided 
in future.) 
 
5. Upon the completion of shift, a debrief is conducted to discuss how the operator perception 
campaign has affected operators and to hear their thoughts on the exercises. 
 
6. Ongoing monitoring of tire average life will establish the value of this program on your site. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
4.0 Benefits 
There are many benefits that were observed throughout the campaign.   
In addition to the direct perception improvements and corresponding average tire life 
improvements, the wiffleballs were seen in many areas throughout the site.  
They were on display within the prestart and operator lunchrooms.  Most managers had a 
wiffleball on their desks all highlighting their support for a common goal to improve tire life and 
reduce costs on site.

5.0 Resources Required 
 
The costs of the wiffleball campaign are relatively low.  Bulk purchases of the wiffleballs will 
reduce the per unit cost and should be well under $1 per ball. With the above projections and site 
history of rock related tire damage it is very easy to justify a campaign of this nature.
