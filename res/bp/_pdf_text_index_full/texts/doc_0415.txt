1.0 Introduction  
 
Information contained in this document is intended for all dealers globally when utilizing thermal 
spray arc technologies as a primary method for component salvage. This document provides and 
overview of the tooling requirements for this manual thermal spray cabinet.  
 
This inexpensive system was developed to allow small components (exhaust manifolds journals, 
powertrain yokes, etc.). It is a cost -effective solution as it improves the thermal spray capabilities 
and capacities of the shop with a minimal investment.

2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
Complex robotic spraying booths are commonly needed for major component salvage (cylinder 
blocks, spindles, wheels, housings, etc.) MATCO’s demand for smaller components exceeded the 
capacity of the current enclosed spray booth. The addition of a second large spray booth would 
have required significant capital investment. To optimize the resources of the shop and improve 
production output the MATCO Engineering team decided to implement a handheld thermal spray 
booth.  This solution eliminated the congestion caused by the smaller components. 
 
3.0 Implementation Steps 
 
3.1 Defining the scope: The two important variables Matco took into consideration when deciding 
on the capacity of the cabinet, was the size and weight of the components Matco would manually 
thermal spray weld. They elected to convert an existing grit blast cabinet that was sufficient in size.  
 
3.2 Preparing the cabinet:  A rotating table with a variable frequency drive was installed inside the 
cabinet so that part rotation speed could be made adjustable. The table can include T -slots that 
would allow a variety of part holding solutions to be use d. The cabinet should allow for dust and 
fumes extraction hoses to be mounted. Allows for flexible positioning of the spray gun.  
 
3.3 Turntable details:  The assembly consist of an electric motor, gearbox, mounting surface (Table 
with T-slots) and a variable frequency drive (VFD). The VFD permits the operator to adjust the 
desired rotation speed of the table, which is essential when spraying components of varying 
diameter and shape.

3.4 Work holding: It is necessary to clamp and position a variety of components of varying sizes 
and shapes. In addition, the part must be held rigid during the process.  Matco uses a T -slot 
clamping kit to allow the operator flexibility in mounting the co mponents to the table. It may be 
necessary for the end user to develop specialized fixtures or jigs for the various components they 
will be spraying.  
 
                                   
 
    Image 5.  T-slot Clamping Kit and Fixtures 
 
 
3.3 Selecting Thermal Arc spray equipment: 
Matco elected to use TAFA 8830 for their application, although other equipment could be selected 
based on the needs. The power control unit was installed at the rear of the cabinet.

3.5 Debris and Fumes Extraction:  
Since the arc spray process generates a considerable amount of fumes and debris it was necessary 
to incorporate a means to extract these from the cabinet. MATCO selected a Donaldson system 
for this requirement. It was mounted outside and adjacent to the machine shop wall.  
 
  
 
                            Image 8.  Debris and fumes extraction unit

3.6   Spraying Process: 
Since the process has been placed in service the following photos show that it’s capable of 
salvaging small components via the arc spray welding process. Control parameters must be 
maintained to achieve acceptable results. Standoff distance, table rotation speed, voltage, current, 
temperature, and operator experience can all effect the results. Further technical information on 
spray welding can be referenced in SEBF2125 Thermal spray procedures for exhaust manifolds.

4.0 Benefits 
 
4.1 When properly applied the thermal arc spray process is an excellent process to salvage 
components and in turn reduce owning and operating cost for the customer.  
4.2 Minimal floor space requirements. 
4.3 Versatility for spraying applications. 
4.4 Low-cost solution. No robot or complex systems required. 
 
5.0 Resources Required 
 
5.1 Thermal Spray Equipment (TAFA 8830 or similar) 
5.2 Turntable with variable frequency drive and gearbox 
5.3 Debris and Fumes extraction system (Donaldson or similar) 
5.4 Grit Blast Cabinet (Econoline or similar)
