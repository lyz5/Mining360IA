1.0 Introduction  
 
This Best Practice provides an overview of Westrac’s Australian apprentice program. This 
curriculum was developed by WesTrac to address the specific requirements of Caterpillar 
machines and equipment.  
 
The purpose of this best practice is to encourage dealer engagement with Caterpillar when 
developing an Apprenticeship Course. This document provides an example of Westrac’s 
Apprentice Program. 
 
WesTrac’s apprentice program is a three and a half year course that is structured around 
Australian recognised training qualifications. The aim of this program is for WesTrac to educate 
and develop their employees through a combination of classroom style learning and hands on 
field experience. The implementation of this program has enabled and will continue to enable 
WesTrac to provide the best possible support and after sales service to its Caterpillar customers.  
  
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
Apprentice Induction 
All new apprentices are put through a one-week induction and are required to attend the following 
sessions: 
 
• Management greeting 
• Personal protective equipment issue 
• Tool store operation 
• Warranty and report writing introduction 
• Clocking on/off procedure 
• Signing of agreements 
• Occupational Health and Safety (OH&S) training 
• Human resources policies and procedures 
• Drug and alcohol awareness 
• Counselling availability 
 
Further safety training is conducted during their first year after the induction period. 
 
WesTrac Institute 
 
The primary aim of the WesTrac Institute is to deliver a skilled and competent workforce. 
 
Trained and competent personnel are essential when building a workplace culture that ensures: 
• The highest priority on health and safety 
• Applicable technical skills 
• Robust processes and procedures 
• Improved and standardised training at all employee levels

WesTrac’s Institute currently delivers training and assessment in the following areas: 
 
• Certificate III in Automotive Mechanical Technology (Heavy) 
• Post Trade Technical 
• Machine Operation 
• Occupational Safety and Health 
• Training and Assessment 
 
The WesTrac Institute curriculum has been developed through close consultation with various 
industries and the Western Australian government, ensuring apprentices receive a 
comprehensive trade qualification. This program is industry focussed and sets a new benchmark 
in the heavy mobile equipment mechanical trade.  
 
The Institute provides an industry specific learning program that draws upon current and 
emerging industry technologies. This program also allows the immediate access to Caterpillar 
machines, components and simulators that will reinforce classroom learning. 
 
The program exposes apprentices to technical, technological, business and interpersonal 
competence that is otherwise hard to come by. 
WesTrac Institute trainers have recent and relevant industry experience and hold relevant 
training, assessment and occupational, safety and health qualifications.

WesTrac workplace Assessor  
 
Skills Testing 
• Select and create skills tests for apprentices that match the year of apprenticeship and 
expected skills level 
• C arry out skills test – minimum four hours per apprentice year 
 
Skills Testing Remedial 
• Carry out remedial training (study, hands on) 
• Retest skills that were below acceptable level 
 
Competency Record Book 
• Carry out regular audits on competency record book 
• Liaise with Workshop Supervisor and Mentor to ensure competency tasks related to the 
particular section are available for the apprentice to complete 
 
Competency Testing 
• Monitor competency record book for completed tasks of a particular competency 
• Carry out oral testing of that competency 
• Carry out practical testing of competency or part of if necessary, to satisfy if participant is 
competent in the unit concerned 
 
Off The Job Training 
• Follow off the job training results 
• Remedial coaching on the hold/s using teaching aids available 
 
Tool Inspection 
• Inspect tool kits on a regular basis to ensure that the kits meet the conditions of the WesTrac 
tool support contract 
 
Monthly Assessments 
• Ensure apprentices have the monthly assessments carried out and the assessment sheets are 
received for recording and filing. Poor performance areas in the assessment are to be followed 
up and addressed in the appropriate manner 
 
Appearance 
• Encourage apprentices to maintain a standard of dress and appearance that the company 
expects

On The Job Training 
 
• Cooperate with Supervisors and Mentors to raise the skill level of the apprentice in the section 
or area as quick as possible so as to be able to be in a position to carry out productive work 
• Map out a chart of the skills required by the various sections and ensure that the apprentice is 
competent in these skills.  This is achieved by working under suitable supervision or through 
simulated exercises. 
• With the cooperation of the supervisor select suitable tasks within the section or area to match 
skills and ability of the apprentice. 
• Have the apprentice carry out these tasks as a part of the apprentice duties thus allowing more 
qualified personnel to do more complex tasks. 
 
Awards 
• Encourage apprentice to excel in their given trades and compete in State and National skills 
competitions.  Carry out skills coaching as directed. 
 
Apprentice Initiatives 
 
 
• No 1st Year apprentices in workshops 
o Dedicated practical skills areas (electrical laboratory, fabrication, hand skills focus); 
• Fast track competent apprentices 
• More branch, Field Service and MARC support 
• Improve mentoring 
• During their first year, apprentices attend dedicated skills areas placed on workshop floor 
• Formal training in forklift operation, slinging and lifting is also provided 
• The skill areas focus on: hand skills, fabrication and electrical 
• Throughout their second year, apprentices are introduced to machine repairs and assembly 
• The third year consists of component overhauling followed by field and regional sites 
• Final year apprentices are made aware of the opportunities and support they can expect after 
completion of their apprenticeship. 
• A minimum offering of two technical training programs per year ongoing 
o Career pathways into Service or Marketing occupations after completion of trade 
qualifications 
o Opportunity to attend Leadership and Management Training programs 
o High performing final year apprentices can be nominated to attend the Future Leaders 
Program

WesTrac apprentice assessment 
 
Workplace trainers are competent in the skills they teach using: 
 
• Clearly stated skill requirements 
• Skill-based observation of workplace performance 
• Skill-based records of progress 
• Skill-based criteria for certification 
• Providing trainee access to records 
• Immediate feedback on performance 
Independent monitoring of training and assessment standards will assure valid, accurate and 
meaningful assessments 
 
Learning strategy 
To acquire new skills and knowledge on components or machines, the following sequence 
should be used. 
1. Occupational Safety and Health issues involved 
2. Purpose of the component or machine 
3. Description of the component or machine  
4. Operation of the component or machine 
5. Repair of the component or machine 
6. Diagnosis of the component or machine  
7. Failure Analysis of the component or machine

Outcome: Heavy vehicle mechanical apprenticeship 
On successful completion of this apprenticeship the participant will be able to: 
• Describe and demonstrate safe work practices 
• Maintain and operate hand and power tools and equipment typically involved with the 
earthmoving and industrial mechanic trade 
• Identify earthmoving and industrial components and systems and state their purpose and 
operating principles 
• Perform service, repair and overhaul procedures to earthmoving and industrial 
components/machines in accordance with manufactures or accepted industry specifications 
(performance and time factors) 
• Apply diagnostic procedures to earthmoving and industrial components/machines using 
manufactures or accepted industry procedures (performance and time factors) 
 
3.0 Implementation Steps 
In conjunction with the Caterpillar/Dealer institute an apprentice program should be developed 
and tailored to the specific requirements of the dealer. 
 
4.0 Benefits 
Delivering outstanding value to customers, employees, shareholders and the community. 
 
What the customer wants from today’s service technician 
• Only necessary work gets done 
• Cost is reasonable 
• Work be completed quickly  
• Problem is fixed right the first time 
 
The only way to satisfy this customer 
• Quick, complete and accurate diagnosis 
 
And the way to good diagnosis 
• Be competent in the purpose, description and operation of the component/machine 
• Discover the problem with the customer’s help 
• Write a clear and accurate repair order 
• Test the diagnosis in a logical sequence
