1.0 Introduction  
The maintenance of truck bodies is a vitally important function of the mine maintenance 
department and, if managed poorly, can have a significant negative impact upon owning & 
operating costs and machine availability. 
 
This Best Practice describes a process and method for monitoring and trending wear in OHT 
bodies.  Cracks and other body defects are recoded on a plan view of the body and included in a 
comprehensive report. 
 
The Best Practice incorporates both: 
• The condition monitoring procedure and discipline 
• The OHT tray/body checklist and data collection matrix. 
 
For various reasons, the bodies at this site had originally been installed with additional armour to 
control deterioration in high wear areas.  When this armour was removed the tray condition 
deteriorated rapidly over all trucks, increasing workload and making body maintenance 
unmanageable without a central register.
   
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
An Excel database has been developed to capture truck body wear.  Today, recording of cracks 
and other defects is done on a separate template and incorporated into the body condition 
reports.  An Access database version is currently being developed that will include all functions in 
one and have even greater flexibility. 
 
The database allows the user to select between dual slope and MSD bodies (refer figure 1) and 
will provide the appropriate inspection point map for the selected body type (refer to Figure 2a)

Importantly, the tool was developed together by WesTrac’s on-site Fleet Analyst and Boilermaker 
Leading Hand.  The tool recognizes which of the inspection locations (eg. D2, D3 & D4 in Figure 
2a) are on the same liner plate and colour codes the hot-sheets accordingly (eg. Figure 3).  This 
means that the hot-sheets report on the overall condition of a full line plate section rather than an 
individual point location. 
 
The Tray Inspection dashboard is a very effective tool for tray backlog management.  Bodies with 
locations coloured orange allow the maintenance planner/scheduler to order required materials in 
advance and schedule labor resources so that when the dashboard turns red for that location, the 
repairs can be completed with minimized downtime. 
 
For sites that collect the number of cycles completed per truck via VIMS, this can also allow for 
accurate estimation of body life remaining in terms of time. For example, by knowing the wear 
rate per 100 cycles (note: a high percentage of the abrasive body wear will occur during the 
dumping of material on each cycle), then estimating the number of shifts until wear-out should be 
possible, to a high degree of accuracy, in all inspection locations.

3.0 Implementation Steps 
 
• Develop the Tray Wear Database to suit your site’s needs 
• Incorporate Tray Wear Inspections into your site’s condition monitoring program with 
appropriate sign-off process to ensure that inspections always take place.  The dealer who 
developed these check sheets completes the inspections every 250, 500 & 3000 hours. 
• The on site Fleet Analyst monitors the wear trends for all bodies 
• The planner/schedulers will order required resources as wear trending indicates that 
sections are reaching the end of their wear life. 
• Scheduler updates Backlog Management system 
• Project Manager uses database-reporting capabilities to update on the body maintenance 
program. 
• All role descriptions and process maps should be updated to reflect all the above steps 
 
 
4.0 Benefits 
 
Tony Blake is a Leading Hand boilermaker with WesTrac 
in Western Australia and is a primary user of this tool.   
 
Benefits realized through this Best Practice include: 
• No unscheduled delays due to material shortages 
- material is ordered and delivered in advance    
• Reduced risk of machine downtime due to 
unscheduled body repair 
• Reduced chassis maintenance costs due to 
maximizing remaining body life 
• Improved scheduling accuracy to enable 
manpower efficiency 
• Use of chassis crack data to better understand crack initiation points and propagation rates 
• Improved data collection for maintenance budgeting 
• Improved communication between maintenance and production departments relative to 
chassis management and scheduled chassis repairs
