1.0 Introduction  
 
Developing an effective NMI (New Market Introduction) process is a challenging process for most CAT 
dealers.  It is important that the Project Manager brings a 360 -degree technical-operational vision of 
the Key Factors that are a part of the validation process for the product. 
 
While there are many publications available on the NMI  (New Market Introduction) and NPI (New 
Product Introduction), most tend to be more fragmented, highly specialized for a particular product, 
more maintenance focused or more operations focused.  Typically, they also fail to connect and explore 
the integrated business vision with the customer’s value chain, addressing aspect of Total Cost of 
Ownership (TCO), CPH (Cost Per Hour)  and CPT (Cost Per Ton),  Maintenance Ratio (MR) and 
more.  With these it is not uncommon to have surprises once the trucks go into production cause a lot 
of rework or other financial intervention to achieve customer objectives for the machines. 
 
This case study was an effective roadmap for introducing the Caterpillar electric -drive 794AC’s into 
Brazil, a market dominated by Komatsu  electric drive 930E -4’s; until the 794AC’s  were introduced, 
there were no better options for this customer.  Utilizing this roadmap  was critical to achieving al l the 
customer critical KPI targets for th e 6 x 794AC trucks.  The client was very impressed with the whole 
NMI process (from preparation through implementation through actual operation/production).  The high 
performance of this 794AC fleet based on the successful NMI has given the customer confidence to 
move forward with additional 794AC purchases. 
 
Other dealers facing challenges with NMI should be able to utilize the concept of this  roadmap 
presented in this paper to successfully introduce new machines into their territory.  SOTREQ  is a big 
proponent of dealer collaboration and would be available to discuss this NMI in more detail.

2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
This Best Practice is a roadmap developed by SOTREQ for introducing the CAT 794AC truck into Brazil 
with an approach well integrated into the mining customer’s value chain. This same roadmap could be 
applied to NMI by other Dealers in other regions.  
 
This introduction brings together the following: 
 
• SOTREQ experience/expertise. 
• Use of NMI documentation. 
• Analysis of other operations (different models, types of customers/operations, etc).  
• Interaction with Caterpillar LMT/OHT Product Groups. 
• Specific product support and commercial (operations/customer) support at the site. 
 
From concept standpoint, the 794AC NMI in Brazil was developed and build based on two key drivers: 
technical requirements needed, and commercial goals demanded. For this case, must consider these 
two ones: 
 
Product Support Perspective – Key Technical Requirements 
 
a. Technical Mindset: Utilize technical information already available from Caterpillar as well as other 
dealers… 
b. Strategy & Experience: Build a MSR (Maintenance Strategy Review)  and understand the risks 
with introducing the new model.  Utilize the experience gained from other new model introductions 
at your site. 
c. Challenges - Learning & Assessment : Utilize the lessons learned from other projects 
implemented on sit e:  challenges of qualified labor, evaluation of customer performance (KPI’s), 
indirect cost (CPH) that occur, etc. 
d. Strategy & Process:  Understand the business model for the operation / support of the new 
equipment on site - MARC, CSA, CVA, etc.  Clearly establish the strategies and processes for parts, 
labor, tooling, shop productivity, etc. 
e. Customer Expectations: Fully understand all the customer’s KPI  targets/required performance 
(Physical Availability, MTBF, MTTR, MR (Maintenance Ratio, Cost Per Hour, Cost Per Ton, etc. 
f. Put in place all the technical infrastructure in order to achieve the customer performance targets –  
safely and productively. 
 
Operational & Customer Support – Commercial Goals demanded  
 
a. Benchmark the competition .  Understand what they are doing better or different than the Cat 
model on site and see how some might be applied to the Cat NMI. 
b. Consider innovation: Technology, techni cal support, continuous improvement projects, safe 
maintenance, manpower productivity, etc.  
c. Understand your customer: The Customer will always compare you with the competition, not only 
in relation to the quantitative indicators to be delivered but also t o the qualitative aspects.  
Remember:  The NMI Trucks are the “newcomer” to the site.

3.3. PRODUCT EXPERIENCE 
 
Once you have done your risk management assessment and understand the need/acceptance 
criteria, it is important to construct your NMI process focused on the product itself.   
 
3.3.1. PARTS  
A Parts Strategy for NMI needs to consider the service challenges, logistics, costs and 
required Service Level (i.e. >90%). 
 
• Building an Appropriate Spare Parts List:  
 
o Utilize the Caterpillar 794AC RDSL (Recommended Dealer Stocking List)  as the 
starting point for the site parts list.  Note: SOTREQ did a detailed review with the 794AC 
Team where some adjustments were made to actual need and quantities. 
o Utilize your backlog list and the parts consumption list (0 – 2000 hours) from other 794AC 
dealers around the world.   
o Utilize your experience from other models on site (some which may have been captured 
through site MSR’s) 
o Understand parts related to current CPI, PIQ and other known failures from other dealers 
and adjust your list accordingly. 
 
  NOTE:  SOTREQ’S 794AC NMI Spare Parts list had 1,345 items. 
 
• Ensuring a Stocking Level of > 90% 
 
o Identify key service areas and group parts by those groups instead of a general “line 
item” list.  Manage and take-action on parts by the groups.  One such grouping is as 
follows:  A/C, Cab., Hydraulic, Electrical, PM, Minor Components etc.

3.3.2. MAJOR COMPONENTS 
 
You will want to support the customer on establishing a plan for planned component removals 
as part of the NMI.   Each customer has their strategies (hours, condition, condition + hours, 
budget availability, etc.).   
 
SOTREQ developed a specific strategy for the 794AC  NMI which has the trucks on a two-year 
trial.  A key feature of this strategy was that the impact of components needed to have <1%  
impact of Physical Availability.  To reach this target and reduce capital, SOTREQ developed and 
applied the approach below.  SOTREQ believe each dealer could “capitalize” on this approach 
to support their specific needs.  SOTREQ  would be happy to walk interested dealers through 
this approach. 
 
1. Develop a Reliability Curve (Weibull Analysis) – starting with data available. 
2. Evaluate Failure Rate of each component (CPI’s/Warranty) vs. Impact on Physical 
Availability. 
3. Account for the logistic availability challenges for critical parts/components. 
4. Develop a Risk Buying Curve – gradual buying as the machines’ SMU’s increase. 
5. Evaluation competition acquisition costs if the data is available and compare with Cat 
costs, determine best cost/hour/useful life ratio. 
 
NOTE: For this NMI, 7 Major Components and 4 Hydraulic Components with high physical 
availability were purchased.  See Figures 8 and 9 below for details.

3.3.3. TOOLING 
 
The added NMI Tooling requirements should be defined through a combination of the Tooling 
List provided by Caterpillar and the results of the MSR done and include the following: 
 
1. Basic Truck Tooling not currently available on site. 
2. PM Tooling, Diagnostic Tooling, Assembly Tooling currently not available on site. 
3. Torque Tooling currently not available on site. 
4. Tooling recommended by Caterpillar and other Dealer (based on their experience). 
 
It is important to connect the tooling to the strategy of increasing productivity and safety of the 
workforce.   
 
NOTE: In this NMI case, SOTREQ  made numerous acquisitions that reduced assembly time  
and maintenance repair time while still maintaining a safety focus (Elimination of Live Work); 
the list is attached. 
 
 
3.3.4. MAINTENANCE STRATEGY 
 
The Mainten ance Strategy must be built upon the MSR, Risk Management and those key 
aspects from the customer surveys.  Discussions with other dealers  on their experience 
(including downtime drivers) and strategies also are useful.  Use of Best Practices, FPC 
Simulation and other tools will also support a great strategy. 
 
In this NMI case, in addition to the above, there were additional KPI’s as part of the commercial 
agreement that were also a foundation to the strategy.

Understanding the Mine Operation Model 
 
It is important to understand the Mine Operation Model (MARC, CVA, CSA, other) for the site in 
building the Equipment Management Process.  The customer is observing the “effort” that is spent

will show that the NMI product is not a “heavy maintenance”!  Bear in mind that most mining relates 
to 4.0 Industry and are looking for mechanisms to reduce rework and manpower on site. 
 
In this NMI, the operation model was a CVA  with parts and labor under dealer responsibility  and 
commitments in both performance and costs. SOTREQ took this opportunity to implement several 
mechanisms to support in this new mining environment. 
 
NOTE: In the case of the typical mining dealer, they may look for market alternatives or simplified 
maintenance processes.

3.3.6. TRAINING PEOPLE & BUILDING KNOWLEDGE 
 
Training and Building Knowledge are critical to the overall success of the NMI and future performance 
of the product.  Many operations make the mistake of just looking for technical training associated with 
the product maintenance and the operation of the product.   
 
The training and a knowledge base are necessities for e veryone involved in the NMI  process - from 
operators and mechanics in the field to site management to company management at corporate 
headquarters. 
 
In this NMI, the engineers that developed the NMI process developed the training material; this detailed

3.3.7. PRODUCT PROBLEMS MANAGEMENT 
 
Depending on where your NMI  machines were in the production line, not all updates and 
product improvements that are available to those machines may be on your machines.  
Consider implementing the updates and improvements during the assembly to minimize the 
impact of them in production. 
 
Some key actions: 
 
• Work with your Caterpillar NMI contacts to be fully aware of the updates and product 
improvements.   
• Determine what should be fixed or applied during assembly.   
• Plan appropriately for the required work (manpower, parts, scheduling, etc.). 
 
Two important “windows of opportunity” for understanding and implementing fixes or quality 
corrections/updates are as follows: 
 
• Pre-Delivery Audit (P DA):  Request that PDA’s  (“quality inspections”) are done on your 
trucks and review of those PDA’s.  Confirm that all appropriate updates are made.  If they 
cannot be corrected before delivery, make sure that you have the resources and planning 
in place to correct before commissioning. 
 
• Partners In Quality (PIQ):  Work with your Cat representatives to prepare for and perform 
all quality corrections and product improvements during machine assembly.  Performing 
these corrections at machine assembly will minimize what needs to be done at PM or other

            3.3.8. COMUNICATION PLAN 
 
Building your NMI  Plan in the project manager methodology of a  WBS – Work Breakdown 
Structure, will give more organization and clarity on all the important aspects for introducing a new 
model into the region.  Establishing a robust Communication Plan is cr itical for creating alignment 
and engagement throughout the process. 
 
 
Figure 22 – Example of WBS build for NMI Process 
 
 
Regular established meetings with proper participants, update status, actions, responsibilities 
are key to successful communication.   
 
For this NMI, the following communication routine was established: 
• Week Meetings (Follow Up Meeting) – Update the status of the NMI Plan with key process 
owners:  Dealer Process Owners, PMO Team, CAT Region and NMI Teams. 
 
• Bimonthly Meetings (Executive Meeting) – Asses the status of the implementation with 
stakeholders:  CAT & Dealer Sponsors, Dealer Process Owners, PMO Team, CAT & Dealer 
NMI Team and Dealer Implementation Team.
