1.0 Introduction  
 
Efficient planning and scheduling of CRC manpower and resources to the workload, and 
adjusting for constant changes is a major challenge for most dealerships. Caterpillar has no 
preferred software available to dealers to perform this function. Often, planning and scheduling of 
work orders is performed on a display board or on an Excel worksheet. Supervisors spend much 
of their day managing work assignments and workload in their areas. 
 
When a CRC has more than 50 technicians, and especially when there is more than one shift, a 
formal scheduling process and supporting software becomes a necessity. 
 
Several dealers have found and implemented software designed to facilitate planning and 
scheduling. These software programs come from systems used in manufacturing plants and from 
the airline industries to manage many resources and tasks in a constantly changing environment. 
 
This Best Practice discusses a scheduling process implemented at Finning Chile.  
  
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
The dealer objective in implementing a new planning and scheduling process was to improve 
customer satisfaction with component rebuilds completed in a time, predictable manner. At the 
same time, to increase labor efficiency and CRC resource utilization by improving short and 
medium term planning and scheduling processes in the CRC, Machine Shop Salvage, and Parts 
deliveries. 
 
The pain: 
• Lack of coordination between the CRC and machine s hop support. 
• Lack of the ability to track and monitor work orde r status and completion target. 
• Poor use of available resources – bottlenecks and delays. 
• Unknown actual current and future work load 
• Ineffective communication between planners, foreme n, technicians 
• Work order completion forecast seldom achieved. 
• Lack of scheduling and coordination of subcomponen t and the major component rebuild. 
• Lack of coordination between the arrival of a comp onent and the start of the rebuild. 
 
     
    The major steps in implementing a new scheduling process and system include: 
• Create a team to work on the project.  
• Study software packages available and select suppl ier. 
• Create detailed process maps for most common rebui ld activities. Each separate activity 
that requires a resource must be identified. Standard times for each segment are 
necessary. 
• Define each resource.  This includes technicians a nd workbays, but can also include major 
tooling, such as test bench time, dyno time, overhead crane, etc.

activity (segments) in a process map. The activity must define the task and the time required. The 
activity needs to include parts ordering and delivery, and work performed in the machine shop. 
 
Many process maps will be similar, such as for 3500 series engines, so that a standardize 
template can be used. Some models may have to be customized.

Sample Technician Schedule 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Near the end of each shift, the technician turns in his schedule for the day. Each task is 
marked either as “Done”, “Not Initiated”, or “Need X amount more time”. 
 
The information is entered into the schedule program, and a new schedule created based on 
progress and any changes. 
 
Unplanned changes, such as an unplanned technician absence, can be entered into the 
schedule as it occurs and work for that day will be automatically redistributed. 
 
4.0 Benefits 
 
• All available manpower is fully scheduled and util ized. 
• Schedule can show shop workloads months into the f uture and help plan incoming workloads. 
• Management reports show area manpower utilization and bottlenecks. Enables corrective 
measures to be taken. 
• Component turnaround time in the CRC is greatly re duced. Capacity is improved. 
• Supervisors do less crisis management. 
• Customers can have an exact status check on their work and expected completion time in
