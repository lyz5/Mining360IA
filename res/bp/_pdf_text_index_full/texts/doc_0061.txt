1.0 Introduction  
 
This Best Practice assists in the visually tracking of activities (repairs) that are performed 
when working with equipment due to: 
 
a. Equipment planned repairs  
b. Equipment unplanned repairs  
c. Inspections 
 
Visual controls allow: 
 
a. The Supervisor to have a real time monitoring of the work’s progress  
b. Work quality verifications in an organized manner 
c. Visual machine transfer of the in processes and pending work  
 
Generally, the staff is focused on their tasks and they do not update logs or Gantt charts until 
their shift is ending in order to deliver it to the supervisor for the shift change.  
 
Machine transfers are usually performed orally and come with an achieved progress log and 
pending repairs (usually performed among Supervisors).  This log is then reviewed after the shift 
ends, losing the machine’s progress real control.     
 
Following one of the CPS’s principles of “Make it Visual”, the card system allows everyone to 
quickly see the jobs that have been performed on a machine and those that have not. 
 
It is important for the dealer processes because it: 
 
a. Give the supervisor a real time monitoring of the repairs progress. 
b. Allows work quality verification in an organized manner: when finishing each repair, the 
technician will handle the card to the Supervisor and this is a warning to verify the 
performed work quality.    
c. Allows a visual machine transfer of the in progress and pending work. The progress is 
visual for supervisors, but also for the machine’s technicians.    
d. Allows technical staff to continue performing other activities without waiting for the 
supervisors’ indication.

This Best Practice is intended to achieve:  
 
• Improved work transfer system (quality)  
• Formalized job completion quality control process (quality) 
• R eal time progress on machine visual inspection (management control)

3.0 Implementation Steps 
 
This improvement emerged from the component cannibalization process.  When removing a part 
from a component, the logistic area used to hang cards to indicate that some parts had been 
removed. 
 
In order to implement this Best Practice, the following steps must be considered: 
 
1. Card manufacturing:  Create design and determine quantity according to the historical 
data for work orders 
2. Buy plastic bags, pull-tight seals and magnets in order to fix the cards on the machine 
3. Explain procedure and how to fill out the cards to Planning and Maintenance staff 
4. I mplementation the process on the machines 
 
This method is based on the CPS principle - “Make it Visual.” It was implemented on March 10, 
2011. 
 
During the implementation stage, Supervisors and Maintenance technicians took part, apart from 
Planning Staff in the initial deployment. 
 
In order to distribute this process to a wider audience it was documented as a Best Practice. 
 
The greatest challenge is the cultural change of the involved staff.

4.0 Benefits 
 
The greatest benefits are improving the transfer process quality and the performed tasks quality 
by formalizing the jobs’ inspection supervisor function for each received card.    
 
 
5.0 Resources Required 
 
Investment:
 
  Manufacture cards and buy plastic bags, pull-tight seals and magnets.  Quantity 
depends on the orders history per working day. 
Time:
 
   Time to train and follow-up with the involved staff.
