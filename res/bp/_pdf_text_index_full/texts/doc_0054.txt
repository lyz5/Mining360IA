1.0 Introduction 
The Cat 6015 Hydraulic Front Shovel final drive brakes are an important part with a complex removal 
process.  
The Caterpillar procedure (BI644878) recommends using two bolts with their M8 nuts by pressing 
the washer (thrust collar) while tightening the nuts uniformly to extract the retaining ring (Circlip).  
The implementation of the bolts does not guarantee that the surface goes down uniformly every time 
which generates an abrasive wear between the piston and the housing (stripes). This could alter the 
reliability of the repair of the component and the reusability of parts if not done precisely. It could 
also increase the time under repair when the tightening is not adequate, or the applied loads are not 
uniform. This best practice can also reduce risk of the part ejecting out in an uncontrolled manner. 
Parts reusability for damage to the piston at the submitting dealership is around 70%.

This Best Practice aims to be able to disassemble the brake safely and reliably from a tool, 
preventing the washer ( thrust collar) from injuring the technicians, and it is also possible to 
eliminate the redo and cost overrun in case something comes up.

2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
This tool applies to the disassembly and assembly process on the final drive brakes on a 6015. 
It is geometrically “t-shaped". The vertical piece is a worm drive of 1” UNC that at one end has 
a hollow cylinder to rest on the pressure washer (thrust collar). The horizontal  piece is a flat 
base of A36 steel that has a nut fixed in the center where the bolt travels; In addition, it has two 
holes of 22mm diameter at its ends designed to improve the grip between the tool and the final 
drive, ensuring the safety of the technicians and the component. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
       
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
3.0 Implementation Steps 
 
1. Final Drive measurements are taken. 
2. Design of the tool according to the measurements and ergonomics of the operation. 
3. Load analysis of the tool. 
4. Tool manufacturing. 
5. Test conducted at the shop. 
6. Start-up, after disclosing it to the operation with its change format.

4.0 Benefits 
 
 Minimized risk of parts ejecting while disassembling. 
 Reduction in 70% as of the disassembly and installation time of the brakes in the final drives. 
 Elimination of redoes due to failures in the thread of the M8 bolts. 
 
 
5.0 Resources Required 
 
The investment regarding the manufacturing process of the tool was $200 USD.
