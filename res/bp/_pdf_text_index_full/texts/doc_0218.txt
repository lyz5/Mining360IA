1.0 Introduction  
 
During the operation of hydraulic cylinders, burr scratches may appear in heads of hydraulic 
cylinders. Previously, those heads were replaced with the new ones. Now Borusan Makina 
Kazakhstan salvages hydraulic cylinder heads by installing bronze bushings. It enables to 
significantly reduce the repair cost of hydraulic cylinders. 
 
This method of hydraulic cylinder head salvage is applicable for any big hydraulic cylinders on 
condition that the place where the bronze bushing is installed should not have any grooves for 
seals, because the bronze bushing serves as the guide there. 
 
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
In order to salvage hydraulic cylinder heads, bronze bushings are purchased from local suppliers. 
Then those bushings are installed to the heads and bored to the necessary sizes according to 
drawings. Thus, hydraulic cylinder heads are salvaged but not replaced that allows saving money 
on overhauls of hydraulic cylinders because the salvaged cylinder head provides a 31.5% cost 
saving compared to the new one. 
   
 
3.0 Implementation Steps 
 
As an example, 777F Truck Body Lift/ Hoist Cylinder Head salvage by installing the bronze 
bushing is shown below.  
1) The damaged area in the hydraulic cylinder head is bored. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
2) The bronze bushing is installed into the cylinder head. In the edge of the joint it needs to 
drill 3 x holes with the depth of 12-15 mm and at the distance of 120
0 from each other.

4.0 Benefits 
 
• Hydraulic cylinder overhaul cost has been reduced (the salvaged cylinder head gives a 
31.5% cost saving compared to the new one). 
 
 
5.0 Resources Required 
 
• Machine Shop’s equipment (milling machine, etc.); 
• Bronze bushings purchased from local suppliers; 
• Toggle Bolts; 
• Loctite 640. 
 
 
6.0 Supporting Attachments / References 
 
• Bushing information – The diameter is 224.15 -0.05mm. The tension is about 0.1mm. 
Before pressing, the bushing is cooled down to -50 degrees. 
• Highest hours of cylinders operating in the field with this rework. – It works up to the next 
overhaul (12,000 hrs.) 
• No failures to date.  We started to use this procedure a long time ago (more than 1 year). 
Now this procedure has become the standard job. 
• At the time of the next rebuild, do you need to replace the bushing due to wear? – If the 
burrs are revealed in the bushing during inspection, it is replaced. If not, it’s not replaced.
