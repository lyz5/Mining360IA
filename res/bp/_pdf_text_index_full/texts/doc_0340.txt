1.0 Introduction  
 
Many dealers use an employee suggestion program as one source of ideas for improvements.  
Often, employee suggestion programs function more like a one-time event rather than a 
continuous, sustained process with a steady flow of suggestions and implemented improvements. 
Keeping the program active and maintaining employee participation and engagement is a key to 
successful results. 
 
This best practice describes an Employee Suggestion Program implemented by Thompson 
Tractor Co. 
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
An effective employee suggestion program must be well defined and have a formal process and 
structure including: responsibility, action, feedback, as well as reward and recognition. 
 
Key fundamentals include: 
 
• Ownership:  One person with overall responsibility for the program. 
• E mployee training and communication on the program. 
• A reward structure for an accepted idea.  (Thompson pays $200 to the idea submitter). 
• A standard submission form. 
• A review / screening process 
• Feedback to submitters: Accepted (to be implemented) or not accepted and why. 
 
Simply creating a program is not in itself enough, employees must feel that they have a voice in 
change and that voice is valued and makes a difference.
