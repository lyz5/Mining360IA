1.0 Introduction  
 
In order to achieve mining customer cost per ton objectives, customers must manage the 
component life of their equipment fleet.  The key success factors in managing component life are 
to minimize unplanned failures and to optimize planned component rebuild intervals.  
 
Planned component rebuild intervals can be based on a number or factors including past 
experience, condition monitoring, fuel consumption, oil analysis, or even arbitrary target hours. 
 
Minesite conditions can change over time and impact component life. Negative impacts can come 
from deeper pits, longer hauls, overloading, as well as maintenance and monitoring.  Positive 
changes can come from continuous improvement initiatives such as contamination control, fluid 
cleanliness, application and payload management, and better maintenance and monitoring. 
 
When a component is repaired or rebuilt in the dealer CRC, dealers are able to assess the 
condition of the component.  Dealer-documentation of the component’s condition can be valuable 
information for the customer.  Some dealers have provided technical reports back to the customer 
after every component rebuild or repair. These reports may also offer recommendations and 
information the customer can use to better manage component life. 
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
This Best Practice contains several standard reports from MCTS used by some dealers to 
provide information and recommendations to mining customers. 
 
Failure Analysis Report 
   
 This report is generated any time a component has suffered a failure or unplanned rebuild or  
     repair.  The report includes: 
• Component identification  
• Hours, rebuild history (and total fuel consumption for engines) 
• Any applicable supporting data:  VIMS / SOS / Particle Count 
• Root Cause of Failure identification 
• Recommendations to avoid future failures  
 
       Component Rebuild Report  
 
This report is generated for every planned before-failure component rebuild.  The purpose of 
the report is to assess the condition of the component and potential remaining life. The report 
includes: 
• Component identification 
• Hours, rebuild history  (total fuel consumption for engines) 
• Any applicable supporting data:  VIMS / SOS / Particle Count 
• Technical assessment of internal wear. 
• Recommendations to improve life or adjust PCR interval.

       Fleet Component Life Summary 
 This is a management report usually generated once a year and presented to the customer.  It  
           provides an overview of average component life by model and by component.  It also reports  
           the percentage of rebuilds performed before-failure and after failure. 
 
• Average component life by component and model. 
• Average component life for planned before-failure rebuilds 
• Average component life for after-failure rebuilds. 
• Percent of rebuilds made before failure. 
• Percent of rebuilds made after failure. 
 
A sample report is attached.  This example was generated using the Caterpillar Major 
Component Tracking System (MCTS). 
          
 
 
 The component life information is also sometimes reported as a histogram bell curve graph. 
 
3.0 Implementation Steps 
 
1. Determine level of customer and dealer interest in CRC reports and specific needs. 
2. Create standard report forms, per customer and dealer feedback. 
3. Create a process to collect the information and create the report.  Assign ownership. 
4. Implement a component life tracking system.  (See the Caterpillar Major Component 
Tracking System Best Practice under Site Management – Component Life) 
5. Develop customer communication process for these reports with feedback. 
6. Document Benefits.

4.0 Benefits 
   
• Improves customer cost per ton and preference for Caterpillar equipment.

• Contributes to both dealer and customer Continuous Improvement initiatives.
