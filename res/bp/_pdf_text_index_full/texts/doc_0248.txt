1.0 Introduction  
 
A good readiness process of the engine on the dynamometer at the time of the performance tests is 
key to ensuring the correct assessment of all operational parameters. 
The large number of electrical, hydraulic and pneumatic connections and the great lengths of cables 
and hoses increase the risk of accidents for the technicians and for the components as well. They 
also originate connection errors and increase the engine readiness time. The aforementioned gives a 
disorganized appearance during the engine setting before running the test. See Fig. 1. 
 
 
  
Fig. 1. Engine electrical connection before the upgrade. 
 
This Best Practice aims to improve the aforementioned problems thru the design of a plug and play 
system for the various connections required at the engine readiness stage when testing on the 
dynamometer.

2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
This improvement consists of quick, safe and orderly “plug and play” connections, for the 
installation of the hydraulic, pneumatic and electrical lines that the engine requires  when it is 
tested during its readiness stage on the dynamometer. This routing of lines from the SISWEB 
workstation to the dynamometer is done through internal  channel drains of the room; 
centralizing all the terminals in a single panel on one side and very near the equipment and the 
engine so as to reduce the lengths and organize the final connecting bridges to the engine. 
See Fig. 2. 
 
The panel basically consists of the relocation of the following connections listed in the following 
chart: 
PLUG AND PLAY CONECTORS 
1 
P1 ENGINE OIL PRESSURE INLET 
P2 ENGINE FUEL PRESSURE INLET 
P3 ENGINE BOOST PRESSURE INLET 
P4 TEST POINT PRESSURE INLET 
2 T ENGINE TEMPERATURES INLET 
3 ECM ELECTRONIC CONTROL MODULE CONNECTOR 
4 HYDAC IN HIGH PRESSURE FROM ENGINE INLET (PARTICLE COUNTDOWN) 
HYDAC OUT LOW PRESSURE TO ENGINE OUTLET(PARTICLE COUNTDOWN)

3.0 Implementation Steps 
From the workstation ports (A) the hoses to the pressure taps of the lubrication system, fuel and air 
are connected; and the c ables extension for the thermocouples , are taken to the channel drains , 
enter the point (B) and exit through the point (C), then by a metal conduit s are routed up to the plate 
(D), which is anchored by bolts to a channel welded to the dynamometer base. See Fig. 3.

4.0 Benefits 
 
The benefits that come with this best practice are safety  oriented, as it seeks to eliminate the risk of 
falling by tripping due to the large number of electrical, hydraulic and pneumatic connections , which 
are organized and protected. Thanks to the implementation of plug and play the risk of making wrong 
connections are greatly diminished, leading to a faster readiness process when running the test. 3.
