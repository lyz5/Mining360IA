1.0 Introduction 
 
Significant improvements in machine availability can be gained through an integrated partnership 
between dealer and customer mechanics for the preventative maintenance process.  Availability 
is improved by increasing the PM process efficiency and better completion of backlog repairs.   
 
Low availability and frequent unscheduled repairs drove a restructuring of the PM process.  A full 
time dealer mechanic dedicated to PM’s was put in place to: 
1. Assist with minor mechanic repairs,  
2. Ensure complete inspections,  
3. Create/manage backlogs to be performed in  the repair shop immediately after PM 
completion.   
 
The number of site mechanics was also increased to enable the completion of as many minor 
repairs as possible while minimizing the time spent in PM.   
 
Dealer mechanic assistance, sufficient manpower, disciplined execution of procedures, and 
accurate backlog management resulted in increased machine availability and decreased 
unscheduled shop repair time. 
 
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
The duration of preventative maintenance for each machine is four hours. 
• 1 hour in wash bay 
• 1 hour for lubrication 
• 2 hours of mechanical repair 
 
Lubrication activities include 
• Oil replacement,  
• Kidney loop filtration for remaining oil, 
• Collecting of SOS samples,  
• Cutting and examination of every filter.  
 
The mechanical repair bay work is driven by a serial number specific clipboard documenting the 
activities to be completed. 
 
Clipboard includes: 
• Backlog repair work orders 
• Mag plug inspection sheet 
• Operator “fix list” 
• Checklist of inspections and functionality checks 
 
The dealer mechanic handles the VIMS download and acts upon the data to perform additional 
diagnostics or repairs.

Overall, the dealer mechanic resides in the mechanical repair bay to complete as many backlogs 
as possible and ensure that the remaining are documented accurately and addressed thereafter in 
the main repair shop. The main repair shop will keep the machine as long as it takes to complete 
all backlogs unless lacking parts or resources force the work to be postponed until next PM. 
 
On a daily basis, one PM mechanic works the fuel island to check lube levels, perform general 
inspections, check for leaks, and document operator feedback. 
 
 
3.0 Implementation Steps 
 
• Identify and train PM customer and dealer mechanics. 
• Provide adequate separate areas for wash, lube, and mechanical repair. 
• Provide inspection checklists for filters, mag plugs, structures, and functional checks. 
• Provide kidney lube filtration equipment and efficient means to cut filters and document 
findings for further investigation. 
• Create management system for backlog, work order, and inspection documentation to be 
available to the mechanics by serial number. 
• Provide all required PM tools. 
• Provide means for the dealer mechanic to provide accurate and timely communication of 
backlog repairs to the main shop.

• Lube bay:  3 – 4 
• Mechanical bay:  2 – 3
