1.0 Introduction  
The sprocket/chain drive in a Motor Grader is a large source of fine metal debris in the 
tandem drive box. A natural property of sprocket/chain drive is that there is rubbing at points 
of contact, creating small filings. These filings contaminate the lubrication oil and move to 
other components, which accelerates the normal wear. 
 
This Best Practice outlines the installation of magnetic plugs in the tandem box to aid in the 
removal of metal particles from the lubrication fluid. The plug should not only keep the oil 
cleaner, but also reduce component degradation and extra wear. 
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
Currently, only the standard magnetic plug is installed on certain M-series machines. However, to 
inspect and clean it requires oil in the tandem case to be drained, as it is located in the bottom of 
the case. This makes regular inspections and cleaning of the magnetic plug only as frequent as 
the oil-change, preventing it from being effective after time. 
 
Two new magnetic plugs can be installed per tandem box, on the inner side of the cover-plates 
where the plug would not interfere in the operation of the parts in motion. This location would 
enable the plugs to be exposed to a considerable amount of oil for cleansing, as it circulates 
during operation.

3.0 Implementation Steps 
1. According to Appendix 1, fabricate the magnetic-plug mounting plate. 
Notes: 
a. The mounting plate can be made from 16mm Grade 250 mild steel plate 
b. The magnetic plug (6V-3298) shown in Figures 1-3 are from a D11T Track-Type 
Tractor. As the plug is the same across most models, and also used in different 
machines, it is readily available making this Best Practice easier to undertake. See 
Appendix 2 for an abridged list of machines that use the 6V-3298 magnetic plug. 
2. M ark the cover plate with the welding location, and weld the mount to it. 
Notes: 
a. On the outer side of the cover plate, it is an option to stamp “MAGNET” into the metal 
so that technicians are aware of the new service point. This should be done before the 
welding process to make it easier to stamp the letters. 
b. A thread should be cut into the hole that matches the magnetic plug. The thread size 
on the plug is 1⅝”-12. 
c. The mounts should be welded to the back or “inner side” of the cover-plate and be 
centralised. The weld seams must run parallel to the edges of the cover plate and 
oriented so that the stem of the magnetic plug faces downward. Figures 1 and 3 shows 
the orientation relative to the cover plate, and Figure 2 shows orientation relative to the 
tandem box. Figure 3 shows 
the cover plate with the 
breather, where the mounting 
plate is offset. 
d. Figure 1 shows a cover-plate 
(8D-1802) from a 16H Motor 
Grader, which is also shared by 
the 14H Motor Grader. This 
Best Practice works for the 16M 
and 14M Motor Grader models, 
which have a slightly different 
cover-plate (266-9728). The 
same idea can be applied to 
other Motor Grader models; 
however, mounting plate 
dimensions may differ. 
3. After assuring weld quality, grind the 
welding seams to give a nice, smooth 
finish. 
a. Spray-paint the mounting plate 
for a proper finish. 
4. Install the cover plate to the machine. 
 
4.0 Benefits 
1. Effective contamination control 
2. Cleaner lubrication oil for longer 
3. Reduce piston bore scuffing, as seen in brake housing 
4. Increase seal and component life
