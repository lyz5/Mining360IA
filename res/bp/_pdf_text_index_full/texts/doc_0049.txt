1.0 Introduction  
 
Availability is the most common metric currently use in the mining industry to measure equipment 
fleet performance. The main reason is that the production plan is based on a specific amount of 
equipment hours. The mine production department determines the required hours for the 
production and ancillary equipment, defining the utilization and availability targets.  
 
The difference between the calendar hours and the availability target is the unavailable time.  The 
maintenance department has to use this time efficiently to perform all maintenance and repair 
events needed to maintain the equipment running. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
This diagram illustrates the various types of maintenance events that will be considered in the 
maintenance plan. These events take time away from the asset being utilized. Our objective as 
the maintenance organization is to manage the unavailable time effectively to meet and support 
the production plan requirements.

2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
The Maintenance Organization as part of the production chain of the mine takes the availability 
target as one of their primary performance indicator. The availability not only defines the needed 
hours to support the production plan, but also the amount of hours that can be dedicated to 
Maintenance & Repair (M&R) activities.  The goal is to effectively distribute these available hours 
for M&R activities to execute the monthly scheduled and unscheduled events. 
 
As another way to understand the concept, the maintenance personnel manage the time of the 
equipment when they are “down” and the production department does  the same with the “up” 
time. 
 
There are some maintenance operations that daily track time used for M&R activities and this 
compared with the available time for maintenance. In that way they are able to make adjustments 
to prevent availability shortfalls at the end of the period.  It is the first step to start getting control 
of the M&R time. This basic approach can be improved and made more effective by separating 
the available time into the various event categories, and start tracking the time for each category 
of events. We call this unavailable time categorization the, “Unavailability Distribution”. 
 
 
 
  
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
The diagram above illustrates the sequence of transforming each of the M&R events into downtime, unavailability and 
finally represented in the unavailability distribution.

Building the Unavailability Distribution 
We will use a calculation example to explain the steps to follow when building the unavailability 
distribution.  
 
1. - The first step is to acquire the equipment fleet characteristics, the availability, and utilization 
goals.  
 
Case-scenario for our calculations: 
 
 
 Equipment Type   793 OHT 
 Number of Machines  30 
 Physical Availability Goal 87% 
 Utilization of Asset  81% 
 Month    January 
 
 
2. – With the information provided we analyze and determine the hours distribution in the period.  
 
Calendar hours = Number of machines x days in the period x 24  (h) 
 
Available hours (Physical) = Calendar hours x Physical availability   (h) 
 
Operating hours = Calendar hours x Utilization of Asset    (h). 
 
It is convenient to remember at this point the formula for physical availability: 
 
 
Physical Availability = 
Total Calendar Hours – Maintenance & Repair Hours 
               Total Calendar Hours 
   x 100% 
 
Note: care must be taken when dealing with utilization of asset or utilization of availability. In some 
countries when you refer to utilization it is always utilization of availability.  
 
Calculations for our case scenario 
 
Calendar hours = 30 x 31x 24 = 22,320 (h) 
 
Available hours = 22,320 x 87% = 19,418 (h) 
 
Operating hours = 22,320 x 81% = 18,079 (h) 
 
Now the difference between the calendar hours and the available hours is the amount of hours 
available to do all maintenance and repair activities. 
 
Hours for M&R = 22,320 – 19,418 = 2,902 (h)

Preventive Maintenance (PM) Services 
The PM services in this scenario will follow the 8 Step strategy with an interval of 250 hours and a 
target duration for each step of 10 hours. 
 
First, determine the number of PM services to execute in the month: 
 
Number of PMs to execute = Operating hours (Fleet) / PM Interval  
 
PMs to execute = 18,079 h / 250 h = 72.3 
 
 The total duration that we will have to dedicate to PM this month will be: 
 
PM total duration   = Number of PMs x PM duration 
      =  72.3 x 10 h = 723 (h) 
 
Now the unavailability due to the PM services will be: 
 
PM unavailability = 1-(Calendar hours – PM total duration time)/Calendar hours = ( % )  
 
Or a simpler formula: 
 
PM unavailability = PM time total duration time / Calendar hours = ( % ) 
 
PM unavailability = 723 (h) / 22,320 (h) = 3.24  ( % ) 
 
These are our first results to enter into our distribution table:

3.0 Implementation Steps 
 
The implementation of this concept is relatively simple. This concept and procedure has to be the 
responsibility of the on-site planner; it is a strategic task. 
 
The steps to follow in the implementation are as follows: 
 
 Evaluate your planning process. Determine with the “As Is” situation if your current process. 
Consider the use of the proposed procedure in this BP. 
 Design a spreadsheet  with the various categories to be included. Create your Unavailability 
Distribution template. You can use the format suggested in this BP. 
 Determine the equipment fleet operating parameters (utilization, availability target, …).  
 Investigate and gather all needed information. 
 Populate your Unavailability Distribution. 
 Present the concept and tool to your organization. This step is very important to get the “buy 
in” of your organization. The success of this procedure depends on the collective work of the 
on-site organization. 
 Incorporate the Unavailability Distribution in all your planning meetings.  
 Link the Long, Medium and Short-Term plans to the Unavailability Distribution process. 
 Follow up. Closely monitor the used of the remaining hours for performing the maintenance 
and repair activities. 
 Revise and update as necessary. 
 
4.0 Benefits 
 
The use of the Unavailability Distribution concept described in this BP will give your maintenance 
organization a better understanding of their particular roles and responsibilities in meeting the 
expected fleet performance goals.  It will also provide an effective way to work with specific goals 
linked to each functional area of your operation. 
 
For Planning, who is responsible of building and leading this effort, the benefits are: 
 
 Clear goals and targets for following up and controlling the execution of the scheduled tasks. 
 Better management of the adjustments that may be necessary. 
 Gain control. 
 Moving from expecting “good” things to occur to a controlled and decision making situation

5.0 Resources Required 
 
The implementation of this BP will not require any extra manpower or infrastructure; however, 
depending on your particular situation it will be necessary to redefine the function, role, and 
responsibility of the planner. Special attention must be dedicated to the planner who gets 
primarily focused in building and structuring the Short -Term Plan. Building the Short -Plan is a 
scheduling function. In this situation we recommend separating the functions of planning and 
scheduling, and redistributing both functions. 
 
The procedure presented in this BP is a responsibility of the planner. It is part of building strategic 
guidelines for the organization to follow.
