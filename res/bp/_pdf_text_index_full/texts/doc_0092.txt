MINING DEALER BEST PRACTICE  
 
1.0 Introduction  
 
With the advancement of Technology in today’s Electrical & Hydraulic Systems, it is a 
requirement that Service Technicians have ready access to a readable System Schematic. 
It is important for these schematics to be readily accessible for Shop and Field use, as well as 
being large enough to read, durable enough to withstand wind & rain, and allow for temporary 
marking with grease pencils during the trouble-shooting process. 
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
Newmont’s Phoenix Mine has created a cabinet that stores laminated copies of hydraulic and 
electrical schematics for all their mobile equipment.  This cabinet is located in the Main Shop and 
is easily accessible to all technicians.  Technicians can remove a laminated schematic, take it to 
the machine they are working on, mark it up with a grease pencil as they trace wires or hoses and 
work their way through a diagnostic fault-tree. 
When they are finished with the schematic, it can be easily wiped clean and placed back in the 
cabinet for the next use. 
 
3.0 Implementation Steps 
 
Cabinet  
Deciding what type of Cabinet is available or that space constraints allow for. 
Ideally the cabinet should allow the schematics to hang in a way they can dry if they are wet. 
Newmont’s cabinet is 18” deep x 39” wide x 54” height. 
By having two rods in one direction and two in the other direction, you can quickly sort through 
and find the schematic you want and then slide all to the rearward area, the others to the front, 
then when you open the front panel, the one you want is easily removed from the rods. 
 
Schematics 
“Blown Up” schematics should be printed and laminated in the appropriate size to hang in the 
selected cabinet.  The schematics are taken directly from the CAT service manual. 
The schematics should hang in a way that when the cabinet is opened, the description of each 
schematic is readily visible. 
On the back side of each schematic, critical system spec’s are provided for reference. 
At least once a year the schematics should be checked to verify they still match what is available 
in SIS. 
 
4.0 Benefits 
 
This resource facilitates a more rapid and accurate trouble-shooting & diagnostic process 
because technicians are inclined to fully utilize these accessible, user friendly schematics. 
These schematics are relatively inexpensive to produce, so they can be easily updated any time 
SIS changes are made to the source document.
