1.0 Introduction  
 
Hydraulic presses use multiple tons of force to remove/install pins, bushings, and other press-fit 
parts.  This force can cause the part to suddenly give way or fracture, creating a potential risk of 
flying debris. 
 
Some dealers have designed and built a simple chain link cage around hydraulic presses to 
protect the operator and other shop personnel from this risk. 
 
This Best Practice is a Quick Win project. 
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
The protective cage (see photos) shown in this Best Practice was designed and fabricated by the 
dealer.  The key features are: 
• It is portable and has channels in the base to acc ommodate transport using a forklift. 
• The cage is painted high visibility yellow. 
• The back of the cage opens like a gate to allow ac cess to the press and the set up of 
carriers and other parts on the press. Specific cage dimensions will vary with the press in 
use, but should allow ample room for operators to move about the press. 
• The top of the cage is open to allow access by ove rhead or jib crane. 
• The press is operated by a pigtail remote control while the operator stands outside the 
cage. 
• The cage has a view port so the operator can see t he press and hydraulic pump gages. 
 
 
3.0 Implementation Steps 
 
 
 
Cage has open top, gated back, and forklift channels.
