1.0 Introduction  
 
The mining ind ustry has managed to make  safety a main value and create actionable rules to 
increase safety in operations. Our permanent challenge is to mitigate and better remove the causes 
of any incidents. Procedures related to maintenance of mining equipment are constantly reviewed 
and improved due to the risk involved in handling large components. Furthermore, there is also the 
risk of workers’ permanent exposure to crushing, falling and shock hazards, which has the potential 
to result in energy incidents. 
 
Although there are specific procedures for safely conducting maintenance, the workers conducting 
these pr ocesses should continue to identify additional measures that would mitigate  undesired 
situations. 
 
Another important source of information is the Incident Records of our Clients  which helped to 
develop additional safety measures in order to prevent future incidents from occurring. 
 
In the case of the MD6640 Drill, several operating error incidents with high fatality potential were 
reported during prolonged maintenance due to uncontrolled movements of the mast machinery (10-
ton weight). 
 
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
This best practice proposes the installat ion of a hard barrier which  prevents any uncontrolled 
movement or sliding of the  mast machinery in the event of operational error s during prolonged 
maintenance of the MD6640 Drill. The main safety syste m to fix the drill mast machinery is the 
transmission pulldown brake applied by a spring when the system is de -energized, and then 
released electrically when energized. This system  involves a risk in the case of  involuntary 
energization due to human error which may release the fixation of the mast machinery. 
 
This best practice proposes  the installation of two (2)  T/T (thimble-thimble) steel wire rope slings 
(10 ton each) and two (2) screw pin anchor shackles  (8.5 ton each) to secure mast machinery to 
the mast frame at a certain height. This should prevent any sliding even when brakes are set and 
ensure that all energy sources are isolated and blocked. 
 
The installation of these slings allows for a hard barrier which guarantees better safety standards 
for a large-scale work and prolonged exposure to the mast machinery travel area.

3.0 Implementation Steps 
 
The implementation of this Best Practice is recommended for prolonged exposure and is targeted 
to work on mast machinery platform with the mast in vertical position. 
 
Installation of the slings: 
 
 
Stage 
 
 
Process 
 
Guidelines 
 
 
Safety slings should be installed for activities involving prolonged exposure 
(works involving continuous exposure of at least 4 hours, considering 2 
hours for the installation and removal of the slings, which is approx. 50% of 
the actual work time) . This installation procedure should be included in 
safety documentation and risks should be considered in addition to control 
measures of the activity. 
 
 
Use of Slings 
 
Use certified ¾” slings and 1" shackles. Check them frequently for cracks 
in the slings or forces badly exerted on shackles. 
 
Car positioning 
 
Before starting, when the equipment is still energized, position the upper 
part of the mast machinery between the 5th and 6th diagonal beam of the 
mast (from top to bottom). The operator shall perform this operation 
assisted by maintenance personnel. 
 
 
Brake 
 
 
The operator must apply the lifting brake and perform the isolation and 
locking procedure. 
 
 
Mast anchorage 
 
 
Safety harnesses must be worn at all times. 2 personnel should conduct 
this task. Man-lifting equipment must be used for slings and shackles. 
 
 
Sling Installation 
 
 
Two ¾” slings must be used in the installation (¾” as minimum diameter) 
with 1” shackles (1” as minimum diameter). There must be a sling on each 
side of the mast machinery, embracing the 5th diagonal beam and the mast 
machinery frame . Th e slings must be tightened as much as  possible, 
requiring 2 people to assist. 
 
 
Sling 
Uninstallation 
 
Two people and man -lifting equipment are necessary to perform this 
operation. With the hoist brake applied and the equipment locked, remove 
the pin from both shackles, and then remove the shackles. Finally, remove 
the slings.

4.0 Benefits 
 
Reduce the possibility of a potentially fatal incident caused by  high energy exchange if the mast 
machinery slides through the mast. In such case, the mast machinery hoist/lifting brake would also 
fail. 
 
5.0 Resources Required 
 
2 ¾" slings (¾" minimum diameter) 
2 1" shackles (1” minimum diameter)
