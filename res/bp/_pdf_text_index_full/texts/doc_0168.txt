1.0 Introduction  
 
The demand for productivity and cost optimization has pushed the OHT manufacturers to address 
bigger hauling capacity, which subsequently has increased the size of the equipment and 
components.  These bigger equipment and components has to be maintained representing a 
significant challenge to the maintenance & repair operation in terms of facilities, special tooling, 
people skills and safety. In this scenario the removal and installation of the dump body of a 
mining truck represent a task that requires special care because of its size and weight. This task 
requires extensive coordination of resources and observation of safety procedures to minimize 
the inherent risk of working with a suspended load of such weight and dimensions. 
 
Historically the dump body removal and installation has been executed by the use of two high 
capacity portable cranes that are operated in precise coordination guided by a crew of normally a 
minimum of 5 skilled people per crane plus 2 low boy truck operators for the transportation of the 
dump body. In some specific cases it has been noticed that as much as 7 to 14 persons are being 
assigned to this operation. 
 
 
Illustration 1: Pictures of a typical dump body removal / installation operation executed with the assistance of two portable cranes. 
 
This operation has to be done in an open and leveled area and in some cases can last more than 
30 hours. Therefore, the service crew has to pay special attention to all safety procedures to 
minimize the inherent risks of working with a “suspended” load that in some cases can be over 50 
metric tons.

It is a risky and unpredictable operation, because of being performed in an open area can be 
adversely affected by the weather conditions, especially wind conditions that can delay or stop 
the execution of it for as long as it is necessary to operate within the safety recommendations. 
Normally a wind speed of 35 km/h (21.7mph) is accepted as the maximum limit for maneuvering 
this type of loads with a portable crane.  
 
The following bullets summarize the main concerns or disadvantages of the traditional 
“suspended load” dump body removal and install procedure: 
 
• Method or concept used. Suspended load of as much as 52 plus tons (797 Dump Body 
case) 
• Accessories. Use of lifting devices such as cables/chain/rope that represent a high risk if 
not maintained, inspected and installed / used carefully.  
• Service crew is forced to execute the task of removing the dump body and hoist cylinder 
pins under a suspended load. 
• Availability of suitable portable crane to handle the dump body weight. This normally 
requires extensive coordination to ensure the availability of the cranes when they are 
needed. A perfect coordination won’t ensure 100% crane availability when for example a 
plant emergency arises. 
• Weather conditions. It can adversely affect the dump body R&I operation, delaying or 
cancelling completely the task. This in fact can ruin any possible coordination done in 
advance. As a consequence, the maintenance planning cannot be achieved. 
• Manpower. Significant manpower is required to complete the operation. Normally the 
people involved in this operation can be as much as 7(using one crane) to 14 persons 
(using two cranes). 1 lead supervisor, 1 safety expert, 2 or 4 mechanics to control the 
position cables (“wind control” cables), 2 or 4 Riggers to assist the crane operator by 
signals, 1 or 2 skilled crane operators and 2 low boy truck operators. 
• Execution time. Because of all the concerns and uncontrolled events that could be present 
at the time of the operation, the dump body execution time can be as high as 24 hours, 
and in some cases higher. Normally the planning department schedules the dump body 
change for 2 eight hours shift, but this estimate can be easily affected as explained before 
by many external factors. 
• Pin removal. The pins removal operation can be also a challenge for the service crew 
when using the two cranes method, because of the inability of controlling the dump body 
position in the four (4) lifting points separately. The pin removal can be in some cases a 
bottle neck in terms of execution time. 
• Dump body transportation costs. The uncontrolled factors already mentioned may in some 
cases increase the transportation costs when dump body repairs are done outside the 
mine site, e.g. parking costs

The Best Practice herein presented represents a significant improvement, an innovative solution 
to minimize or eliminate the risks and disadvantages of the traditional dump body removal & 
install operation. 
 
Illustration 2: Picture of the OHT Dump Body Removal & Installation System 
 
 
 
The OHT Dump Body Removal & Install System eliminates the risks and disadvantages of 
working with a suspended load by the use of an hydraulically operated system that consist mainly 
in four (4) support arms located in 4 respective columns (single zone configuration) that lift, 
maneuver and position the dump body as needed by remote control. This approach changes the 
operation from a “suspended load” to a “supported load” scenario eliminating or minimizing the 
disadvantages mentioned previously. 
 
The OHT Dump Body R&I System is a solution created and manufactured by Novamine SpA. 
(Patents involved: CL20080002638 and CL20080002637; Patent Pending: CL20070001876), a 
specialized company in the field of development and manufacturing of innovative mining solutions 
located in Antofagasta Chile. 
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
The OHT Dump Body Removal & Install System consist of an adjustable structure of 6 columns 
(full configuration) operated hydraulically that are positioned to form 2 work zones dedicated to 
perform the operation of dismounting and mounting the truck dump body.

Illustration 3: Dump Body R&I System diagram 
 
 
The system operates basically with 4 support arms installed in 4 respective columns that 
combined provides tridimensional adjustment (x, y, z). The sideway or horizontal adjustment of 
the columns is designed to facilitate the positioning (entry and exit) of the truck. And the vertical 
adjustment of the support arms are dedicated to lift and maneuver the body to remove it from the 
truck and to positioning the dump body in 4 support fixtures or directly onto a low boy 
transportation on highway truck.   
 
The horizontal movement of the columns is handle by the operator from the low part of each 
column and the vertical movement of the arms is operated either through wireless remote control 
or panel control located in the OTH Dump Body R&I System. 
 
Note: The central columns rotate in 180 degrees to allow the operation in both work zones.

After the dump body is supported by the arms, the dump body gets lifted enough to remove the 
load from the pins and ensure the easy removal of them. To facilitate the pins removal the 4 
support arms can be adjusted independently.  
 
 
Once the pins are removed the dump body can be lifted and the truck can be released.

Next the dump body can be loaded into a low boy truck or mounted on supports to perform a local 
repair. 
 
The general technical specifications of the system are the following: 
 
• Columns hoist capacity (per column): 20 Tons. 
• Total hoist capacity: 80 Tons. 
• Distance between columns (width): 6 meters. 
• Truck models capable of servicing: from Cat 785 to Cat 797 size range. 
• Hoist displacement: 5.25 meters  
• Lift time: 8 minutes. 
• Maximum Hydraulic Pressure: 3.000 Psi. 
• Electrical power supply: 380 V, 50 – 60 Hz. 
• Electric Motor Power: 10 HP. 
• Fully functional wireless remote control. 
• Support fixtures (support dump body). 
• Dump body download from low boy truck System.

4.0 Benefits 
 
The main benefits aimed by the designer / manufacturer and documented by the customers 
currently using the system are: 
 
• Significant improvement in safety by moving from a “suspended load” to a “supported load” 
operation. The risk reduction using the body R&I system can be observed in many 
locations: 
o Working conditions under a heavy suspended load eliminated. 
o No need for operating mobile cranes eliminating the risk of potential erroneous 
coordination between both lifting machines. 
o No need for lifting devices (Cables, slings,…), eliminating the risk of faulty material 
and inadequate installation of these devices. 
o Improved visibility for the person in charge of the operation. 
o Risk associated to the pins removal mitigated. 
o Mechanical locks prevent /minimize the risk of a dump body falling in the case of a 
malfunction of the hydraulic hoist system. 
o Unexpected weather changes influences eliminated. 
• In terms of the Maintenance & Repair Processes, the benefits are also significant in terms 
of efficiency, resources, and effectiveness. The following are the main benefits 
documented: 
o Dump Body R&I time reduce significantly. Execution time reduction of as much as 
10 times has been documented. 
o Positive influence in MR (Maintenance Ratio) by a significant reduction of the 
personnel needed to complete the dump body R&I operation. Normally 3 to 4 
trained people is sufficient to perform the task. 
o No need for the use of cranes eliminating the cost and coordination of highly 
demanded equipment. 
o Unexpected weather related delays eliminated contributing effectively to comply 
with maintenance event planning.

o No additional specialized equipment needed for a different truck model or size. The 
system is capable of handling various dump body sizes and configurations.  
o Eliminate extra costs associated with the dump body transportation system by
