1.0 Introduction  
 
Mining equipment is fundamental in achieving production goals at any mining operation. To ensure 
the availability and reliability of the equipment, it is necessary to include maintenance and repair tasks 
that must be executed with efficiency, quality, and safety.  
 
When conducting maintenance procedures, blocking or deactivating stored energy in equipment is a 
routine task. However, this requires rigorous follow-up of established safety procedures - procedures 
known in Finning as "The Rules of Life". The Rules of Life are a set of rules that define behaviors of 
mandatory compliance for all employees, disobedience of these rules is considered unacceptable.  
 
We have developed this Best Practice to ensure that all personnel involved in the maintenance and 
repair processes identify these life rules when working with mining equipment. Elimination of energy 
points in a practical and fast way will directly improve efficiency and increase safety standards. 
  
2.0 Best Practice Description  
 
This best practice consists of the development and implementation graphics for the different 
Caterpillar equipment models, with a visual and easy understanding representation of the energy 
points that must be neutralized or blocked before carrying out the maintenance and repair work.  
 
This best practice (BP) complements the information of the maintenance guidelines and procedures 
and increases access to information – especially for technicians that are in the training process – by 
summarizing and facilitating the identification of the components and systems of the equipment.  
 
2.1 Opportunity  
 
In 2016, a serious accident involving energy exchange occurred due to the release of oil 
subjected to residual pressure. This accident brought attention to effective procedures and 
controls of energy blockage/elimination. 
  
First, measures were aimed at identification of energy only in areas and places where 
measuring instruments were used. Once all APCIEs (High Potentials for Energy Exchange) 
were identified, the need to continue to strengthen and develop the content was identified and 
information was extended to all the machine systems. Communication of this information to the 
end users was simple, graphical, didactic and in a quick-access format.  
 
The first equipment models selected for the development of the “Equipment Energy Maps”.  
were the CAT-793F Truck and the CAT 994H Wheel Loader. These diagrams identify each of 
the components and systems that have or may have energy and include basic energy isolation 
procedures (system or component to block and / or isolate - Blocking point - How? - With 
what? - Risk).

3.0 Implementation Steps  
 
We recommend the following steps when implementing this Best Practice 
 
3.1 Tasks assignment  
 
Once the need to generate energy maps is identified, it is necessary to bring together the 
areas of the organization involved in the development and implementation of this BP (In our 
case: SHEQ, Engineering, Operations, Mentoring).  
 
Each of these areas must assume the responsibility of developing certain tasks – according to 
their specialization – forming an effective project team. The area of prevention and operations 
are those who define what is required to improve those identified areas of high potentials for 
energy exchange. In this case, the Engineering and Mentoring Area, due to their technical 
capabilities, were responsible for identifying and delivering the complete and detailed 
information of each machine type, consolidating the necessary data, and ensuring that no 
additional information was required.  
 
3.2 Select Format  
 
Once the Engineering and Mentoring area compile the technical information of the equipment 
and the potential energies, they proceed to choose the design and format of the maps. In our 
situation, the Safety area (SHEQ) was responsible for the execution of this task, since they are 
the ones with experience to define the visual requirements, so that the message gets 
communicated effectively to the service personnel (See the examples of Energy Maps).  
 
3.3 Maps Distribution  
 
Once the first draft of the Energy Maps has been completed, we recommend distributing and 
analyzing them with the technicians. At this stage, make sure to gather suggestions from the 
technicians and define the final form of implementation for the field service and the workshop. 
For the field service, we recommend including the Energy Maps information in the service 
folders with instructions to be applied to scheduled and unscheduled jobs.

3.5 Start-up and Benefits Validation  
 
Once the information / maps get implemented in the workshop, field operations, and shop work 
areas, their proper use should be monitored and their positive contribution in avoiding accidents 
validated.  
 
4.0 Benefits  
 
The main benefits of this best practice are in: Safety and Efficiency of the Technicians.  
 
4.1 Safety  
 
With the implementation of these maps, technicians were trained and made aware of the 
importance of blocking and eliminating stored or residual energies that represented a high risk 
in the execution of maintenance and repair tasks. Since the implementation of this Best 
Practice no incident has occurred due to this type of risks.  
 
4.2 Efficiency  
 
It should be noted that the Equipment Energy Maps, consolidate in a single document all the 
information required by the technicians, improving the information search times that is normally 
done using SIS (Service Information System) or Workshop service manuals. Improved times 
that positively impact the efficiency of maintenance and repair tasks.
