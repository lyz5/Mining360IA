1.0 Introduction  
 
Efficient Component Rebuild Center (CRC) space management is an important factor of productivity. 
Missing tools in a specific area can cause major time loss and can lead to potential accidents using 
“will-do” tools instead of the one fit for the job.  Overstocking of unnecessary material and tooling 
increases the cost of the department without adding any value.   
 
Many CRC shops around the globe have been in place for several  years.  T his Best Practice  is a 
structured approach to address facility revamp.   The principles outlined can apply to numerous 
facilities, both for dealers and customers.  
 
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
Different principles are used in the industry to help organizations manage their working environments. 
This Best Practice utilizes a workplace organization methodology that originated from Henry Ford, and 
was later impr oved by the Japanese.  It is commonly known as “5S” because it uses a list of five 
Japanese words, when translated into English, start with the letter S. 
 
1 – Seiri = Sort: Go through all the tools, materials, etc.  in the work area and keep only essential items.  Everything else is 
stored, returned or discarded. 
2 – Seiton = Straighten or Set in Order: Focus  on efficiency.  Arrange the tools, equipment and parts to  promote work flow 
and maximize efficiency.  A place for everything and everything is in its place (separation and labeling).  Limit the objects 
sitting on the floor.  If possible, make things mobile.  Final set-up must take consideration of ergonomics for the workers. 
3 – Seisō = Sweeping, Shining or Cleanliness: Systematic Cleaning.  At the end of each shift, the work area is cleaned up 
and everything is restored to its place.  This makes it easy to know everything is accounted for and where it should be.   
Everyone becomes a janitor. 
4 – Seiketsu =  Standardizing: Standardized work practices or operating in a consistent and standardized fashion.   
Everyone knows exactly what his/her responsibilities are to keep above 3S’s.   
5 – Shitsuke =  Sustaining the discipline: Maintaining and reviewing standards.   Maintain the focus on this new way of 
operating, and do not allow a gradual decline back  to the old ways .  However, when an issue arises such as a suggested 
improvement, a new way of working or a new tool, then a review of the first 4S's is appropriate.

A sixth phase, "Safety," is sometimes added.  Purists, however, argue that adding it is unnecessary 
since following 5S correctly will result in a safe work environment. 
Two basic rules of the 5S methodology are as follows: 
• It should take no more than 5 seconds to find the tool you need 
• It should take no more than 5 minutes to clean the area.

• Assign a team to perform 5S 
• Conduct training on the 5S methodology 
• Select  an area to implement 5S

5. Sustain the discipline 
 
Upon completion of implementing 5S,  employees will be proud of their work bays  and keep them 
clean, ensuring others do as well.  But in time what was once new will become routine.  Therefore, an 
audit schedule must be an integral part of every project with no exception tolerated, constantly work on 
keeping everyone’s commitment alive.  Additionally, a maintenance schedule for the area should be  
defined (painting, equipment maintenance, contamination control and process audits, etc.). 
 
 
4.0 Benefits 
 
• Additional work bays utilizing the same floor plan 
• Improvements in safety, productivity and engagement 
• Eliminate excess parts and tools 
• Tools become more accessible and are more easily accounted for 
• Consistent workflow and quality 
 
At a customer’s site, the implementation should include the dealer’s participation to make sure that the 
necessary tooling and procedures are correct, and that contamination control included.

5.0 Resources Required 
• Project sponsor 
• Logistic manager 
• Trainer (1 day) 
• 2 full time employees 
• 1 painter (occasional) 
• 1 welder to fabricate catwalks and mobile carts (occasional) 
• 1 drawing engineer for above (occasional) 
• Dealer involvement for contamination control, special tooling and procedures advice. 
• Project duration per area: approx 8-10 days w/ 30 min. meetings beginning & end of each day 
• Shelving, racking, High density polyethylene (HDPE) for assembly tables, labeling, paint etc…
