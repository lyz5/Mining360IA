1.0 Introduction  
 
This Best Practice provides an overview of the Road Analysis Control (RAC) tool and best practices 
for a successful implementation of RAC as a haul road management and productivity tool.   
 
RAC is an on-board information technology product designed to measure and benchmark haul road 
quality. Integrated with the Vital Information Management System (VIMS), RAC measures component 
loading and impact shock, communicates that data to the operator and to the mine office in real time. 
Trucks equipped with GPS can export a RAC event location so the events can be plotted on the mine 
map and proactively creating a work plan for the mine’s road maintenance team. RAC provides data 
and reports through Technician Toolbox and enables mine managers to quantify, monitor and 
manage haul roads.  
 
2.0 Opportunity Recognition 
 
The best opportunities for RAC implementation can be found in mines with: 
 Poor tire life 
 Frame, lower powertrain, and strut quality issues – especially frame downtime 
 Operator complaints of poor ride and haul road quality 
 Inconsistent cycle times day-to-day 
 Underfoot prone to pits 
 Haul road maintenance repair is determined by operator feedback 
 
3.0 Benefits 
 
Road Analysis Control (RAC), is a haul road management tool providing feedback to help manage 
operator technique, haul road and loading conditions. 
 
This feedback enables the site to, quantify the severity of the condition and allows mine management 
to make more informed decisions to reduce overall haulage costs.  
 
The long-term benefits of using RAC can include tire life improvements as well as the same benefits 
mine’s receive from good haul road maintenance – haul trucks move faster and have less issues 
when they travel along smooth, well maintained haul roads. Faster trucks increase mine production, 
having less maintenance issues on trucks reduces haulage costs so incorporating RAC into mine 
operations improves mine cost-per-ton. 
 
When used properly RAC can be a significant productivity tool to: 
 Improve haulage cycle times and raise truck productivity. 
 Contribute to longer tire and rim life. 
 Identify haul road problem areas to avoid and correct. 
 Lower operating costs. Help achieve intended component life cycle. 
 Identify haulers with poorly charged suspension 
 Improve repair before failure processes. 
 Reduce stress on drive components, frame and suspension. 
 Reduce fuel consumption.

5.0 Setup 
  
Installation: 
 
RAC is a Software Control Application (SCA) within Caterpillar Electronic Technician (ET) that can be 
activated by authorized dealer personnel. Machines must be equipped with GPS to pin-point locations 
on the haul roads that triggered RAC events. The mapping of this events can be reviewed within 
Health Equipment Insights (HEI).  
 
RAC has 3x  severity levels: 
 Level 3 – 170% of target level 1  (least sensitive) 
 Level 2 – 135% of target level 2 
 Level 1 – baseline    (most sensitive) 
 
Recommendation is to start a mine site at Level 3 (least sensitive). If they’re not getting events, they 
should move down to level 2 (more sensitive) and then onto level 1 (most sensitive). The mine does 
not start at Level 3, the number of events is likely to be difficult to handle. 
 
At this point, it’s also important to make sure the strut pressures are maintained properly and 
reference SEHS9411 document; otherwise, you’d get poor results from RAC. 
 
It’s recommended that all trucks activate RAC to monitor to collect the most data.

7.0 Tire Management 
 
  
There are several common areas where RAC can help support a good tire management program.  
The operators need to be trained on each of the unique RAC events and the conditions that create 
the event.   
 
7.1 Common Bias Events - Cornering 
 
The most common bias event is observed where the operator is steering a truck through a corner.  If 
the cornering speed is too high the load of the truck will transfer to the outer tires and the tires will 
experience a lateral loading at the same time.  The combination of these loads can be extremely 
stressful on the tire construction and often lead to shoulder separation issues on the tire. 
 
  
 
7.2 Common Bias Events – Bias Tire Loading 
 
Another common bias event often seen is where the truck is approaching the dump.  In this example 
the operator is applying the brakes (transferring load to the front tires) and simultaneously turning the 
truck.  These examples have observed tire loads to reach over 180% of equivalent static loads while 
corning.  These events often result in a simultaneous rack event.   
Coaching the operators to remove most of the speeds in a straight line and then cornering at a 
constant speed will under normal circumstances reduce the loads on the front tires by around 30 to 
40%.  Also, the team can instructor the motor grader to increase the cross fall angle or supper 
elevation on this turn to handle additional speed.

7.3 Common Rack Events – Bias Tire Loading 
Rack events are very common while under the loading equipment and are effectively an indication of 
how significantly the loads are being applied to ‘twist’ the frame.   
 
The following sequence of events is commonly seen on many operations during the loading phase: 
  
Prior to reversing in to the loading tool,  The suspension pressures are 
very  consistent left to right suggesting that they are well charged.  Rack 
values are close to zero at this time.   
 
 
 
 
 
 
As the truck approaches the loading position it is possible to observe 
when one of the duals is on the rilled material of the bench.  This could be a 
single rock that could be a significant point loading on the tire or 
alternatively or the toe which will create frame stresses.   
In this position while empty it will not create a Rack event and is not 
creating significant harm to the tires/frame 
 
 
 
As subsequent passes from the loading tool are added to the truck the 
weight will increase and subsequently thee stresses across the machine 
can increase to the level that a Rack event will be induced.   
 
It is worth noting that if there is a single rock underneath one of the duals 
that is inducing this situation it is reasonable to consider that 2/3 of the 
Gross vehicle mass (rear of truck with load) is largely being point loaded 
on the tire.   
 
Rack events under the loading tool can easily be avoided by moving 
forward by a few meters between the first and second loading tool pass with negligible production 
implications but very signficiant benefits. Another best pracice is when the truck is posititioned under 
the shovel, instruct the operator to place the truck in Neutral, release the foot brake, and let the truck 
settle into a flat location before applying the brake again.

8.0 Follow-up and Sustaining 
 
Once the RAC and haul road maintenance plan has been implemented, the dealer and customer 
should schedule a follow-up to review the progress and improvements. The teams should keep track 
of the events logged over time and see a reduction in events over time if the road maintenance team 
is following the reports. It’s important that the data and recommendations made to the haul road team 
be confirmed as completed and the process be implemented permanently, as to not go back to haul 
road conditions as before.
