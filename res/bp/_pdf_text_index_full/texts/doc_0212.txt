MINING DEALER BEST PRACTICE  
 
1.0 Introduction  
 
This Best Practice contains information about salvaging water line group parts of 3500 
series engines, such as elbows, bonnets and flanges. Salvaging these parts can achieve 
significant cost savings. 
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
The parts of water line groups, such as flanges, bonnets and elbow tubes, usually have 
damaged connecting areas.  The damaged areas are machined down and fabricated 
sleeves are installed to enable reusing the parts.

3.0 Implementation Steps  
 
CAUTION: SAFETY PROCEDURES FOR THE SHOP EQUIPMENT INVOLVED MUST BE 
FOLLOWED.  
Implementation steps are defined for salvaging a 146-7383 flange. Similar steps may be to the 
other parts listed below.   
 
• Make sure the part has been properly cleaned before beginning of the repair 
• Inspect the part for cracks. If there is a crack, salvage of the component is not 
recommended. 
• Machine the bore of the flange 146-7383 to 111.2 mm ID; and depth of 48 mm. 
 
 
 
 
Machining the Flange 146-7383 
 
 
 
• Fabricate a sleeve using ASTM A48 -30B (Gray Iron). Detailed dimensions are given 
below.

4.0 Benefits 
 
• Cost saving on engine overhauls 
• O verhaul turnaround time can be reduced for an engine rebuild, salvaged parts can be 
inventoried. 
 
5.0 Resources Required 
 
• Machining Center or similar tool to machine part counterbore 
• Lathe machine to machine sleeve 
• Band saw 
• Parts Freezer  
• Retaining compound (recommend Loctite 640) 
• Sleeve installation driver adapters 
 
6.0 Supporting Attachments / References 
 
EDITOR’S NOTE:  While the procedure noted in this Best Practice may work well for some 
dealers, Caterpillar’s preference is to salvage the bore with flame spray and machine to size. 
The inserts can come loose during service and or leak, however, dealers with no flame spray 
capabilities will typically install sleeves.  See attached SEBF-8148 document for more 
information.
