1.0 Introduction  
Condition Monitoring (CM) is a critical component to any successful Maintenance & Repair (M&R) 
repair-before-failure strategy.  The foundation of any successful Condition Monitoring program is 
regular and thorough machine inspections.  Discipline to conduct inspections and the attention to 
detail to detect potential problems or existing defects is very important.  Defect detection must be 
followed by prompt and appropriate execution by the Backlog Management and Planning & 
Scheduling departments. 
 
Dust entry through the air induction system and into the engine can cause significant downtime, 
productivity loss, customer frustration, and significant man-hours to repair or replace the engine.  
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
Pressurizing the air induction system can allow the maintenance team to check for proper 
integrity and sealing of the induction tubes, hoses and clamps.  Early detection of problems or 
potential problems is very important. Use machines with known healthy air systems to establish 
site specific baselines for silicone and aluminum in engine oil SOS. This will aid SOS interpreters 
in identifying air systems issues. 
 
3.0 Implementation Steps 
1. Identify service interval to employ this test. (For instance: Every 500 hour PM, etc).   
2. Employ lockout and tag-out procedure.  
3. Remove air filters. 
4. Install test caps to all filter housings and secure in place using threaded nut. 
5. Pressurize test cap using clean and dry shop air.   
a. Suggest air pressure to 4 psi (28 kpa). 
6. Listen for any sounds of air leakage from the system.   
a. Good: No sound of air leakage. 
b. Not Good: Sound of air leakage. If sound of air leakage, identify air source and take 
appropriate action to resolve. 
7. Spray mild dilution of soapy water on joints and rubber hoses in air induction system. 
8. Visually inspect for bubbles (bubbles indicate the presence of a leak in that location). 
a. Good:  No soapy bubbles (machine has a well sealed induction system). 
b. Not Good: If air bubbles are seen.  If so, identify and take appropriate action to resolve. 
 
Photos of Test Caps to Test Air Induction System for Leaks.  Design features are: 
1) Long hollow tube to allow cap to be secured in place of air filter 
2) Test port to supply air pressure to cap during pressure testing 
3) O- ring groove to seal to machine and prevent air leakage

4.0 Benefits 
Detection of air induction pressure loss and / or loose induction clamps clamp can have 
significant benefits.  Early detection is key to limiting severity of failure, repair cost of parts and 
labor, and machine downtime to resolve. 
 
5.0 Resources Required 
The major resource required to implement this Best Practice is the fabrication of the test caps to 
install on the air filter boxes.   
Fabrication costs are approximately US $ 1000 / per set of four. 
 Parts List is as Follows: 
1) O- ring Seal: 096-0037  (ID: 264.5 mm, Cross Section: 8.4 mm) 
2) Wing Nut:  1N-8161 
3) Material for Test Cap:  Aluminum
