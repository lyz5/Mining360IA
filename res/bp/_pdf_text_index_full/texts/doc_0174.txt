1.0 Introduction  
 
The importance of clean machines and components when performing maintenance and repair 
tasks has been understood and promoted for a long time.  Traditional cleaning methods with high 
pressure water cannons and steam clean units will continue to play a vital role, though in certain 
circumstances and applications, it is difficult to achieve the desired cleanliness standards. 
 
This document describes the trial and results of Cryogenic or Dry Ice Cleaning at an Australian 
coal mine.  The focus was the engine and pump rooms of a Cat 5230 excavator.  The mine 
operation intends to continue the use of this method for select cleaning jobs such as in-frame 
component overhauls when it is critical to have a clean working environment.  The process is also 
extremely effective when cleaning electrical connectors and components as well as hydraulic and 
pneumatic hoses and connectors. 
 
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
The trial of this cleaning process was conducted in the engine and pump rooms of a 5230 
excavator.  The purpose was to deliver a cleaner working environment for fitters.  This allows for 
improved identification of mechanical and electrical faults.  A local dry ice cleaning contractor 
performed the cleaning.   
 
The work may be conducted in any safe location at the mine.  The process does not require 
machine cool down (other than for any safety related reasons for those performing the cleaning).  
The process generates a moderate level of noise, so it is recommended that the cleaning 
operation be conducted within an area requiring hearing protection. 
  
Cryogenic cleaning uses dry ice pellets (CO
2 in solid form) as the cleaning media. The dry ice 
pellets are directed at high speed at the surface to be cleaned.  The intended surfaces are 
cleaned very effectively and quickly via a combination of 1) Kinetic energy, 2) Low temperature 
thermal energy, and 3) sublimation (instantaneous conversion of the pellets from a solid to vapor 
state).   
 
The following illustrations describe the physical processes that take place during the cleaning 
operation. 
   
 
1) Kinetic. When dry ice pellets are accelerated in a jet of compressed air and strike a 
surface at the speed of sound, they crack and loosen the coating on the surface being 
treated.

2) Low temperature thermal. The low temperature of dry ice pellets (-79C) makes the coating 
brittle, cracks it and loosens it. This allows dry ice to permeate the coating. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
3) Sublimation. Dry ice penetrates the coating and immediately sublimes (passes direct from 
solid to vapor state). This results in a 700– fold increase in volume, an explosive effect that 
lifts the coating off the surface.  
 
The process uses compressed air and a variety of nozzle configurations to deliver and direct the 
dry ice. The velocity and distribution of the media can be controlled to remove only the desired 
contaminants and will not disturb paint, decals, labels, registration marks, etc.  Since the dry ice 
pellets convert into a vaporous state, there is little to no residual material left (mainly dirt and grit 
that is cleaned from the machine or components).  Chemicals or solvents are not used so 
contaminated water treatment is not required.  Clean up and disposal time is minimal and drying 
time is eliminated. 
 
There are a number of mine operations in the region currently using this method for select 
cleaning jobs.  The process is extremely effective when used for in-frame component overhauls, 
major PCR work, cleaning of draglines, cleaning of electrical components and cabinets, or any 
application where it is critical to have a clean, dry working environment.

3.0 Implementation Steps 
 
Few mine operations are likely to invest in dry ice cleaning facilities or equipment.  Therefore, if 
there are dry ice cleaning contractors within the mine vicinity, implementing this process is as 
simple as engaging the services of local suppliers.  The cleaning operations can be performed in 
any location allowed by the mine operation management. No special preparations are required.

4.0 Benefits 
 
The list of benefits is significant.  Here are a few of the major advantages delivered and realized 
as a result of the trial: 
• Fast and efficient  
o During the above trial the equipment was completely decontaminated in 5 hours.  It 
was estimated that it would have taken at least 10 hours with normal ‘wet’ methods 
to achieve a significantly lesser result. 
• Many time consuming and labor intensive steps are no longer required, including: 
o Cool down, disassembly, transport of equipment and reassembly. 
• Improve electrical equipment reliability 
o As with so many sites, electrical issues due to damaged harnesses and connectors 
are a major cause of machine stoppages.  The local dealer recognizes that 
introducing high-pressure water into harnesses and connectors creates many of 
these problems.  Further, in many cases, the mine water used for cleaning is highly 
saline so the development of corrosion on electrical contacts develops over time.  
Dry Ice cleaning removes the possibility of water being introduced into critical areas. 
• A very clean result.  The photos above show how dramatic the results are, even in hard to 
reach areas.  Technicians can easily identify faults and defects on thoroughly cleaned 
components. 
• High resistance paths in electrical connections caused by build up of dust and dirt over 
time is believed to be the cause of many electrical faults.  This dust and dirt can be 
effectively cleaned using dry ice. 
• Due to the sublimation of the dry ice (turning to vapor) there are no secondary wastes left 
behind.  This reduces costs for disposal, containment and collection of wastes and 
significantly reduces clean-up time. 
• Will not damage hydraulic/pneumatic hoses, electrical cable insulation or electronic 
components.  
• No chemicals required.
