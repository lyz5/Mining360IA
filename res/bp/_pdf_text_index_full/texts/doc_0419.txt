1.0 Introduction  
 
Machines create a host of data that is used to monitor machine health and to point out concerns 
with the application. However, even though operator behaviors and human error have a 
significant impact on safety as well as machine and component durability, alarms related to 
operator behavior often do not get the required attention early on when training might still be used 
to correct behaviors. This then represents a lost opportunity to proactively use such information to 
prevent incidents and limit the adverse effect on machine durability and operating cost. This Best 
Practice seeks to drive a proactive reporting into the mining organization including especially 
operations and training departments.   
 
 
2.0 Best Practice Description 
 
Operator induced event reporting creates a means to monitor and communicate about machines 
being operated outside of their expected limits resulting in events being created in the on board 
monitoring systems. While not always black and white, e.g. a payload overload event might be 
driven by a faulty suspension, the ability to track these events enables mining maintenance and 
operations team to identify trends in operator behavior that might affect safety, machine and 
component health. That knowledge then creates a solid foundation to work with operators to 
change undesired or even harmful behavior ideally before it results in failures or incidents.  
 
The approach mining operations take for this reporting might vary in level of detail, frequency of 
reporting etc. depending on preferences, availability of data or local regulations. Shorter intervals 
make it easier to provide almost real time feedback to operators, monitoring trends over longer 
periods of time can help judge the effectiveness of the actions taken to improve. However, the 
principle remains the same:

3.0 Implementation Steps 
 
Prerequisite for implementing this best practice is a connected fleet that reports events into the 
cloud on a regular basis (e.g. via MineStar Health Equipment Insights or Vision Link), the more 
frequent that reporting happens the more “real-time” the available data.  
 
In addition, the following is required: 
- List of events that are caused by operator abuse that can be married up with VL or HEI data 
using Event IDs. That list might vary depending on the models in question and based on what 
maintenance and operations teams on site decide to classify as “operator driven event” 
(example of a list below, can be amended depending on site requirements). 
- Vision Link or HEI to generate dashboards directly or Power Bi or other data analysis 
application to link different datasets and create visualizations. 
- Resource(s) dedicated to the creation of dashboards, analysis of alerts and making 
recommendations regarding corrective actions to improve the operations & optimize cost per 
tons. 
Once a decision has been made to implement Operator Induced Event (OIE) reporting and the 
prerequisites mentioned above are in place, a decision needs to be made regarding the 
frequency of the report. Experience has shown that operations departments prefer daily reports to 
enable timely follow up with operators. This is particularly true if “operator id” is not used which 
makes it difficult to tie back events to individuals over longer periods.

4.0 Benefits 
 
OIE reporting that is shared across the mining organization makes operator driven events more 
visible in the entire organization. Needs for additional training can be identified on a group or 
individual basis and used to develop customized learning plans that drive the reduction of events. 
Targeted training can help improve site safety and help reduce machine operating cost. 
 
5.0 Resources Required 
 
• Access to machine events through the cloud (Vision Link / Health Equipment Insights).  
• User proficient in creating dashboards in HEI or VL, Power Bi or other data analysis 
solution. 
• Cross reference list to classify operator driven events (example below) 
• 3W action plan to facilitate tracking of actions for training, production & maintenance 
departments, dealers can be added to the plan as required.
